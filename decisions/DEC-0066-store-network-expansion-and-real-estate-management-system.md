# DEC-0066 — Store Network, Expansion & Real Estate Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0066  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Store Network, Expansion and Real Estate Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will expand its physical footprint through disciplined location selection, lease management, store economics, standardized design, operational readiness and post-opening measurement.

> **Open stores where the economics, customers and operating model can all win.**

## 1. Expansion Principles

Expansion must be driven by customer demand, sustainable economics, operational capability and manageable risk.

## 2. Expansion Gate

No material store expansion should proceed without documented commercial, financial, operational and legal review.

## 3. Market Selection

Potential markets should be assessed using customer demand, competition, accessibility, demographics and operating conditions.

## 4. Site Selection

Sites should be evaluated for:

- Visibility
- Accessibility
- Footfall
- Parking or transport
- Nearby demand generators
- Competition
- Rent
- Fit-out requirements

## 5. Catchment Analysis

Each proposed store should have a defined primary catchment and customer hypothesis.

## 6. Demand Estimation

Expected sales should be based on evidence, comparable stores and explicit assumptions.

## 7. Conservative Forecasting

Expansion cases should include downside scenarios and should not depend solely on optimistic projections.

## 8. Store Economics

A store business case should consider:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Rent
- Labor
- Utilities
- Marketing
- Fit-out
- Maintenance
- Working capital

## 9. Investment Case

Capital expenditure and expected returns should be evaluated under DEC-0057.

## 10. Break-Even

Expansion cases should identify expected break-even timing and key assumptions.

## 11. Sensitivity Analysis

Material cases should test changes in rent, sales, margin, labor and investment.

## 12. Cannibalization

New stores should be evaluated for potential impact on existing stores and channels.

## 13. Channel Interaction

Store expansion should consider website, delivery, marketplace and other existing channels.

## 14. Location Pipeline

Potential sites should be maintained in a structured pipeline with status and next actions.

## 15. Site Due Diligence

Before commitment, the company should assess legal, physical, commercial and regulatory characteristics of the site.

## 16. Lease Review

Material leases should receive appropriate legal and financial review under DEC-0058 and DEC-0057.

## 17. Lease Terms

Important terms include:

- Rent
- Escalation
- Term
- Renewal
- Deposit
- Maintenance
- Taxes
- Permitted use
- Termination

## 18. Occupancy Cost

Occupancy costs should be evaluated against realistic sales and margin expectations.

## 19. Lease Renewals

Renewal dates and notice periods should be tracked centrally.

## 20. Exit Rights

Material leases should be assessed for termination and exit flexibility.

## 21. Site Compliance

Required licenses, permits and safety conditions should be confirmed before opening.

## 22. Store Design

Store design should support brand identity, customer flow, product discovery, accessibility and operational efficiency.

## 23. Standardization

Common store components should be standardized where it reduces cost and complexity.

## 24. Local Adaptation

Local conditions may justify controlled design adaptations.

## 25. Customer Flow

Layout should make navigation, discovery and checkout straightforward.

## 26. Accessibility

Stores should incorporate appropriate accessibility requirements.

## 27. Safety

Store layouts should support safe customer and employee movement.

## 28. Back-of-House

Storage, receiving and staff areas should support efficient operations.

## 29. Inventory Capacity

Store inventory capacity should align with assortment and replenishment policy under DEC-0064 and DEC-0065.

## 30. Technology Readiness

Stores should have required connectivity, devices, security and systems before opening.

## 31. Point of Sale

POS systems should be tested for sales, refunds, payments, inventory and reporting.

## 32. Network Resilience

Critical store connectivity should have appropriate fallback or recovery procedures.

## 33. Security

Stores should have proportionate physical and digital security controls under DEC-0062.

## 34. CCTV and Monitoring

Security monitoring should follow applicable legal, privacy and retention requirements.

## 35. Construction Management

Fit-out projects should have defined scope, budget, schedule, owners and quality controls.

## 36. Contractor Management

Contractors should meet applicable safety, insurance, quality and contractual requirements.

## 37. Fit-Out Budget

Material fit-out changes should follow approval thresholds and financial controls.

## 38. Project Change Control

Material scope changes should be documented and approved before commitment.

## 39. Opening Readiness

A store should not open until critical:

- Staff
- Inventory
- Systems
- Safety
- Licensing
- Signage
- Customer-service

requirements are ready.

## 40. Staff Readiness

Store teams should be trained before opening under DEC-0056.

## 41. Product Readiness

Opening assortment should be confirmed against the approved merchandising plan.

## 42. Marketing Readiness

Launch marketing should coordinate with inventory and customer-service capacity under DEC-0059.

## 43. Soft Opening

Where appropriate, stores should use controlled soft openings to identify operational issues before full launch.

## 44. Opening Review

Initial performance should be reviewed against the business case and operating assumptions.

## 45. Store KPIs

Store dashboards should include relevant measures such as:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Conversion
- Average transaction value
- Inventory
- Labor
- Customer satisfaction

## 46. Four-Wall Economics

Store performance should distinguish store-level economics from corporate overhead where useful.

## 47. Labor Productivity

Staffing should align with customer demand and service requirements.

## 48. Peak Staffing

Peak periods should receive advance staffing and inventory planning.

## 49. Store Service

Customer-service standards should align with DEC-0054.

## 50. Store Merchandising

Merchandising should align with DEC-0065 and approved category strategy.

## 51. Store Replenishment

Replenishment should align with demand, inventory and supply planning under DEC-0064.

## 52. Local Inventory

Store inventory should balance availability against carrying cost and space constraints.

## 53. Shrinkage

Store shrinkage should be measured and investigated.

## 54. Returns

Returns should follow standardized customer-service and inventory procedures.

## 55. Maintenance

Facilities should have preventive maintenance schedules and escalation procedures.

## 56. Utilities

Utilities should be monitored where material to store economics.

## 57. Energy Efficiency

New and renovated stores should consider practical energy-efficiency opportunities.

## 58. Vendor Management

Store maintenance and service vendors should be governed under procurement and contract controls.

## 59. Lease Compliance

Stores should comply with material landlord and lease obligations.

## 60. Landlord Relations

Material landlord communications and commitments should be documented.

## 61. Expansion Portfolio

Management should evaluate the store network as a portfolio rather than only as individual locations.

## 62. Store Clustering

Cluster strategies may improve logistics, staffing, marketing and brand visibility where appropriate.

## 63. Regional Operations

As the network grows, regional management structures may be introduced.

## 64. Shared Services

Stores should use centralized support where it improves consistency and economics.

## 65. Store Standardization

Common SOPs should cover critical customer and operational processes.

## 66. Local Authority

Store managers may have defined local decision rights within company policy and financial limits.

## 67. Store Performance Reviews

Stores should receive periodic performance reviews with clear corrective actions.

## 68. Underperforming Stores

Persistent underperformance should trigger root-cause analysis and a recovery plan.

## 69. Recovery Plans

Recovery plans should identify demand, margin, cost, staffing, assortment and customer-experience issues.

## 70. Store Closure

Closure should be considered when long-term economics, strategic value or site conditions no longer justify operation.

## 71. Closure Planning

Store exits should address employees, inventory, customers, suppliers, leases, assets and communications.

## 72. Asset Recovery

Equipment and fixtures should be redeployed, sold or disposed of appropriately.

## 73. Inventory Exit

Closing-store inventory should be transferred, sold or otherwise managed with controlled economics.

## 74. Lease Exit

Lease termination should follow contractual and legal requirements.

## 75. Expansion Funding

Expansion should remain consistent with liquidity and capital-allocation priorities under DEC-0057.

## 76. Expansion Risk

Material expansion risks should be recorded under DEC-0051.

## 77. New Market Legal Review

Entering a new jurisdiction should receive legal and regulatory review under DEC-0058.

## 78. New Market Technology

New locations should use approved technology architecture under DEC-0061.

## 79. Data Integration

Store performance data should integrate into business intelligence under DEC-0060.

## 80. AI Store Operations

AI may assist with staffing forecasts, demand planning, anomaly detection and customer insights under DEC-0063.

## 81. Expansion Dashboard

Management should monitor the store pipeline, opening costs, time to launch, sales ramp and return on investment.

## 82. Opening Schedule

Expansion should be paced according to management capacity and operational readiness.

## 83. Expansion Capacity

The company should avoid opening more stores than its supply, people, technology and leadership systems can support.

## 84. Replication

A successful store model should be documented sufficiently to support repeatable expansion.

## 85. Store Playbook

The store playbook should include design, opening, staffing, inventory, service, technology and daily operating standards.

## 86. Site Learning

Post-opening lessons should feed back into future site selection and design.

## 87. Benchmarking

Stores should be compared using consistent metrics while accounting for meaningful local differences.

## 88. Portfolio Optimization

Management should periodically evaluate whether capital and space are allocated to the highest-value locations.

## 89. Real Estate Data

Site records should maintain key lease, location, cost and performance information.

## 90. Documentation

Material real-estate decisions should record assumptions, approvals and contractual commitments.

## 91. Governance

Expansion decisions should use defined approval thresholds and decision rights.

## 92. Board / Owner Review

Material expansion commitments should receive leadership approval appropriate to their financial and strategic impact.

## 93. Customer Experience

Expansion should preserve the service and brand standards established under DEC-0054 and DEC-0059.

## 94. Brand Consistency

New stores should express the approved brand identity while fitting local customer needs.

## 95. Community Impact

Where relevant, new locations should consider local community relationships and practical neighborhood impact.

## 96. Continuous Improvement

The store network should improve through data, customer feedback, operational learning and disciplined portfolio management.

## 97. Key Risks

Major expansion risks include:

- Overestimating demand
- Excessive rent
- Construction overruns
- Cannibalization
- Staffing shortages
- Supply constraints
- Regulatory delays
- Technology failures
- Weak store economics
- Overexpansion

## 98. Expansion Gate Review

Management should periodically review whether expansion criteria remain satisfied before committing additional capital.

## 99. Sustainable Growth

Expansion should strengthen the business rather than create growth that outpaces cash, people or operational capability.

## 100. Success Standard

The expansion system is successful when leadership can answer:

- Where should we open?
- Why there?
- What will it cost?
- What should it earn?
- What could go wrong?
- Are we ready to operate it?
- Should we expand, wait, improve or exit?

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish site pipeline and standardized site-evaluation criteria.
2. Build store business-case and approval templates.
3. Standardize lease, legal and financial due diligence.
4. Build store design and opening-readiness playbooks.
5. Establish store KPI and portfolio dashboards.
6. Formalize underperforming-store recovery and closure processes.
7. Improve regional logistics and shared-service capabilities.
8. Use data and AI to improve site selection and store operations where appropriate.
9. Scale only at a pace supported by people, cash, supply and technology capacity.
