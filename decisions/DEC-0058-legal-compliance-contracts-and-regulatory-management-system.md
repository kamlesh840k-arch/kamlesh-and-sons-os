# DEC-0058 — Legal, Compliance, Contracts & Regulatory Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0058  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Legal, Compliance, Contracts and Regulatory Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0042, DEC-0051, DEC-0055, DEC-0056, DEC-0057

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will manage legal, regulatory, contractual and compliance obligations through a structured system that makes obligations visible, assigns ownership, preserves evidence and escalates material risks before they become expensive problems.

The company will seek qualified local legal, tax and regulatory advice whenever an issue requires professional interpretation.

> **Make obligations visible, decisions defensible, and commitments clear.**

## 1. Legal and Compliance Principles

The system will prioritize:

- Lawful operation
- Clear accountability
- Evidence and documentation
- Proportionate controls
- Early escalation
- Contract discipline
- Regulatory awareness
- Ethical conduct

## 2. Compliance Ownership

Material legal and compliance obligations should have named owners and defined review responsibilities.

## 3. Compliance Register

The company should maintain a register of material obligations covering, as applicable:

- Corporate requirements
- Tax
- Employment
- Consumer protection
- Product requirements
- Privacy
- E-commerce
- Payments
- Import/export
- Store/facility requirements
- Advertising
- Intellectual property

## 4. Legal Entity Records

The company should maintain controlled records for legal entities, registrations, ownership documents and other foundational corporate records.

## 5. Licenses and Permits

Required licenses, permits and registrations should have:

- Owner
- Issuing authority
- Issue date
- Expiry/renewal date
- Supporting evidence
- Renewal status

## 6. Regulatory Calendar

Material regulatory, tax and license deadlines should be tracked in a centralized calendar or equivalent control system.

## 7. Regulatory Change Monitoring

The company should monitor material changes to laws and regulations affecting its operations.

## 8. Legal Advice

Questions involving material legal interpretation, disputes, regulated activities or significant exposure should be escalated to qualified legal professionals.

## 9. Legal Risk Register

Material legal and compliance risks should connect to the enterprise risk register established under DEC-0051.

## 10. Contract Management

Material contracts should be centrally tracked rather than relying only on individual employees' inboxes or local files.

## 11. Contract Register

The contract register should capture, where relevant:

- Counterparty
- Contract type
- Effective date
- Expiry date
- Renewal terms
- Notice periods
- Commercial value
- Owner
- Key obligations

## 12. Contract Approval

Material contracts should receive appropriate commercial, financial, operational and legal review before commitment.

## 13. Signing Authority

Only authorized representatives should execute contracts on behalf of the company.

## 14. Contract Repository

Executed contracts and important amendments should be stored in a controlled repository with appropriate access restrictions.

## 15. Contract Version Control

Drafts, negotiated versions and executed agreements should be distinguishable to avoid accidental reliance on outdated terms.

## 16. Contract Obligations

Important contractual obligations should be converted into operational tasks or controls where necessary.

## 17. Renewal Management

Contracts with automatic renewal or important notice periods should trigger advance review.

## 18. Termination Rights

Material termination rights and notice requirements should be visible to accountable owners.

## 19. Supplier Contracts

Supplier agreements should align with procurement controls under DEC-0052 and include appropriate terms for:

- Price
- Delivery
- Quality
- Warranty
- Returns
- Authenticity
- Payment
- Confidentiality
- Data handling

## 20. Service Provider Contracts

Service-provider agreements should define appropriate standards for quality, security, confidentiality and customer handling.

## 21. Technology Contracts

Technology contracts should be reviewed for:

- Data use
- Security
- Availability
- Support
- Pricing
- Renewal
- Exit rights
- Intellectual property

## 22. Data Processing

Contracts involving personal or sensitive information should address appropriate data-handling responsibilities consistent with DEC-0055 and applicable requirements.

## 23. Customer Terms

Customer-facing terms should be reviewed for clarity, consistency and applicable consumer requirements.

## 24. E-Commerce Terms

Online commerce should provide appropriate information about:

- Pricing
- Payment
- Delivery
- Returns
- Warranty
- Customer support
- Privacy

## 25. Refund and Return Policies

Customer policies should be consistent with applicable law and the operating controls established under DEC-0054.

## 26. Warranty Terms

Warranty representations should be accurate and should clearly distinguish company commitments from manufacturer obligations where applicable.

## 27. Advertising Compliance

Marketing claims should be supportable and should not materially mislead customers.

## 28. Pricing Claims

Claims involving discounts, savings, availability or comparisons should be supported by appropriate evidence.

## 29. Product Claims

Product specifications and performance claims should use reliable sources and approved information.

## 30. Intellectual Property

The company should protect and appropriately manage its:

- Trademarks
- Brand assets
- Copyrighted materials
- Domain names
- Software
- Proprietary processes

## 31. Brand Protection

Unauthorized use of company branding or confusingly similar representations should be assessed and addressed appropriately.

## 32. Third-Party IP

Employees should avoid using third-party intellectual property without appropriate rights or permission.

## 33. Software Licensing

Software used by the company should be appropriately licensed and tracked where necessary.

## 34. Open-Source Software

Software development should follow applicable open-source license requirements and maintain appropriate attribution/notice practices.

## 35. Confidentiality

Confidential company information should be protected through appropriate agreements and operating controls.

## 36. Non-Disclosure Agreements

NDAs may be used when sharing confidential information with appropriate external parties.

## 37. Employment Agreements

Employment arrangements should use appropriate agreements and policies consistent with applicable employment requirements.

## 38. Employee Handbook

As the workforce grows, material employee policies should be consolidated into a controlled handbook or equivalent policy system.

## 39. Workplace Compliance

People operations should monitor material employment requirements relating to:

- Pay
- Working time
- Leave
- Safety
- Non-discrimination
- Termination
- Employee records

## 40. Workplace Safety

Safety obligations should connect to DEC-0053 and DEC-0056.

## 41. Consumer Protection

The company should operate customer-facing processes in a manner consistent with applicable consumer-protection requirements.

## 42. Product Compliance

Products should be sourced and sold with appropriate attention to applicable safety, labeling, certification and regulatory requirements.

## 43. Product Documentation

Required product documentation should be retained and made available to relevant teams.

## 44. Import / Export Compliance

Cross-border transactions should address applicable customs, duties, documentation, sanctions and import/export requirements.

## 45. Customs Records

Material import documentation should be retained and reconciled with procurement and finance records.

## 46. Sanctions and Restricted Parties

Where applicable, the company should use proportionate screening and controls for restricted or sanctioned counterparties and transactions.

## 47. Anti-Bribery

The company should prohibit bribery and improper payments in accordance with applicable law.

## 48. Gifts and Hospitality

Gifts, hospitality and supplier benefits should follow documented limits and approval requirements.

## 49. Conflicts of Interest

Employees and decision-makers should disclose material conflicts of interest.

## 50. Whistleblowing / Speak-Up

The company should provide an appropriate route for reporting suspected misconduct or compliance violations.

## 51. Non-Retaliation

Good-faith reporting should not result in retaliation.

## 52. Investigations

Material allegations should be assessed promptly and investigated in a fair, documented manner appropriate to the issue.

## 53. Evidence Preservation

Potentially important evidence should be preserved when a dispute, investigation or legal obligation requires it.

## 54. Litigation Management

Material disputes should have a designated owner and, where appropriate, qualified legal counsel.

## 55. Claims Register

Material claims, disputes and legal matters should be tracked with status, owner, exposure and next actions.

## 56. Settlement Authority

Settlements and material legal commitments should follow defined approval thresholds.

## 57. Regulatory Inquiries

Government or regulatory inquiries should be routed to authorized personnel and responded to accurately and on time.

## 58. Regulatory Correspondence

Important regulatory communications should be preserved in the appropriate legal/compliance repository.

## 59. Compliance Evidence

The company should maintain evidence demonstrating completion of material compliance activities.

## 60. Compliance Testing

Material controls should be periodically tested or reviewed according to risk.

## 61. Compliance Exceptions

Exceptions should be documented, risk-assessed, assigned and closed or formally accepted.

## 62. Corrective Actions

Compliance findings should have accountable owners and target dates.

## 63. Compliance Training

Employees should receive training appropriate to their role and exposure to legal/compliance risks.

## 64. Policy Management

Material policies should have:

- Owner
- Version
- Approval
- Effective date
- Review date

## 65. Policy Review

Policies should be reviewed when laws, systems, products or business models materially change.

## 66. Records Management

Legal and compliance records should be retained according to applicable requirements and the company's retention framework.

## 67. Legal Holds

Where required, routine deletion of potentially relevant records should be suspended under an appropriate legal hold process.

## 68. Privacy Coordination

Legal/compliance operations should coordinate with privacy controls under DEC-0055.

## 69. Cybersecurity Coordination

Security incidents with legal or regulatory implications should be escalated under DEC-0055 and this framework.

## 70. Finance Coordination

Tax, payments, accounting records and financial compliance should connect to DEC-0057.

## 71. HR Coordination

Employment and workforce compliance should connect to DEC-0056.

## 72. Procurement Coordination

Supplier contracts and compliance should connect to DEC-0052.

## 73. Store Coordination

Store licenses, leases, safety and facility requirements should connect to DEC-0053.

## 74. Customer Service Coordination

Warranty, returns, complaints and customer commitments should connect to DEC-0054.

## 75. Contract Risk Review

Material contracts should be reviewed for:

- Liability
- Indemnity
- Warranty
- Confidentiality
- Data protection
- Termination
- Dispute resolution
- Insurance

## 76. Liability Management

Management should understand material contractual and operational liability exposures.

## 77. Insurance Coordination

Insurance requirements in contracts and enterprise risk controls should align with DEC-0051.

## 78. Vendor Compliance

Material vendors should meet relevant contractual, legal, safety, security and ethical requirements.

## 79. Compliance Due Diligence

Higher-risk counterparties should receive proportionate due diligence before commitment.

## 80. Third-Party Risk

Third-party legal/compliance risk should be evaluated based on access, geography, activity, financial exposure and regulatory sensitivity.

## 81. AI and Legal Compliance

AI tools may assist with:

- Contract search
- Obligation extraction
- Policy comparison
- Compliance calendars
- Drafting support
- Risk triage

AI output should not be treated as legal advice without appropriate human review.

## 82. AI Contract Controls

Sensitive contracts should only be provided to approved AI systems consistent with DEC-0055 and applicable confidentiality obligations.

## 83. Automated Alerts

The company should use automated reminders for important:

- Renewals
- Expiries
- Licenses
- Filing dates
- Compliance reviews

## 84. Compliance Dashboard

Management should eventually monitor:

- Expiring licenses
- Contract renewals
- Open compliance actions
- Legal matters
- Regulatory inquiries
- Policy reviews
- Training completion

## 85. Legal Spend

Material external legal spend should be monitored for budget, matter and business impact.

## 86. Outside Counsel

External counsel should be selected according to expertise, independence, responsiveness, cost and conflict considerations.

## 87. Legal Privilege

Where applicable, communications intended to receive legal advice should be handled in a manner that preserves applicable legal protections.

## 88. Compliance Culture

Compliance should be treated as a practical operating discipline rather than paperwork performed only before audits.

## 89. Management Escalation

Material legal, regulatory or ethical risks should reach appropriate management promptly.

## 90. Board / Owner Reporting

Where applicable, material legal and compliance risks should be included in leadership reporting.

## 91. Risk Scoring

Legal/compliance risks may be prioritized using:

- Likelihood
- Impact
- Regulatory severity
- Customer impact
- Financial exposure
- Reputational impact

## 92. Regulatory Readiness

The company should maintain sufficient records and ownership to respond efficiently to legitimate regulatory inspections or requests.

## 93. Business Expansion

New stores, products, markets, channels or services should receive appropriate legal/compliance review before launch.

## 94. New Product Review

New product categories should be screened for applicable safety, labeling, warranty, import and consumer requirements.

## 95. New Market Review

Entering a new jurisdiction should trigger assessment of relevant:

- Corporate requirements
- Tax
- Employment
- Consumer law
- Privacy
- Product requirements
- Import/export rules

## 96. Compliance Change Management

Material regulatory changes should be translated into operational requirements, owners and deadlines.

## 97. Audit Readiness

The company should maintain organized evidence so important compliance activities can be demonstrated without excessive reconstruction.

## 98. Continuous Improvement

Legal and compliance processes should become more systematic as the organization grows, while remaining proportionate to actual risk.

## 99. Key Risks

Major legal and compliance risks include:

- Missed licenses or filings
- Contractual disputes
- Consumer-law violations
- Product non-compliance
- Employment violations
- Privacy violations
- IP infringement
- Bribery/corruption
- Import/export failures
- Regulatory penalties
- Poor evidence retention

## 100. Success Standard

The legal/compliance system is successful when management can answer:

- What obligations apply to us?
- Who owns each important obligation?
- What is expiring or due soon?
- What contracts have material commitments?
- What legal/compliance risks are open?
- Where do we need professional advice?

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish legal/compliance ownership and risk registers.
2. Build the license, permit and regulatory calendar.
3. Establish the contract register and controlled repository.
4. Standardize contract approval and signing authority.
5. Document customer, supplier, employment and technology compliance requirements.
6. Establish compliance evidence, policy and training controls.
7. Formalize claims, disputes and regulatory inquiry management.
8. Connect legal/compliance workflows with finance, HR, procurement, security and store operations.
9. Introduce automated obligation and renewal alerts.
10. Use controlled AI assistance for contract and compliance workflows.
11. Continuously review obligations as K&S expands into new products, stores, channels or jurisdictions.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **legal, compliance, contracts and regulatory management framework**. Specific legal requirements, contract language, filings and regulatory interpretations should be confirmed with qualified professionals and relevant authorities for the applicable jurisdiction.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document legal, regulatory, financial, operational and customer implications.
