# DEC-0032 — People, Hiring & Employee Development System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0032  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS People, Hiring and Employee Development System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0028, DEC-0029, DEC-0031

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build its organization around **capable, trustworthy, curious, customer-focused people** and a structured system for hiring, onboarding, training, performance, leadership development, and continuous learning.

Technology and processes can create leverage, but the company's reputation will ultimately be delivered by its people.

> **Hire for character and potential. Develop for excellence. Reward the behavior that builds trust.**

## 1. People Philosophy

KAMLESH & SONS should cultivate a culture that is:

- Customer-first
- Knowledge-driven
- Respectful
- Accountable
- Curious
- Calm under pressure
- Technology-positive
- Improvement-oriented
- Professional
- Entrepreneurial

Premium service requires premium behavior, not merely premium interiors.

## 2. Hiring Principles

Hiring decisions should consider both capability and character.

Relevant evaluation areas may include:

- Integrity
- Customer orientation
- Communication
- Learning ability
- Product curiosity
- Reliability
- Problem solving
- Team behavior
- Relevant technical or commercial capability

Hiring should not be based solely on prior sales volume or credentials.

## 3. Role Clarity

Every significant role should eventually have a documented role profile covering:

- Role title
- Purpose
- Responsibilities
- Decision authority
- Required capabilities
- Reporting relationship
- Key performance indicators
- Development expectations

Roles should be designed around outcomes rather than unnecessary task lists.

## 4. Store Team Structure

The eventual flagship-store structure may include functions such as:

- Store leadership
- Technology advisors/sales staff
- Customer experience/service staff
- Inventory/operations
- Technical support
- Administration/finance support

The exact organization chart should be determined by store size, operating model, and workload rather than copied blindly from another retailer.

## 5. Recruitment Process

A controlled recruitment process should eventually include:

**Workforce Need → Role Definition → Sourcing → Screening → Interview → Practical Assessment → Reference/Verification where appropriate → Selection → Offer → Onboarding**

The process should be consistent enough to reduce bias while allowing judgment.

## 6. Product Knowledge

Technology retail employees must continuously understand the products they recommend.

Training should cover, as relevant:

- Product specifications
- Use cases
- Strengths and limitations
- Competitor comparisons
- Compatibility
- Accessories
- Warranty/service
- Financing information
- Common customer questions

Employees should be trained to explain technology simply rather than use technical language to impress customers.

## 7. Customer Experience Training

Employees should be trained according to DEC-0028 and DEC-0029, including:

- Hospitality
- Consultation
- Demonstration
- Communication
- Complaint handling
- Service recovery
- Privacy
- Escalation

Training should use practical scenarios, not only written material.

## 8. Onboarding

Every employee should eventually receive a structured onboarding path covering:

- Company story and philosophy
- Brand standards
- Customer experience
- Role expectations
- Store operations
- Security
- Privacy
- Technology systems
- Product knowledge
- Relevant SOPs
- Escalation procedures

Onboarding should include supervised practical work before independent responsibility for critical tasks.

## 9. Training System

Learning should be continuous rather than limited to joining the company.

The training system should eventually include:

- New-hire training
- Product launch training
- SOP updates
- Customer experience training
- Technology training
- Compliance/security training
- Leadership development
- Refresher training

Critical training completion should be recorded where appropriate.

## 10. Certification

Some responsibilities may require demonstrated competence before an employee performs them independently.

Potential certification areas include:

- Product consultation
- Device setup/data transfer
- Service intake
- Inventory handling
- Financing procedures
- Security-sensitive processes
- AI-assisted workflows

Certification requirements should be proportional to operational risk.

## 11. Performance Management

Performance should be evaluated using a balanced view of:

- Business outcomes
- Customer experience
- Operational discipline
- Product knowledge
- Team contribution
- Learning and development
- Compliance

A single sales number should not define employee performance.

## 12. Incentives and Behavior

As established in DEC-0031, commercial incentives should not encourage harmful behavior.

Rewards should reinforce:

- Customer trust
- Sustainable sales
- Knowledge
- Service quality
- Teamwork
- Operational accuracy
- Continuous improvement

The company should avoid compensation systems that encourage unnecessary upselling, misleading claims, or policy violations.

## 13. Career Development

KAMLESH & SONS should provide visible paths for employees to grow.

Potential paths may include:

**Advisor → Senior Advisor → Specialist → Store Leader**

or specialist tracks such as:

**Technology → Service → Operations → Training → AI/Systems**

Actual career architecture should evolve with the company's size and roles.

## 14. Leadership Development

Future leaders should be developed intentionally rather than promoted only because they are strong individual contributors.

Leadership development should include:

- Coaching
- Decision making
- Conflict resolution
- Customer recovery
- Performance management
- Financial understanding
- Operational control
- People development

## 15. Internal Knowledge System

Employees should have access to a reliable internal knowledge environment containing approved:

- SOPs
- Policies
- Product information
- Training material
- FAQs
- Decision records
- Operational updates

The Internal AI established under DEC-0026 may eventually provide a conversational interface to approved internal knowledge.

## 16. AI and Employees

AI should augment employees by reducing repetitive information work and improving access to knowledge.

Potential uses include:

- SOP lookup
- Product comparison support
- Training assistance
- Knowledge checks
- Meeting summaries
- Workflow guidance
- Internal search

AI should not silently make employment decisions, disciplinary decisions, or high-impact judgments without appropriate human oversight and approved governance.

## 17. Employee Privacy

Employee information should be collected and used only for legitimate business purposes and handled appropriately.

Access should be limited according to role and need.

People systems should not become unrestricted surveillance systems.

## 18. Feedback Culture

Employees should have safe and practical ways to raise:

- Customer problems
- Process weaknesses
- Product issues
- Security concerns
- Ethical concerns
- Improvement ideas

The company should distinguish between a person reporting a problem in good faith and a person intentionally violating a standard.

## 19. Innovation Participation

Employees should be encouraged to contribute ideas to the Innovation Vault where appropriate.

Useful ideas should be evaluated through a structured process rather than disappearing into informal conversations.

Employee contribution should not require every idea to be implemented.

## 20. Conduct and Professional Standards

Employees should be expected to demonstrate:

- Honesty
- Respect
- Confidentiality
- Professional communication
- Appropriate customer boundaries
- Responsible system use
- Compliance with approved policies

Detailed conduct requirements should be developed through the company's formal policy and legal framework.

## 21. Workforce Planning

As the company grows, staffing decisions should consider:

- Store traffic
- Sales volume
- Service workload
- Operating hours
- Customer experience requirements
- Inventory workload
- Management span
- Seasonal demand

The company should avoid both chronic understaffing and unnecessary organizational complexity.

## 22. Measurement

Future people dashboards may include:

- Time to hire
- Training completion
- Certification status
- Employee retention
- Absence patterns
- Internal promotion
- Customer experience indicators
- Productivity
- Product knowledge assessment
- Employee engagement

Metrics should be used to improve the organization, not create fear-driven management.

## 23. Risks

Major people risks include:

- Hiring for sales volume over character
- Weak product knowledge
- Poor onboarding
- Inconsistent training
- Toxic management
- Misaligned incentives
- Excessive employee turnover
- Knowledge concentrated in individuals
- Uncontrolled employee access to systems
- AI replacing judgment where human accountability is required

The company should treat culture and organizational capability as strategic assets.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Define organizational principles.
2. Identify critical roles for the flagship store.
3. Create role profiles.
4. Design the recruitment process.
5. Build onboarding.
6. Establish product and CX training.
7. Create priority certifications.
8. Establish performance expectations.
9. Develop leadership pathways.
10. Build the internal knowledge system.
11. Introduce employee development metrics.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **people, hiring and employee development direction**. It does not yet define final salaries, headcount, employment contracts, bonus percentages, leave policies, disciplinary procedures, or a complete organization chart.

Those require financial planning, operational requirements, legal review, and further decisions.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. The people system should evolve with store scale, company culture, labor requirements, technology adoption, and leadership maturity.

Material changes should document the rationale, organizational impact, affected roles, and related repository documents.
