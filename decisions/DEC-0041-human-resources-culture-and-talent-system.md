# DEC-0041 — Human Resources, Culture & Talent System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0041  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Human Resources, Culture and Talent System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0032, DEC-0036, DEC-0038, DEC-0040

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build its people system around **character, competence, hospitality, ownership, learning and long-term development**.

The company will treat employees as a strategic capability rather than simply a staffing expense.

> **Hire for character. Develop for capability. Lead with standards.**

## 1. People Philosophy

KAMLESH & SONS should attract and develop people who are:

- Honest
- Respectful
- Curious
- Customer-focused
- Responsible
- Technically capable or willing to learn
- Calm under pressure
- Comfortable with technology
- Accountable for outcomes

## 2. Culture Principles

The culture should reinforce:

- Customer respect
- Truthful recommendations
- Continuous learning
- Ownership
- Teamwork
- Professionalism
- Safety
- Ethical conduct
- Long-term thinking

Premium customer experience begins with internal culture.

## 3. Employee Lifecycle

The people system should eventually cover:

**Plan → Recruit → Select → Onboard → Train → Perform → Develop → Recognize → Retain → Transition**

Each stage should have appropriate documentation and ownership.

## 4. Workforce Planning

Staffing should be planned against:

- Store traffic
- Business hours
- Product categories
- Service demand
- Sales requirements
- Corporate sales
- Technology operations
- Future expansion

Headcount should grow with business requirements rather than simply following ambition.

## 5. Role Design

Every important role should eventually have a role profile covering:

- Purpose
- Responsibilities
- Decision authority
- Required capabilities
- Key performance indicators
- Reporting relationship
- Development expectations

Roles should be designed to avoid unnecessary duplication.

## 6. Recruitment Philosophy

Recruitment should prioritize long-term suitability over filling vacancies quickly.

Selection should assess:

- Integrity
- Customer orientation
- Communication
- Learning ability
- Technical capability where relevant
- Problem solving
- Reliability
- Team behavior

## 7. Hiring Process

A controlled hiring process should eventually include:

1. Workforce need
2. Role definition
3. Job description
4. Candidate sourcing
5. Screening
6. Interview/assessment
7. Reference/background checks where appropriate and lawful
8. Selection
9. Offer
10. Onboarding

Employment practices must comply with applicable law.

## 8. Equal Opportunity and Respect

KAMLESH & SONS should maintain a professional workplace free from unlawful discrimination, harassment and retaliation.

Employment decisions should be based on legitimate role-related criteria.

Specific legal requirements should be verified under DEC-0036.

## 9. Onboarding

Every new employee should receive structured onboarding covering:

- Company story and philosophy
- Brand standards
- Customer experience
- Role expectations
- Store procedures
- Technology systems
- Security/privacy
- Code of conduct
- Safety
- Escalation procedures

## 10. Probation / Initial Development

New employees should have a defined initial development period with clear expectations and feedback.

The exact employment terms and timelines should be defined in accordance with applicable law and company policy.

## 11. Training System

Training should be continuous rather than limited to onboarding.

Core learning areas may include:

- Product knowledge
- Customer discovery
- Demonstration skills
- Sales ethics
- POS/CRM
- Inventory
- Service
- Financing
- Privacy
- Security
- AI tools
- Brand standards

## 12. Product Certification

Customer-facing employees should demonstrate sufficient product knowledge before independently advising customers on important product categories.

Certification may include:

- Product tests
- Scenario assessments
- Demonstrations
- Customer-service simulations

## 13. AI Literacy

As K&S OS develops, employees should understand how to use approved AI systems safely.

Training should cover:

- What AI can do
- What AI cannot guarantee
- Approved use cases
- Sensitive information
- Verification
- Human escalation
- Internal AI access boundaries

## 14. Performance Management

Performance management should combine business outcomes with behavior and customer impact.

Potential dimensions include:

- Customer experience
- Sales performance
- Accuracy
- Product knowledge
- Service quality
- Team contribution
- Reliability
- Learning progress

## 15. Avoiding Unhealthy Incentives

The company should avoid incentive structures that encourage:

- Misleading customers
- Overselling
- Unauthorized discounts
- Hiding problems
- Manipulating metrics
- Unhealthy internal competition

Commercial performance matters, but it must remain consistent with company values.

## 16. Recognition

Recognition should reward behaviors that strengthen the company.

Potential recognition areas include:

- Exceptional customer service
- Problem solving
- Learning
- Teamwork
- Process improvement
- Integrity
- Innovation

## 17. Career Development

Employees should have opportunities to grow through:

- Increased responsibility
- Specialist roles
- Leadership
- Product expertise
- Technology expertise
- Training responsibilities

Career paths should evolve as the organization grows.

## 18. Internal Mobility

Where practical, employees should be able to move between functions based on capability and business needs.

Potential paths may include:

**Sales → Senior Sales → Specialist → Store Leadership**

or

**Service → Senior Service → Service Leadership**

## 19. Leadership Standards

Managers should be expected to:

- Set clear expectations
- Coach employees
- Give feedback
- Protect customer experience
- Address problems early
- Develop successors
- Model ethical behavior

Leadership is responsible for culture through behavior, not slogans.

## 20. Store Manager as People Leader

The Store Manager should own day-to-day people leadership in partnership with the appropriate company leadership.

Responsibilities include:

- Scheduling
- Coaching
- Training readiness
- Performance conversations
- Team culture
- Attendance management
- Escalation
- Development planning

## 21. Attendance and Scheduling

Attendance and working-time practices should be documented and managed consistently while complying with applicable employment requirements.

Scheduling should balance employee wellbeing with customer and business needs.

## 22. Employee Conduct

The company should establish clear expectations regarding:

- Honesty
- Respect
- Customer treatment
- Confidentiality
- Conflicts of interest
- Technology use
- Security
- Gifts and benefits
- Company property

## 23. Confidentiality

Employees may have access to confidential business information.

Access should be limited according to role and business need.

Employees should understand their responsibilities regarding:

- Customer information
- Pricing
- Supplier terms
- Internal documentation
- Credentials
- Business plans
- Personal data

## 24. Employee Data

Employee personal information should be handled through appropriate privacy and security controls.

Only necessary information should be collected and retained.

## 25. Workplace Safety

The company should provide a safe workplace and appropriate safety procedures.

Store-specific emergency and safety procedures should be documented separately.

## 26. Grievances and Reporting

Employees should have appropriate channels to raise:

- Workplace concerns
- Safety concerns
- Harassment concerns
- Ethical concerns
- Fraud concerns
- Management issues

Reports should be handled respectfully and without retaliation, subject to applicable law and policy.

## 27. Whistleblowing / Ethical Escalation

The company should maintain a controlled mechanism for reporting serious misconduct or compliance concerns.

The process should protect confidentiality as far as reasonably possible and provide appropriate investigation and escalation.

## 28. Employee Feedback

Management should periodically collect employee feedback on:

- Leadership
- Training
- Work processes
- Customer challenges
- Tools
- Culture
- Development

Feedback should produce actions where appropriate.

## 29. Knowledge Management

Employees should learn from documented company knowledge rather than relying only on individual memory.

K&S OS should become the institutional knowledge layer for:

- SOPs
- Policies
- Product knowledge
- Decisions
- Training
- Research

## 30. Internal AI for Employees

The future Internal AI may assist employees with:

- SOP retrieval
- Product knowledge
- Training questions
- Policy lookup
- Process guidance
- Internal documentation search

Access should be role-based and governed under DEC-0035 and DEC-0036.

## 31. Employee Technology

Company devices, accounts and software should be assigned according to role.

Access should be removed or changed when employees change roles or leave.

## 32. Joiner-Mover-Leaver Controls

The company should maintain a controlled lifecycle for employee access:

**Joiner → Role Change → Leaver**

Relevant systems may include:

- Email
- CRM
- POS
- Inventory
- Internal AI
- GitHub/documentation
- Customer data

## 33. Compensation Philosophy

Compensation should be competitive enough to attract capable people while remaining sustainable for the business.

The system may eventually combine:

- Base compensation
- Performance incentives
- Recognition
- Benefits
- Development opportunities

Specific compensation levels should be established through financial and market analysis.

## 34. Incentive Design

Incentives should reinforce company strategy.

Balanced measures should reduce the risk of optimizing one metric at the expense of:

- Customer trust
- Margin
- Service quality
- Teamwork
- Compliance

## 35. Employee Benefits

Benefits should be developed according to employee needs, business economics and applicable requirements.

Potential future benefits may include:

- Training support
- Technology learning programs
- Health/wellbeing initiatives
- Leave benefits
- Recognition programs

Specific benefits require separate decisions.

## 36. Succession Planning

Critical roles should eventually have identified backup capability.

The goal is to prevent the business from becoming dependent on one person for critical knowledge or decisions.

## 37. Expansion Talent Model

As KAMLESH & SONS expands, the people system should support replication of culture and operating standards across locations.

Potential structure:

**Flagship → Regional Leadership → National Functions**

## 38. Culture Scaling

Expansion should not mean copying every process blindly.

Core principles should remain consistent while local execution may adapt to market conditions.

## 39. Employee Offboarding

A controlled offboarding process should include, as appropriate:

- Handover
- Access removal
- Company property return
- Knowledge transfer
- Final administrative steps
- Exit feedback

## 40. HR Records

Employee records should be accurate, secure and retained according to applicable requirements and company policy.

Access should be restricted to authorized personnel.

## 41. People Metrics

Management may eventually monitor:

- Headcount
- Hiring time
- Training completion
- Product certification
- Employee retention
- Absence
- Internal promotions
- Performance trends
- Employee feedback
- Customer outcomes

Metrics should support better leadership rather than surveillance for its own sake.

## 42. Risks

Major people risks include:

- Poor hiring
- Skill gaps
- High turnover
- Weak management
- Culture dilution
- Misaligned incentives
- Employee misconduct
- Data/privacy breaches
- Access-control failures
- Overdependence on key individuals
- Burnout

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Define organization and role architecture.
2. Establish hiring standards.
3. Create onboarding program.
4. Build training and certification system.
5. Establish performance and feedback cycles.
6. Define compensation/incentive principles.
7. Establish employee records and access controls.
8. Build leadership-development pathways.
9. Establish succession planning.
10. Connect Internal AI and knowledge systems.
11. Develop expansion-ready talent processes.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **human resources, culture and talent direction**. It does not finalize employment contracts, compensation amounts, benefits, legal classifications, statutory policies, or individual hiring decisions.

Those must be developed using applicable law, professional HR/legal guidance, market research and company financial capacity.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. The people system should evolve as KAMLESH & SONS moves from a flagship operation to a multi-location technology company.

Material changes should document the rationale, people impact, risks, affected policies, and related decision IDs.
