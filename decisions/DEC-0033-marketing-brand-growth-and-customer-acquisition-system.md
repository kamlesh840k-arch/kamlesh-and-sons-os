# DEC-0033 — Marketing, Brand Growth & Customer Acquisition System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0033  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Marketing, Brand Growth and Customer Acquisition System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0001, DEC-0028, DEC-0030, DEC-0031, DEC-0032

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build marketing as a **trust, education, experience, and relationship system**, not as a discount-only advertising function.

Marketing must strengthen the premium brand while creating measurable customer demand and supporting sustainable commercial growth.

> **Build the brand. Earn attention. Educate customers. Create relationships. Convert trust into growth.**

## 1. Brand-Led Growth

Marketing should reinforce the established position of KAMLESH & SONS as a premium technology house.

Communication should feel:

- Premium
- Minimal
- Modern
- Knowledgeable
- Human
- Confident
- Trustworthy

Marketing should avoid unnecessary visual clutter, exaggerated claims, and permanent discount-driven messaging.

## 2. Customer Acquisition Philosophy

Customer acquisition should focus on attracting customers who value:

- Genuine products
- Expert advice
- Product experience
- Transparent pricing
- Financing access
- Setup and service
- Long-term support

The objective is not simply to maximize footfall. It is to build a high-quality customer base that can become repeat customers and advocates.

## 3. Customer Journey

Marketing should connect to the full customer lifecycle:

**Discover → Learn → Visit/Engage → Experience → Purchase → Onboard → Support → Return → Recommend**

Marketing performance should therefore not be evaluated only on the first transaction.

## 4. Target Customer Understanding

Before major campaigns, KAMLESH & SONS should define the relevant customer segment, need, problem, and value proposition.

Research may include:

- Customer demographics
- Technology ownership
- Purchase behavior
- Brand preferences
- Financing behavior
- Local demand
- Customer pain points
- Competitor experience

The company should validate assumptions through evidence rather than relying only on intuition.

## 5. Local Market First

The initial growth strategy should prioritize Nepalgunj and Western Nepal, consistent with the expansion strategy.

Local marketing should build recognition and trust before the company attempts broad national awareness campaigns.

The approach should be:

**Local relevance → Regional reputation → National brand**

## 6. Content Strategy

Content should educate and inspire rather than only advertise.

Potential content pillars include:

- Product education
- Technology comparisons
- Buying guides
- Setup tips
- Accessories and ecosystem education
- AI and future technology
- Customer stories
- Store experience
- Service education
- Workshops/events
- Company story

Content should be accurate, useful, and consistent with the brand.

## 7. Product Launch Marketing

Major launches should coordinate:

- Product availability
- Pricing
- Financing
- Store displays
- Staff knowledge
- Website/catalog information
- AI knowledge
- Social content
- Customer communication

Marketing must not create demand for products the company cannot reliably supply without clearly communicating availability.

## 8. Store as a Marketing Asset

The flagship store itself should be a major brand experience.

Marketing should encourage customers to:

- Visit
- Explore
- Try products
- Attend demonstrations
- Ask questions
- Experience AI Concierge
- Receive consultation

The store should generate word-of-mouth because the experience is distinctive, not because customers are pressured to promote it.

## 9. Digital Presence

The future digital ecosystem may include:

- Official website
- Product catalog
- Customer portal
- AI Concierge
- Social channels
- Messaging channels
- Appointment booking
- Service communication
- Email/SMS/approved messaging

Each channel should have a clear role and ownership.

## 10. Social Media Philosophy

Social channels should be treated as brand and community platforms rather than coupon boards.

Content should demonstrate:

- Expertise
- Product experience
- People
- Store culture
- Customer education
- New technology
- Community participation

Posting frequency should follow quality and customer relevance rather than arbitrary volume targets.

## 11. Performance Marketing

Paid advertising may be used where measurable customer acquisition justifies the investment.

Campaigns should have:

- Defined objective
- Target audience
- Value proposition
- Budget
- Creative concept
- Landing destination
- Conversion event
- Measurement plan

Performance marketing should complement rather than replace brand building.

## 12. Offers and Promotions

Promotions should be strategically used rather than permanent.

Appropriate purposes may include:

- Product launches
- Seasonal demand
- Inventory balancing
- Financing campaigns
- Bundles
- Customer acquisition experiments
- Loyalty offers

Every promotion should have defined terms and an owner, consistent with DEC-0030 and DEC-0031.

## 13. CRM and Lifecycle Marketing

Marketing should eventually use the CRM to provide relevant communications based on customer consent and relationship context.

Potential lifecycle events include:

- Welcome
- Purchase confirmation
- Setup support
- Warranty reminders
- Service updates
- Accessory recommendations
- Product education
- Upgrade opportunities
- Loyalty communication
- Event invitations

Communications should provide value and avoid spam.

## 14. Customer Consent and Privacy

Marketing data should be collected and used only for legitimate purposes with appropriate consent and controls.

Customers should have clear choices regarding promotional communication.

Marketing should never treat personal information as an unrestricted advertising asset.

## 15. Referral and Advocacy

Satisfied customers may become one of the strongest growth channels.

Future programs may include:

- Referral benefits
- Customer stories
- Community events
- Product workshops
- Loyalty recognition

Referral programs should reward genuine advocacy rather than incentivize misleading reviews or artificial engagement.

## 16. Community and Events

KAMLESH & SONS may build community through:

- Technology workshops
- Product demonstrations
- Student/education sessions
- Business technology events
- Launch events
- Customer appreciation events

Events should reinforce the company's role as a trusted technology resource.

## 17. Partnerships

Potential marketing partnerships may include:

- Technology brands
- Banks/financing partners
- Educational institutions
- Businesses
- Creators
- Local organizations
- Corporate clients

Partnerships should be evaluated for brand fit, customer value, credibility, economics, and operational requirements.

## 18. Brand Consistency

Marketing assets should follow the approved brand system.

This includes, where applicable:

- Logo usage
- Typography
- Color system
- Photography
- Tone of voice
- Product presentation
- Store visuals
- Digital interfaces

Marketing should not create disconnected sub-brands without strategic approval.

## 19. Marketing + Sales

Marketing and sales should operate as one commercial system while maintaining clear responsibilities.

Marketing creates awareness, education, demand, and qualified opportunities.

Sales converts customer intent into an appropriate purchase and relationship.

Feedback from sales should continuously improve marketing messages.

## 20. Marketing + Inventory

Marketing campaigns must be coordinated with inventory availability.

Before launching major product campaigns, the responsible teams should verify:

- Stock
- Pricing
- Financing
- Product information
- Demo readiness
- Staff readiness
- Service readiness

This prevents marketing from creating an experience the store cannot deliver.

## 21. Marketing + AI

Customer AI may become an important discovery and education channel.

AI may help customers:

- Explore products
- Compare options
- Understand specifications
- Find accessories
- Receive recommendations
- Continue conversations after visiting the store

AI responses used in marketing must be based on approved information and must not make unsupported claims.

## 22. Measurement Framework

Future marketing dashboards should distinguish between brand and performance metrics.

Potential metrics include:

### Awareness
- Reach
- Search interest
- Direct traffic
- Brand recognition

### Engagement
- Content engagement
- Website engagement
- AI interactions
- Event participation

### Acquisition
- Qualified leads
- Store visits
- Appointments
- Conversion rate
- Customer acquisition cost

### Relationship
- Repeat purchase
- Referral rate
- Customer lifetime value
- Retention

### Brand Health
- Customer satisfaction
- Trust indicators
- Recommendation intent

Metrics should be interpreted together rather than optimized individually.

## 23. Marketing Experiments

New campaigns should be treated as experiments where appropriate.

An experiment should define:

- Hypothesis
- Target customer
- Offer/message
- Channel
- Budget
- Success metric
- Duration
- Result
- Learning
- Decision

Significant learnings should be documented in the Innovation Vault or relevant decision records.

## 24. Reputation Management

The company should monitor customer feedback across appropriate channels.

Complaints should be treated as opportunities to identify operational problems, consistent with DEC-0028.

The company should never manipulate, suppress, or fabricate customer reviews.

## 25. Risks

Major marketing risks include:

- Discount dependency
- Misleading advertising
- Brand inconsistency
- Campaigns without inventory
- Poor customer targeting
- Spam
- Privacy violations
- Vanity metrics
- Influencer misalignment
- Reputation damage
- Overpromising technology or services

Marketing governance should protect both customer trust and commercial sustainability.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Finalize brand system.
2. Define target customer segments.
3. Establish core messaging.
4. Build local market research.
5. Establish digital presence.
6. Create content pillars and workflow.
7. Connect marketing with CRM and sales.
8. Build campaign measurement.
9. Pilot local acquisition campaigns.
10. Develop referral/community programs.
11. Scale what evidence shows is effective.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **marketing, brand growth and customer acquisition direction**. It does not approve specific advertising budgets, agencies, influencers, platforms, campaign calendars, or promotional percentages.

Those require research, financial analysis, brand governance, and separate approval.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Marketing should evolve as customer research, store performance, digital channels, competition, and the brand mature.

Material changes should document the rationale, customer/brand impact, financial implications, and related repository documents.
