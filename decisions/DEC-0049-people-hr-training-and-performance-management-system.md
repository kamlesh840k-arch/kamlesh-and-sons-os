# DEC-0049 — People, HR, Training & Performance Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0049  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS People, HR, Training and Performance Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0042, DEC-0045, DEC-0046, DEC-0047, DEC-0048

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build a people system that develops capable, ethical and customer-focused employees while creating clear accountability and opportunities for growth.

The company will treat people capability as a strategic operating asset rather than an administrative function.

> **Build people who can carry the standard, then build systems that help them raise it.**

## 1. People Philosophy

The people system should balance:

- High standards
- Fairness
- Learning
- Accountability
- Respect
- Ownership
- Career growth
- Customer focus

## 2. Organizational Structure

Roles should have clear responsibilities, decision rights and reporting relationships.

Structure should evolve as the company grows without creating unnecessary hierarchy.

## 3. Role Design

Each important role should have a controlled role profile containing, where appropriate:

- Purpose
- Responsibilities
- Authority
- Required capabilities
- KPIs
- Reporting line
- Escalation responsibilities

## 4. Workforce Planning

Hiring plans should reflect:

- Store count
- Revenue scale
- Service demand
- Operating hours
- Skill requirements
- Expansion plans

The company should avoid hiring ahead of validated need without a clear business rationale.

## 5. Recruitment

Recruitment should prioritize:

- Integrity
- Customer orientation
- Learning ability
- Communication
- Product curiosity
- Reliability
- Role-specific capability

## 6. Fair Selection

Hiring decisions should use consistent criteria appropriate to the role.

The company should avoid discriminatory or irrelevant selection criteria.

## 7. Background and Reference Checks

Where appropriate and lawful, roles involving money, sensitive customer information or high-value inventory may require additional verification.

Checks should be proportionate to the risk.

## 8. Offer and Onboarding

New employees should receive clear information about:

- Role
- Compensation
- Working expectations
- Policies
- Reporting line
- Training
- Customer standards
- Security responsibilities

## 9. First-Week Onboarding

New employees should be introduced to:

- K&S mission and values
- Brand standards
- Store operations
- Customer experience
- Product categories
- Security
- Privacy
- Finance controls relevant to role

## 10. Training Philosophy

Training should combine:

- Product knowledge
- Process knowledge
- Customer skills
- Technology skills
- Practical observation
- Coaching

Training should not be limited to slide presentations.

## 11. Product Knowledge

Customer-facing employees should understand products well enough to explain:

- Key features
- Practical benefits
- Trade-offs
- Compatibility
- Accessories
- Warranty/service considerations

## 12. Customer Experience Training

Training should reflect DEC-0046, including:

- Greeting
- Discovery
- Demonstration
- Recommendation
- Complaint handling
- Service recovery
- Follow-up

## 13. Sales Training

Training should reflect DEC-0048 and emphasize consultative selling rather than pressure selling.

## 14. Operational Training

Employees should be certified or signed off on critical SOPs appropriate to their roles.

## 15. Security Training

Employees should understand relevant requirements for:

- Customer data
- Passwords
- Devices
- Payments
- Inventory
- Access control
- Incident reporting

## 16. AI Literacy

Employees should learn how to use approved AI tools safely.

Training should cover:

- What AI can do
- What AI cannot be trusted to do
- Data handling
- Verification
- Approved use cases
- Escalation

## 17. Training Records

The company should maintain appropriate records of completed mandatory training.

## 18. Certification

High-risk processes may require formal competency confirmation before an employee performs them independently.

Examples may include:

- Cash reconciliation
- High-value inventory handling
- Device data transfer
- Financing workflows
- Service intake

## 19. Coaching

Managers should provide ongoing coaching rather than relying entirely on annual reviews.

## 20. Performance Management

Performance should be evaluated using a balanced view of:

- Results
- Customer experience
- Process discipline
- Team behavior
- Learning
- Ownership

## 21. KPI Design

Employee KPIs should be controllable, understandable and aligned with company goals.

No KPI should encourage behavior that contradicts customer trust or company policy.

## 22. Sales Incentives

Sales incentives should balance commercial performance with:

- Gross margin
- Customer satisfaction
- Returns
- Compliance
- Team behavior

## 23. Store Manager Performance

Store managers should be evaluated across:

- Sales
- Margin
- Customer experience
- Inventory accuracy
- Shrinkage
- Team capability
- Operational execution
- Staff development

## 24. Feedback

Employees should have regular opportunities to receive and give feedback.

Feedback should be specific, respectful and focused on behavior or outcomes.

## 25. Performance Improvement

When performance is below standard, managers should identify:

- Expected standard
- Current gap
- Root cause
- Support needed
- Improvement timeline
- Review point

## 26. Recognition

The company should recognize employees who demonstrate strong ownership, customer service, learning and operational excellence.

Recognition should reinforce desired behaviors rather than create unhealthy internal competition.

## 27. Career Paths

As the company grows, employees should have visible pathways such as:

**Associate → Senior Associate → Specialist/Lead → Store Manager → Regional/Functional Leadership**

Actual titles may evolve.

## 28. Internal Mobility

Qualified employees should have opportunities to move into new responsibilities when business needs and capability align.

## 29. Leadership Development

Future managers should receive training in:

- Coaching
- Planning
- Conflict resolution
- Financial literacy
- Customer experience
- Inventory management
- Hiring
- Decision-making

## 30. Management Standards

Managers should model the behavior expected from their teams.

A manager should not demand standards they personally ignore.

## 31. Attendance and Reliability

Attendance expectations should be clear and consistently applied.

Operational roles require reliable coverage.

## 32. Scheduling

Schedules should balance:

- Customer demand
- Store coverage
- Employee wellbeing
- Skill requirements
- Cost control

## 33. Leave and Absence

Leave processes should be documented and administered consistently according to applicable requirements and company policy.

## 34. Workplace Conduct

The company should maintain standards against:

- Harassment
- Bullying
- Discrimination
- Threats
- Retaliation
- Serious misconduct

Employees should have a safe way to raise concerns.

## 35. Speak-Up Culture

Employees should be able to report serious concerns without fear of retaliation.

Reports should be handled confidentially to the extent appropriate and possible.

## 36. Conflict of Interest

Employees should disclose relevant conflicts, especially in procurement, finance, hiring and supplier relationships.

## 37. Employee Data

Employee information should be handled according to appropriate privacy and security controls.

Access should be limited to legitimate business need.

## 38. Compensation Philosophy

Compensation should reflect role, capability, market conditions, performance and company economics.

Specific salary bands and incentive structures should be maintained separately.

## 39. Payroll Controls

Payroll should connect with attendance, approved compensation changes and finance controls without giving unnecessary access to sensitive employee information.

## 40. Separation

Employee departures should include controlled procedures for:

- Access removal
- Company property return
- Customer/inventory responsibility
- Final payroll processing
- Data protection
- Knowledge handover

## 41. Knowledge Transfer

Important role knowledge should not remain exclusively inside one employee's memory.

Critical procedures should be documented in K&S OS.

## 42. Succession Planning

Critical roles should eventually have identified backup capability.

This reduces dependency on individual employees.

## 43. Employee Experience

The company should seek to make work:

- Clear
- Fair
- Well-supported
- Development-oriented
- Respectful

## 44. Learning System

Training should become continuous rather than event-based.

Learning may include:

- Product launches
- Micro-learning
- Workshops
- Role-play
- Shadowing
- Coaching
- Knowledge checks

## 45. Knowledge Base

The future Internal AI and employee portal should provide access to approved:

- SOPs
- Policies
- Product information
- Training material
- Escalation procedures

## 46. Training Measurement

Training effectiveness should be assessed through evidence such as:

- Knowledge checks
- Observation
- Customer outcomes
- Process accuracy
- Reduced errors

Completion alone does not prove competence.

## 47. People Analytics

Management may use aggregated people metrics such as:

- Headcount
- Turnover
- Hiring time
- Training completion
- Internal promotion
- Absence
- Performance distribution

Analytics should respect employee privacy.

## 48. Workforce Risks

Major risks include:

- High turnover
- Skill gaps
- Key-person dependency
- Weak management
- Poor hiring
- Training gaps
- Misaligned incentives
- Workplace misconduct
- Employee data exposure

## 49. People and Expansion

New stores should use standardized role profiles, onboarding and training while allowing local leadership to adapt execution to legitimate local conditions.

## 50. AI-Assisted People Operations

AI may assist with:

- Training recommendations
- Knowledge retrieval
- Schedule analysis
- Workforce reporting
- Drafting role descriptions
- Learning content

AI should not independently make high-impact employment decisions without appropriate human review and governance.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Define organizational roles and responsibilities.
2. Establish recruitment and onboarding standards.
3. Create core role profiles.
4. Build product, customer and operational training.
5. Establish manager coaching routines.
6. Define performance and KPI frameworks.
7. Establish recognition and career pathways.
8. Implement employee-data and access controls.
9. Build the internal knowledge and training system.
10. Introduce AI-assisted learning and people analytics under governance.
11. Establish succession and expansion-ready workforce planning.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **people, HR, training and performance-management framework**. Specific employment policies, compensation bands, incentive plans, disciplinary procedures and local legal requirements should be maintained as controlled HR documents and reviewed appropriately.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document rationale, employee impact, operational risk, governance implications and related decision IDs.
