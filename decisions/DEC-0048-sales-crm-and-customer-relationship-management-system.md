# DEC-0048 — Sales, CRM & Customer Relationship Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0048  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Sales and Customer Relationship Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0045, DEC-0046, DEC-0047

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build a disciplined sales and CRM system that turns customer interactions into long-term relationships without compromising trust.

The system should make selling more consultative, follow-up more reliable, customer information more useful, and management visibility stronger.

> **A sale is a transaction. A relationship is an asset.**

## 1. Sales Philosophy

Sales should be based on:

- Customer need
- Product fit
- Honest guidance
- Demonstration
- Clear value
- Transparent pricing
- Reliable follow-through

## 2. Sales Process

The standard sales journey should eventually follow:

**Lead → Discover → Qualify → Recommend → Demonstrate → Compare → Decide → Purchase → Handover → Follow-up → Retain**

The exact process may vary by customer and product complexity.

## 3. Lead Sources

CRM should capture relevant lead sources such as:

- Store walk-ins
- Website
- AI Concierge
- Social media
- Phone inquiries
- Events
- Referrals
- Corporate outreach
- Existing customers

## 4. Lead Capture

Lead capture should collect only information useful for serving the customer or managing the sales process.

Potential fields include:

- Name
- Contact channel
- Product interest
- Budget range
- Timing
- Store/location
- Assigned employee
- Source
- Consent/preferences where applicable

## 5. Lead Qualification

Qualification should identify whether the customer has a meaningful current need and what assistance is appropriate.

Qualification must not become unnecessary interrogation.

## 6. Opportunity Management

Significant sales opportunities should have a clear status.

Example statuses:

- New
- Contacted
- Qualified
- Demonstration
- Proposal/quote
- Awaiting decision
- Won
- Lost
- Nurture

## 7. Follow-Up Discipline

Follow-up should be timely, useful and based on the customer's stated needs.

Employees should not repeatedly contact customers who have indicated they do not want further communication.

## 8. Customer Ownership

Every active opportunity should have a clear owner where practical.

Ownership should prevent customers from being forgotten between employees or shifts.

## 9. Handover Between Employees

When responsibility changes, the next employee should be able to understand the customer context without forcing the customer to repeat everything.

## 10. Customer Profile

The CRM may maintain useful relationship information such as:

- Purchase history
- Product ownership where legitimately captured
- Service history
- Preferences
- Interactions
- Open opportunities
- Customer-requested follow-ups

Sensitive information must follow DEC-0042 controls.

## 11. Customer Consent

CRM communication must respect applicable consent and communication preferences.

The company should provide appropriate ways for customers to control marketing communications.

## 12. Sales Notes

Sales notes should be factual, professional and relevant.

Employees should not record insulting, discriminatory, speculative or unnecessary personal comments.

## 13. Customer Segmentation

CRM may support segments based on legitimate business needs, such as:

- Product interest
- Customer lifecycle stage
- Corporate vs consumer
- Service needs
- Purchase history
- Communication preferences

Segmentation should not create unfair or inappropriate treatment.

## 14. Product Recommendation

CRM should support recommendations based on customer needs and relevant product information.

Recommendations should not be manipulated simply to maximize commission or margin.

## 15. Quotes and Proposals

Where appropriate, formal quotes should contain:

- Product
- Quantity
- Price
- Applicable terms
- Validity period
- Financing information where applicable
- Contact information

## 16. Discount Authority

Discounts should follow the authority framework established by finance and management.

CRM should record meaningful discount exceptions where practical.

## 17. Sales Incentives

Sales incentives should reward healthy business outcomes rather than encourage harmful behavior.

Potential measures include:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Customer satisfaction
- Repeat business
- Service quality
- Attachments

## 18. Avoiding Incentive Abuse

Commission structures should not encourage:

- Mis-selling
- Fake transactions
- Unnecessary discounting
- Manipulated returns
- Customer pressure
- Misrepresentation

## 19. Corporate CRM

Corporate customers should have account-level records covering, where appropriate:

- Organization
- Contacts
- Opportunities
- Devices/purchases
- Quotes
- Service requirements
- Payment arrangements
- Relationship owner

## 20. Account Management

Strategic customers should have clear ownership and review cadence.

Account management should focus on long-term value rather than one-off transactions.

## 21. Repeat Purchase

CRM should support legitimate lifecycle opportunities such as:

- Device upgrades
- Accessory needs
- Warranty/service needs
- New product launches
- Business expansion

Communications should remain relevant.

## 22. Customer Lifecycle

The CRM lifecycle should support:

**Prospect → Customer → Active Customer → Loyal Customer → Advocate**

Customers may move between states over time.

## 23. Customer Reactivation

Inactive customers may be re-engaged where appropriate with useful information or offers consistent with their communication preferences.

## 24. Referral Management

Referrals should be attributable where practical while protecting customer privacy.

Referral programs must follow separately approved rules.

## 25. Lost Opportunity Analysis

Lost sales should be analyzed for patterns such as:

- Price
- Availability
- Product fit
- Competitor choice
- Financing
- Timing
- Service concerns

The goal is learning, not blaming employees.

## 26. Sales Forecasting

CRM opportunity data may support forecasting.

Forecasts should distinguish between:

- Pipeline
- High-confidence opportunities
- Expected revenue
- Actual sales

Pipeline value should not be treated as guaranteed revenue.

## 27. Sales Dashboard

Management may eventually monitor:

- Leads
- Opportunities
- Conversion
- Sales value
- Gross margin
- Average transaction value
- Pipeline
- Follow-up completion
- Repeat customers
- Lost opportunities

## 28. Store-Level CRM

CRM should provide useful visibility to store teams without overwhelming frontline employees with unnecessary data entry.

## 29. Data Quality

CRM value depends on accurate data.

The system should identify:

- Duplicate customers
- Missing critical fields
- Stale opportunities
- Incorrect statuses
- Unassigned leads

## 30. Minimal Data Entry

CRM workflows should be designed so that employees can maintain useful records without spending excessive time typing.

Automation should be used where reliable.

## 31. AI Sales Assistant

Future Internal AI may help employees:

- Summarize customer history
- Suggest relevant products
- Draft follow-ups
- Identify overdue follow-ups
- Prepare meeting summaries
- Surface relevant policies

AI recommendations must remain advisory unless explicitly authorized otherwise.

## 32. AI Customer Handover

When an AI Concierge interaction becomes a human sales interaction, relevant context may be passed to the employee where the customer has authorized or reasonably expects that continuity.

## 33. AI Guardrails

AI must not invent:

- Prices
- Discounts
- Availability
- Warranty terms
- Financing approval
- Product specifications
- Company policies

Controlled source data should be used for customer-facing claims.

## 34. Communication Templates

Approved templates may be maintained for common interactions such as:

- Quote follow-up
- Product availability
- Appointment confirmation
- Service update
- Post-purchase check-in
- Event invitation

Templates should still allow appropriate human personalization.

## 35. Omnichannel CRM

Where practical, customer interactions across store and digital channels should be connected to a common relationship record.

## 36. CRM and Customer Portal

The Customer Portal should eventually allow customers to view appropriate information such as:

- Orders
- Invoices
- Warranty
- Service cases
- Appointments
- Account preferences

## 37. CRM and Experience Passport

The future Experience Passport should provide continuity across the customer lifecycle while respecting customer control and privacy.

## 38. Privacy and Access

CRM access should be role-based.

Employees should only see customer information required for their role.

## 39. Retention and Deletion

Customer data should not be retained forever without purpose.

Retention and deletion rules should be established according to business requirements and applicable obligations.

## 40. CRM Security

CRM systems should follow the security architecture in DEC-0042, including appropriate authentication, access control, auditability and incident response.

## 41. Customer Communication Frequency

CRM should prevent excessive outreach.

Communication frequency should reflect customer needs, preferences and the nature of the interaction.

## 42. Service-to-Sales Boundary

Service teams should identify legitimate customer needs but must not turn support interactions into aggressive sales opportunities.

Trust comes first.

## 43. Marketing-to-Sales Handover

Marketing-generated leads should move into sales workflows with enough context to be useful while avoiding unnecessary data transfer.

## 44. Sales-to-Operations Handover

Important customer commitments must be communicated to operations before the customer is promised a service that requires operational capacity.

## 45. Customer Promise Management

CRM should eventually track important promises such as:

- Callback
- Quote
- Product reservation
- Setup appointment
- Service update

An untracked promise is a customer-experience risk.

## 46. Customer Recovery

CRM should record significant service-recovery actions so future interactions have appropriate context.

## 47. Relationship Reviews

Strategic corporate accounts should receive periodic relationship reviews covering:

- Satisfaction
- Open issues
- Upcoming needs
- Service quality
- Commercial opportunities

## 48. CRM KPIs

Potential KPIs include:

- Lead response time
- Lead-to-opportunity conversion
- Opportunity-to-sale conversion
- Sales cycle length
- Follow-up completion
- Repeat purchase rate
- Customer retention
- Referral rate
- CRM data completeness
- Customer satisfaction

## 49. Risks

Major CRM risks include:

- Customer data leakage
- Excessive outreach
- Poor data quality
- Forgotten follow-ups
- Mis-selling
- Incentive abuse
- AI misinformation
- Duplicate customer records
- Weak access controls
- Broken omnichannel continuity

## Implementation Sequence

1. Define the standard sales journey.
2. Establish lead and customer records.
3. Implement opportunity stages and ownership.
4. Establish follow-up and handover discipline.
5. Connect CRM with store and marketing workflows.
6. Establish customer communication and consent rules.
7. Build sales and management dashboards.
8. Add corporate account management capabilities.
9. Introduce controlled AI sales assistance.
10. Connect CRM with Customer Portal and Experience Passport.
11. Continuously improve using sales and customer evidence.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **sales and CRM framework**. Specific CRM software, commission plans, loyalty economics, data-retention periods and customer communication programs require separate implementation decisions where appropriate.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document customer impact, commercial rationale, data implications, operational risk and related decision IDs.
