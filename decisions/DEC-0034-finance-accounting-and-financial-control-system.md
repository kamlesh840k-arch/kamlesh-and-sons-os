# DEC-0034 — Finance, Accounting & Financial Control System

**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Finance, Accounting and Financial Control System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will operate with a **disciplined, transparent, auditable financial system** from the beginning rather than treating finance as an administrative function added after growth.

The system must provide reliable visibility into cash, profitability, inventory investment, liabilities, taxes, financing, and business performance while supporting responsible expansion.

> **Know the numbers. Protect the cash. Understand the economics. Grow responsibly.**

## 1. Finance Philosophy

Financial decisions should support the long-term strategy of building a premium technology house and national retail brand.

The company should prioritize:

- Cash discipline
- Accurate records
- Sustainable margins
- Controlled working capital
- Transparent reporting
- Tax and legal compliance
- Informed investment
- Long-term value creation

Revenue growth without healthy economics should not be treated as success.

## 2. Accounting Authority

Financial records should be maintained through an appropriate accounting system and under qualified professional oversight where required.

The repository may document financial policies, models, assumptions, and decisions, but it should not replace formal accounting records or statutory documentation.

## 3. Chart of Accounts

KAMLESH & SONS should establish a structured chart of accounts appropriate for a technology retail business.

It should eventually support analysis by:

- Revenue
- Cost of goods sold
- Gross profit
- Operating expenses
- Finance costs
- Taxes
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Equity
- Cash flows

The final chart of accounts should be designed with accounting professionals and the company's systems in mind.

## 4. Revenue Recognition and Sales Records

Commercial transactions should connect financial records with operational records where appropriate:

**Customer → Sale → Product/SKU → Invoice → Payment → Inventory movement**

Revenue reporting should distinguish between gross sales, discounts, returns, taxes, and other relevant components.

## 5. Cost of Goods Sold

The company must maintain a reliable method for determining product cost and gross margin.

Inventory costing methodology should be selected with professional accounting guidance and applied consistently.

## 6. Gross Margin Discipline

Management should monitor gross margin by meaningful dimensions such as:

- Product category
- Brand
- Store
- Sales channel
- Promotion
- Corporate vs retail sales

Margin analysis should account for discounts, returns, financing effects, and other material commercial factors where appropriate.

## 7. Cash Management

Cash is a critical operating asset.

The company should maintain controls for:

- Cash receipts
- Cash deposits
- Cash balances
- Digital payments
- Bank reconciliation
- Petty cash
- Refunds
- Cash handling access

Store cash should be reconciled according to approved operating procedures.

## 8. Bank and Payment Reconciliation

Bank and payment-provider records should be reconciled regularly against company records.

Differences should be investigated rather than carried indefinitely as unexplained balances.

## 9. Accounts Payable

Supplier obligations should be recorded and monitored.

Controls should support:

- Purchase authorization
- Invoice verification
- Goods received verification
- Payment approval
- Due-date tracking
- Supplier reconciliation

Material purchases should not rely solely on informal verbal authorization.

## 10. Accounts Receivable

Where credit or corporate sales are offered, receivables should be controlled through defined:

- Credit terms
- Approval authority
- Customer limits
- Invoice records
- Due dates
- Collection procedures
- Escalation

Credit should not be extended simply to increase reported sales.

## 11. Working Capital

Technology retail can require significant working capital because inventory is purchased before it is sold.

Management should monitor:

- Inventory value
- Inventory turnover
- Days inventory
- Supplier payment terms
- Receivables
- Payables
- Cash conversion cycle

Working-capital decisions should be integrated with DEC-0030 inventory management.

## 12. Budgeting

The company should maintain annual and periodic budgets covering, as appropriate:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Payroll
- Rent
- Marketing
- Technology
- Utilities
- Logistics
- Financing costs
- Capital expenditure
- Working capital

Budgets should be treated as decision tools, not immutable predictions.

## 13. Forecasting

Management should update forecasts as new information becomes available.

Forecasts should clearly distinguish:

- Actuals
- Budget
- Forecast
- Assumptions

Major deviations should trigger investigation and, where appropriate, a documented management decision.

## 14. Cash Flow Planning

Profit and cash are not the same.

The company should maintain forward-looking cash-flow visibility sufficient to anticipate:

- Supplier payments
- Payroll
- Taxes
- Rent
- Financing obligations
- Inventory purchases
- Capital expenditure
- Unexpected operating needs

Liquidity should be protected before discretionary expansion spending.

## 15. Capital Expenditure

Major investments such as store construction, fixtures, technology infrastructure, vehicles, or equipment should have a defined approval process.

Capital proposals should consider:

- Initial cost
- Expected useful life
- Operating impact
- Maintenance
- Expected return
- Strategic value
- Cash-flow impact

## 16. Financing and Debt

Debt and financing should be evaluated based on:

- Cost
- Tenure
- Security requirements
- Cash-flow impact
- Repayment capacity
- Strategic purpose
- Downside risk

The company should not use debt simply to conceal weak unit economics.

Specific borrowing facilities require separate financial analysis and approval.

## 17. EMI Commercial Reconciliation

As established in DEC-0031, customer financing may be a major sales channel.

Finance operations should reconcile financing transactions between:

**Customer sale → Financing provider → Settlement → Accounting record → Inventory record**

Fees, settlement timing, cancellations, and exceptions should be tracked accurately.

## 18. Inventory as a Financial Asset

Inventory should be monitored both operationally and financially.

Management should identify:

- Fast-moving stock
- Slow-moving stock
- Obsolete stock
- High-value stock
- Promotional exposure
- Stock requiring markdown consideration

Inventory decisions should balance availability against capital efficiency.

## 19. Expense Management

Operating expenses should have:

- Defined categories
- Budget ownership
- Appropriate approval
- Supporting documentation
- Periodic review

Expense control should not undermine customer experience or critical infrastructure.

## 20. Procurement Controls

Purchasing should have appropriate separation between request, approval, receipt, and payment where practical.

Higher-value or unusual purchases should receive additional review.

Supplier selection should consider total commercial value rather than price alone.

## 21. Fraud Prevention

Financial controls should address risks such as:

- Fake invoices
- Unauthorized payments
- Duplicate payments
- Refund abuse
- Cash theft
- Inventory theft
- Vendor collusion
- Unauthorized discounts
- Manipulated sales records
- Payment confirmation fraud

High-risk transactions should have appropriate verification and audit trails.

## 22. Financial Reporting

Management reporting should eventually provide a consistent financial dashboard including, where appropriate:

- Revenue
- Gross profit
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- EBITDA or other appropriate operating measures
- Net profit
- Cash balance
- Working capital
- Inventory
- Receivables
- Payables
- Debt
- Capital expenditure

The final reporting package should be designed with accounting and management needs in mind.

## 23. Store-Level Economics

As the company expands, management should understand the economics of each location.

Potential store-level metrics include:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Payroll
- Rent
- Utilities
- Marketing
- Store operating profit
- Inventory investment
- Sales per square foot where meaningful
- Sales per employee

Store comparisons should account for differences in maturity, location, size, and market conditions.

## 24. Unit Economics

Before expansion, the company should understand the economics of a typical store.

A future unit-economic model should evaluate:

**Revenue → Gross Profit → Store Operating Costs → Store Contribution → Investment → Payback**

Expansion should be based on validated economics rather than optimism alone.

## 25. Taxes and Compliance

KAMLESH & SONS must maintain appropriate tax and statutory compliance according to applicable Nepalese requirements.

Specific tax treatments, filing requirements, VAT rules, payroll obligations, and statutory procedures should be confirmed with qualified local professionals and documented separately.

## 26. Financial Data Security

Financial records should have controlled access.

Sensitive financial information should not be exposed to employees or AI systems without legitimate authorization.

Internal AI may assist with financial analysis only within approved access boundaries and should not be treated as the authoritative accounting ledger.

## 27. Finance + AI

Future Internal AI may assist management with:

- Financial summaries
- Variance explanations
- Cash-flow analysis
- Inventory economics
- Forecasting support
- Management reporting

AI-generated analysis should remain traceable to authoritative financial data and should not silently alter accounting records.

## 28. Decision Support

Financial models should support major decisions such as:

- Opening a store
- Expanding to a city
- Hiring teams
- Adding product categories
- Launching services
- Taking debt
- Investing in technology
- Marketing expenditure

Every major financial assumption should have an identifiable source or documented rationale.

## 29. Financial Decision Records

Major financial commitments should be documented with:

- Decision ID
- Business objective
- Amount
- Expected benefit
- Key assumptions
- Risks
- Cash-flow impact
- Approval authority
- Review date
- Outcome where measurable

This supports the company's principle that important decisions should not exist only in chat.

## 30. Financial Review Cadence

The company should eventually establish:

- Daily operational cash/payment checks where required
- Weekly commercial review
- Monthly management accounts
- Quarterly strategic financial review
- Annual budgeting and planning

The exact cadence should be adapted to business scale.

## 31. Risks

Major financial risks include:

- Weak cash control
- Excess inventory
- Margin erosion
- Uncontrolled debt
- Poor receivables collection
- Unreconciled payments
- Tax non-compliance
- Fraud
- Overexpansion
- Inaccurate forecasts
- Hidden liabilities

Financial governance should be designed to identify problems early rather than explain them after damage occurs.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Select appropriate accounting architecture.
2. Define chart of accounts.
3. Establish payment and bank controls.
4. Connect sales, inventory, and accounting records.
5. Establish budgeting and forecasting.
6. Build cash-flow reporting.
7. Define procurement and approval controls.
8. Establish management reporting.
9. Build store-level economics.
10. Develop expansion financial models.
11. Introduce financial decision reviews.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **finance, accounting and financial control direction**. It does not select accounting software, banks, auditors, tax advisors, debt facilities, or final budgets.

Those decisions require financial research, professional advice, commercial analysis, and separate approval.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Financial controls and reporting should evolve as the company moves from a single flagship store to a multi-store national operation.

Material changes should document the rationale, financial impact, affected controls, and related repository documents.
