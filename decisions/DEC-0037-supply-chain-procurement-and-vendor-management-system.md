# DEC-0037 — Supply Chain, Procurement & Vendor Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0037  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Supply Chain, Procurement and Vendor Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build a **controlled, transparent and resilient supply chain** that prioritizes product authenticity, availability, commercial sustainability, supplier reliability and customer trust.

Procurement will not be treated as simply finding the lowest purchase price. The company will evaluate the complete commercial and operational value of a supplier relationship.

> **Buy responsibly. Verify authenticity. Protect availability. Build reliable partnerships.**

## 1. Procurement Philosophy

Procurement decisions should balance:

- Authenticity
- Quality
- Availability
- Commercial terms
- Warranty support
- Supplier reliability
- Payment terms
- Logistics
- Risk
- Long-term strategic value

Lowest price alone should not determine supplier selection.

## 2. Supplier Categories

The company may eventually manage suppliers across categories such as:

- Smartphones
- Laptops
- Tablets
- Accessories
- Wearables
- Smart-home products
- Store equipment
- Technology infrastructure
- Packaging
- Logistics
- Professional services

Each category may require different controls.

## 3. Supplier Onboarding

Material suppliers should go through an appropriate onboarding process covering, where relevant:

- Legal identity
- Contact details
- Product categories
- Source of goods
- Brand authorization where applicable
- Commercial terms
- Payment terms
- Warranty arrangements
- Return conditions
- Delivery capability
- References/history where appropriate
- Compliance documentation

Supplier approval should have a defined owner.

## 4. Supplier Verification

Before significant purchases, the company should verify that the supplier and products meet applicable requirements.

Verification may include:

- Business registration information
- Tax documentation where applicable
- Brand authorization evidence
- Purchase documentation
- Product serial/IMEI information
- Warranty documentation
- References
- Physical/operational verification where justified

The level of verification should match supplier and transaction risk.

## 5. Authorized Distribution Strategy

KAMLESH & SONS' long-term objective includes becoming an authorized premium retailer for multiple global brands.

Supplier strategy should therefore progressively prioritize relationships that can support:

- Authentic products
- Official warranty
- Reliable supply
- Brand standards
- Training
- Launch support
- Marketing support
- Long-term partnership

Specific brand authorizations require separate commercial and legal decisions.

## 6. Purchase Orders

Material procurement should use controlled purchase orders or equivalent documented authorization.

A purchase record should identify, as appropriate:

- Supplier
- Product/SKU
- Quantity
- Unit cost
- Taxes/charges
- Delivery terms
- Expected delivery
- Payment terms
- Approver

Informal purchasing should be minimized for material transactions.

## 7. Three-Way Verification

Where practical, material purchases should use a control linking:

**Purchase Order → Goods Received → Supplier Invoice**

Differences should be investigated before payment.

The exact control process may vary by supplier and system capability.

## 8. Inventory and Procurement Integration

Procurement should be connected to the inventory system established under DEC-0030.

The goal is to maintain visibility into:

- On-order stock
- In-transit stock
- Received stock
- Available stock
- Reserved stock
- Returned stock

This reduces duplicate ordering and improves availability planning.

## 9. Demand Planning

Future procurement should increasingly use demand signals such as:

- Historical sales
- Current inventory
- Sales velocity
- Seasonality
- Product launches
- Promotions
- Supplier lead time
- Customer orders

Forecasts should be treated as estimates and continuously improved.

## 10. Reorder Discipline

The company should establish category-appropriate reorder rules.

Potential inputs include:

- Reorder point
- Safety stock
- Lead time
- Minimum order quantity
- Sales velocity
- Cash availability
- Supplier reliability

High-value technology products require tighter working-capital control than low-value fast-moving accessories.

## 11. Supplier Lead Times

Supplier lead times should be measured and documented.

Where supplier performance repeatedly differs from stated lead times, procurement assumptions should be adjusted.

## 12. Payment Terms

Supplier payment terms should be evaluated as part of total economics.

Important considerations include:

- Advance payment
- Credit period
- Deposits
- Settlement timing
- Currency exposure where applicable
- Financing cost
- Early-payment discounts

Longer payment terms can improve working capital but should not justify poor supplier quality or excessive pricing.

## 13. Supplier Pricing

Purchase-price evaluation should consider total landed economics, including relevant:

- Product cost
- Taxes
- Freight
- Insurance
- Handling
- Financing cost
- Returns
- Warranty exposure
- Other material charges

The cheapest quoted unit price may not be the cheapest total cost.

## 14. Supplier Performance

Material suppliers should eventually be evaluated using a scorecard that may include:

- On-time delivery
- Fill rate
- Product authenticity
- Product quality
- Pricing competitiveness
- Warranty support
- Return handling
- Responsiveness
- Documentation quality
- Payment accuracy

Supplier performance should influence future purchasing decisions.

## 15. Supplier Relationship Management

Important suppliers should have defined relationship owners.

Regular reviews may cover:

- Forecast
- Orders
- Availability
- Pricing
- Product launches
- Warranty issues
- Marketing opportunities
- Operational problems

Strategic suppliers should be developed as partners rather than treated only as transactional vendors.

## 16. Vendor Risk Management

Material supplier risks may include:

- Counterfeit products
- Supply interruption
- Financial instability
- Poor quality
- Warranty failure
- Concentration risk
- Fraud
- Compliance issues
- Sudden price changes
- Logistics disruption

Critical suppliers should have appropriate mitigation plans.

## 17. Supplier Concentration

The company should monitor excessive dependence on a single supplier where alternatives are reasonably available.

However, concentration may be strategically justified for authorized brand relationships.

The trade-off between resilience and strategic exclusivity should be documented.

## 18. Product Authenticity Controls

Technology products should have appropriate authenticity controls, particularly for high-value or high-risk categories.

Controls may include:

- Serial/IMEI verification
- Supplier documentation
- Packaging checks
- Warranty verification
- Receiving inspection
- Exception reporting

Suspected counterfeit or unauthorized goods should be quarantined and escalated.

## 19. Receiving Process

Goods receiving should verify, as appropriate:

- Quantity
- SKU/product
- Serial/IMEI
- Physical condition
- Packaging
- Documentation
- Purchase-order match

Receiving discrepancies should be documented before stock is made available for sale where appropriate.

## 20. Returns to Supplier

Supplier returns should have controlled records covering:

- Product
- Serial/IMEI where applicable
- Reason
- Supplier reference
- Date
- Expected resolution
- Financial impact

Open supplier-return cases should remain visible until resolved.

## 21. Warranty and Supplier Support

Procurement decisions should account for after-sales support.

Supplier evaluation should consider:

- Warranty process
- Turnaround time
- Replacement policy
- Parts/service availability
- Escalation path

A product with a strong purchase margin but poor customer support can damage the brand.

## 22. Logistics

The supply chain should eventually define reliable processes for:

**Supplier → Transport → Receiving → Inventory → Store/Customer**

Logistics partners should be evaluated for reliability, security, cost, tracking and service quality.

## 23. High-Value Shipment Controls

High-value technology shipments may require additional controls such as:

- Secure packaging
- Tracking
- Proof of delivery
- Serial/IMEI reconciliation
- Receiving authorization
- Exception escalation

Controls should be proportional to risk and shipment value.

## 24. Procurement Approvals

The company should establish approval thresholds for purchases.

Approval requirements should consider:

- Purchase value
- Supplier risk
- Product risk
- Strategic importance
- Cash-flow impact

Specific monetary limits should be defined separately after financial and operational analysis.

## 25. Conflict of Interest

Employees involved in procurement should disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

Supplier selection should be based on legitimate business criteria.

Personal benefits, undisclosed commissions, or inappropriate gifts should not influence procurement decisions.

## 26. Supplier Gifts and Hospitality

The company should establish clear rules for supplier gifts, hospitality and incentives.

Material or inappropriate benefits should not influence purchasing decisions.

Detailed thresholds should be defined in future policy with legal and cultural considerations.

## 27. Procurement Data

Procurement records should eventually support analysis of:

- Supplier spend
- Product spend
- Purchase price trends
- Supplier performance
- Payment terms
- Lead times
- Returns
- Warranty issues

Data should connect to the financial and inventory systems where practical.

## 28. Procurement + Finance

Procurement should integrate with DEC-0034 through controlled processes for:

**Purchase → Receipt → Invoice → Approval → Payment → Reconciliation**

This reduces duplicate payments and improves financial visibility.

## 29. Procurement + Sales

Sales teams should provide demand feedback to procurement, but customer promises should not be made without reasonable supply confirmation.

Sales forecasts should inform purchasing without automatically becoming purchase commitments.

## 30. Procurement + Marketing

Major campaigns and launches should coordinate with procurement to confirm sufficient supply.

Marketing should not create large demand for products that procurement cannot reasonably support.

## 31. Procurement + Technology

Procurement workflows should progressively move from manual processes toward controlled digital workflows.

Potential capabilities include:

- Supplier records
- Purchase orders
- Approval workflows
- Delivery tracking
- Receiving
- Invoice matching
- Supplier scorecards
- Alerts

Automation should improve control rather than simply increase complexity.

## 32. Sustainability and Responsible Sourcing

As the company grows, supplier evaluation may incorporate responsible sourcing considerations where relevant.

These may include:

- Ethical business practices
- Environmental considerations
- Packaging efficiency
- Responsible disposal/recycling
- Labor practices in relevant supply chains

The company should make evidence-based commitments rather than unsupported sustainability claims.

## 33. Supplier Contracts

Material supplier relationships should have appropriate written agreements where practical.

Relevant terms may include:

- Price
- Payment
- Delivery
- Warranty
- Returns
- Authenticity
- Territory
- Exclusivity where applicable
- Confidentiality
- Data handling
- Termination
- Dispute resolution

Legal review should be obtained for material agreements.

## 34. Procurement Documentation

Important procurement decisions should be traceable through:

- Supplier record
- Quote/comparison
- Purchase order
- Approval
- Receiving record
- Invoice
- Payment
- Performance review

The objective is a complete audit trail without unnecessary administrative burden.

## 35. Risks

Major supply-chain risks include:

- Counterfeit products
- Supplier failure
- Stockouts
- Excess inventory
- Price volatility
- Logistics disruption
- Concentration risk
- Fraud
- Poor warranty support
- Unauthorized sourcing
- Cash-flow pressure

Risk mitigation should be proportional to business impact.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Define supplier master-data standards.
2. Establish supplier onboarding and verification.
3. Create purchase-order controls.
4. Connect procurement with inventory.
5. Establish receiving and reconciliation.
6. Define supplier scorecards.
7. Establish approval thresholds.
8. Build demand/reorder planning.
9. Develop strategic supplier relationships.
10. Integrate procurement with finance.
11. Establish supplier-risk monitoring.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **supply chain, procurement and vendor management direction**. It does not select specific distributors, logistics companies, suppliers, brand partners, payment terms, or procurement software.

Those decisions require supplier research, commercial negotiation, legal review, and financial analysis.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Procurement practices should evolve as KAMLESH & SONS grows from one flagship store to a regional and national retail network.

Material changes should document the rationale, commercial impact, supplier implications, risks, and related decision IDs.
