# DEC-0073 — Procurement Strategy & Supplier Performance Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0073  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will manage strategic procurement and supplier performance through structured sourcing, evaluation, negotiation and review processes that optimize total value rather than purchase price alone.

> **Buy intelligently, build reliable supplier relationships, and measure the total cost of supply.**

## Core Controls

- Category strategy
- Supplier segmentation
- Sourcing events
- Supplier due diligence
- Commercial negotiation
- Total-cost analysis
- Contract alignment
- Supplier scorecards
- Corrective actions
- Supplier risk management

## Supplier Segmentation

Suppliers should be categorized by spend, criticality, switching difficulty, quality risk and strategic importance.

## Selection

Material suppliers should be evaluated using appropriate criteria including price, quality, reliability, capacity, compliance, lead time and financial or operational risk.

## Performance

Supplier scorecards should track relevant measures such as fill rate, on-time delivery, defects, responsiveness, price performance and claims.

## Negotiation

Commercial negotiations should consider payment terms, lead times, warranties, returns, quality, service levels and total cost.

## Risk

Material supplier concentration and dependency risks should connect to DEC-0051 and continuity planning under DEC-0071.

## Improvement

Recurring supplier failures should result in documented corrective action, escalation or sourcing alternatives.

## Success Standard

K&S knows which suppliers are strategically important, how they perform, what risks they create and where better sourcing decisions can improve economics or resilience.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Segment suppliers by strategic importance and risk.
2. Establish sourcing and evaluation criteria.
3. Implement supplier scorecards.
4. Connect supplier performance to quality and inventory.
5. Establish corrective-action and escalation processes.
6. Review category strategies periodically.
