# DEC-0053 — Physical Stores, Facilities, Security & Asset Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0053  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Physical Stores, Facilities, Security and Asset Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0042, DEC-0045, DEC-0050, DEC-0051, DEC-0052

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will manage physical stores, facilities, equipment, security and company assets as a unified operating system designed to protect people, products, property and customer experience.

Stores should be safe, reliable, presentable, operationally efficient and ready to support premium customer service.

> **The store is the physical interface of the brand: protect it, maintain it, and make it work beautifully.**

## 1. Store Operating Standard

Every store should have clear standards for:

- Safety
- Cleanliness
- Security
- Merchandising
- Equipment
- Connectivity
- Customer accessibility
- Staff readiness
- Opening and closing

## 2. Site Selection

Potential sites should be evaluated for:

- Customer access
- Visibility
- Foot traffic
- Catchment
- Parking/transport
- Security
- Utilities
- Rent and occupancy economics
- Expansion potential
- Brand fit

## 3. Store Approval

A new store should receive appropriate commercial, operational, legal, security and financial approval before commitment.

## 4. Lease Management

Material leases should have a designated owner and controlled records covering:

- Term
- Rent
- Escalation
- Deposit
- Renewal
- Termination
- Maintenance responsibilities
- Restrictions

## 5. Facilities Register

Each location should maintain a facilities record covering key:

- Building information
- Equipment
- Utilities
- Maintenance contracts
- Security systems
- Insurance requirements

## 6. Asset Register

Company assets should be recorded according to risk and value.

Examples include:

- Computers
- POS equipment
- Displays
- Cameras
- Networking equipment
- Furniture
- Tools
- Service equipment
- Vehicles where applicable

## 7. Asset Identification

Material assets should have identifiable records and, where practical, asset tags or serial numbers.

## 8. Asset Ownership

Every material asset should have a responsible location, function or owner.

## 9. Asset Lifecycle

Assets should be managed through:

**Acquire → Deploy → Maintain → Monitor → Repair → Replace → Retire**

## 10. Store Opening Checklist

Opening procedures should verify:

- Security status
- Power
- Connectivity
- POS readiness
- Cash controls where applicable
- Store cleanliness
- Product/display readiness
- Safety conditions

## 11. Store Closing Checklist

Closing procedures should verify:

- Cash/transaction controls
- Doors and access points
- Security systems
- Equipment shutdown where appropriate
- Sensitive information
- Product security
- Utilities

## 12. Physical Security

Stores should use security controls proportionate to risk.

Potential controls include:

- Access control
- CCTV
- Alarms
- Locks
- Secure storage
- Visitor controls

## 13. CCTV Governance

CCTV should be operated according to applicable law and company privacy/security policies.

Access to recordings should be restricted and auditable.

## 14. Key and Access Management

Physical keys, access cards and credentials should be controlled.

Lost access devices should be reported and disabled or replaced as appropriate.

## 15. Visitor Management

Visitors requiring access to restricted areas should be appropriately controlled.

## 16. High-Value Product Security

High-value products should receive security controls appropriate to their theft risk.

Controls may include:

- Secure display
- Locked storage
- Serial tracking
- Controlled access
- Inventory reconciliation

## 17. Cash and Valuables

Where stores handle cash or other valuables, procedures should establish secure storage, reconciliation and authorized access.

## 18. Loss Prevention

Loss prevention should address:

- Theft
- Shrinkage
- Fraud
- Damage
- Unauthorized removal
- Inventory discrepancies

## 19. Incident Response

Physical incidents should be reported through an appropriate process.

Examples include:

- Theft
- Break-in
- Fire
- Injury
- Threat
- Property damage
- Major equipment failure

## 20. Emergency Procedures

Stores should maintain clear procedures for relevant emergencies, including:

- Fire
- Medical emergency
- Natural disaster
- Security threat
- Power outage
- Major water leak

## 21. Fire Safety

Fire-safety controls should comply with applicable requirements and include appropriate equipment, access and staff awareness.

## 22. First Aid

Appropriate first-aid resources should be available according to location and applicable requirements.

## 23. Evacuation

Staff should know evacuation routes and assembly procedures where applicable.

## 24. Accessibility

Stores should aim to provide safe and practical access for customers and employees with differing mobility needs, subject to site constraints and applicable requirements.

## 25. Store Cleanliness

Cleanliness should be treated as a customer-experience and asset-protection standard, not merely an aesthetic task.

## 26. Maintenance

Facilities and equipment should receive preventive maintenance based on importance and risk.

## 27. Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Maintenance schedules may cover:

- HVAC
- Electrical systems
- POS equipment
- Networking
- CCTV
- Alarms
- Generators/backup power where applicable
- Fixtures

## 28. Maintenance Requests

Employees should have a simple way to report maintenance problems.

Requests should have status and ownership until resolved.

## 29. Critical Equipment

Critical equipment should have defined fallback procedures where failure could stop store operations.

## 30. Power and Connectivity Resilience

Locations with material operational dependency should evaluate:

- Backup power
- Network redundancy
- Offline POS capability
- Mobile connectivity fallback

## 31. Environmental Controls

Product-sensitive environments should maintain appropriate temperature, humidity and storage conditions where required.

## 32. Warehouse / Backroom Standards

Backrooms should support:

- Safe storage
- Accurate inventory
- Easy picking
- Product protection
- Clear walkways
- Controlled access

## 33. Stockroom Organization

Products should have defined storage locations where practical.

Fast-moving and high-value products should receive appropriate placement and controls.

## 34. Display Standards

Displays should be:

- Accurate
- Clean
- Functional
- Secure
- Consistent with brand standards

## 35. Demo Equipment

Demonstration equipment should be tracked and maintained.

Demo devices should not be confused with saleable inventory.

## 36. Store Technology

Store technology should align with DEC-0050 and support:

- POS
- Inventory
- CRM
- Connectivity
- Security
- Customer experience

## 37. Network Security

Store networks should use appropriate segmentation, authentication and security controls.

Customer Wi-Fi, if offered, should be appropriately separated from internal systems.

## 38. Device Management

Company-owned devices should have appropriate:

- Asset records
- Access controls
- Security updates
- Configuration standards
- Replacement procedures

## 39. Software and License Control

Company devices should use appropriately licensed and approved software.

## 40. Physical Document Security

Sensitive printed documents should not be left exposed in customer-accessible areas.

Secure disposal should be used for confidential records.

## 41. Waste and Disposal

Store disposal should account for:

- Customer data
- Company data
- Electronic waste
- Damaged products
- Packaging

## 42. E-Waste

Electronic equipment should be disposed of through appropriate channels where possible.

Data-bearing devices should be securely wiped or destroyed according to policy.

## 43. Vendor Access to Stores

External technicians and contractors should receive only the physical/system access necessary for their work.

## 44. Contractor Safety

Contractors working on company premises should follow applicable safety and access requirements.

## 45. Facilities Vendors

Important facilities vendors should be evaluated for:

- Reliability
- Safety
- Responsiveness
- Cost
- Insurance/licensing where relevant
- Service quality

## 46. Store Security Reviews

Locations should periodically review physical-security risks and incidents.

High-risk stores should receive stronger review frequency.

## 47. Shrinkage Analysis

Management should analyze inventory shrinkage by:

- Location
- Category
- Product
- Cause where known
- Time period

The goal is prevention, not arbitrary blame.

## 48. Insurance and Property Risk

Facilities and material assets should be reviewed against appropriate insurance requirements under DEC-0051.

## 49. Store Relocation

Store relocation decisions should evaluate:

- Customer impact
- Lease obligations
- Asset movement
- Inventory movement
- Technology migration
- Branding
- Staff impact

## 50. Store Closure

Closure should include controlled handling of:

- Inventory
- Cash
- Assets
- Customer records
- Technology
- Leases
- Signage
- Access credentials
- Vendor contracts

## 51. Asset Disposal

Asset disposal should be authorized and recorded.

High-value disposal should receive stronger controls.

## 52. Asset Theft or Loss

Lost or stolen assets should be reported, investigated where appropriate and removed or updated in asset records.

## 53. Capital Expenditure

Material store capital expenditure should follow appropriate approval and budget controls.

## 54. Store Renovation

Renovation projects should coordinate:

- Design
- Budget
- Safety
- Technology
- Branding
- Operations
- Customer disruption

## 55. Store Experience

Physical facilities should support the brand positioning established by marketing and customer-experience decisions.

The store should feel trustworthy, organized and easy to navigate.

## 56. Customer Safety

Store layouts and operations should minimize foreseeable customer hazards.

Hazards should be corrected or isolated promptly.

## 57. Employee Safety

Employees should receive appropriate training for physical hazards associated with their work.

## 58. Workplace Ergonomics

Workstations should be designed and maintained to reduce avoidable physical strain.

## 59. Security Training

Store employees should understand:

- Access procedures
- Theft response
- Emergency procedures
- Incident reporting
- Data/physical security

## 60. Incident Escalation

Serious incidents should escalate quickly to appropriate management and emergency authorities where required.

Employees should not attempt unsafe intervention.

## 61. Physical Security and Privacy

Security measures should be balanced with customer and employee privacy requirements.

## 62. Facilities Data

Facilities information should be connected to the broader asset and technology records where useful.

## 63. Facilities Dashboard

Management should eventually monitor:

- Open maintenance tickets
- Preventive maintenance completion
- Security incidents
- Shrinkage
- Asset status
- Utility/occupancy costs
- Store uptime
- Facility issues

## 64. Store Readiness Score

A future store-readiness score may evaluate:

- Safety
- Cleanliness
- Technology
- Inventory
- Displays
- Security
- Staff readiness

## 65. Opening New Stores

New locations should use a repeatable opening playbook covering:

- Site readiness
- Fixtures
- Technology
- Security
- Inventory
- Staff
- Training
- Marketing
- Compliance

## 66. Multi-Store Standardization

As the network grows, core physical standards should be standardized while allowing appropriate local adaptation.

## 67. Local Risk Assessment

Each location should consider local conditions such as:

- Crime risk
- Weather
- Natural hazards
- Infrastructure reliability
- Customer traffic
- Nearby competitors

## 68. Business Continuity

Store continuity plans should identify minimum viable operations during disruptions.

## 69. Technology Failure Fallback

Stores should have practical procedures for temporary POS, network or system failures consistent with DEC-0050.

## 70. Physical Security Technology

Security technology should be selected based on risk and total operating cost rather than technology for its own sake.

## 71. Automation and AI

AI may assist with:

- Maintenance prioritization
- Security anomaly analysis
- Store readiness analysis
- Asset lifecycle planning
- Energy optimization

High-impact physical security actions should retain appropriate human oversight.

## 72. Risks

Major physical and facilities risks include:

- Theft
- Fire
- Injury
- Equipment failure
- Power outage
- Connectivity outage
- Water damage
- Asset loss
- Poor maintenance
- Unauthorized access
- Security/privacy failures

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish store opening/closing standards.
2. Build facilities and asset registers.
3. Standardize physical security and access controls.
4. Establish maintenance request and preventive-maintenance processes.
5. Implement inventory-loss and incident controls.
6. Establish emergency and business-continuity procedures.
7. Connect store technology and asset records to K&S OS.
8. Build store-readiness and facilities dashboards.
9. Standardize new-store opening and closure playbooks.
10. Introduce controlled automation and predictive maintenance as scale justifies.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **physical stores, facilities, security and asset-management framework**. Specific building standards, security vendors, equipment specifications, leases and site-level procedures should be approved through appropriate implementation processes.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document safety, security, financial, operational and customer-experience implications.
