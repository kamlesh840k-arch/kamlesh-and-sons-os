# DEC-0056 — People, HR, Culture, Training & Workforce Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0056  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS People, HR, Culture, Training and Workforce Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0045, DEC-0051, DEC-0053, DEC-0054, DEC-0055

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat people, culture, capability and workforce planning as operating infrastructure. The company will build a fair, accountable and high-performance environment in which employees understand their responsibilities, receive appropriate training and can grow with the business.

> **Build capable people, clear accountability and a culture worthy of the customer trust we seek.**

## 1. People Philosophy

The company will seek to:

- Hire for capability and character
- Set clear expectations
- Train people for their roles
- Reward responsible performance
- Develop future leaders
- Treat people fairly and respectfully
- Protect employee safety and dignity

## 2. Workforce Planning

Workforce plans should reflect:

- Store footprint
- Customer demand
- Service requirements
- Operating hours
- Seasonal peaks
- Growth plans
- Required capabilities

## 3. Organization Design

Roles should have clear accountability, decision rights and reporting relationships.

The organization should avoid unnecessary complexity while the company is small and add structure as scale requires it.

## 4. Role Architecture

Important roles should have documented:

- Purpose
- Responsibilities
- Decision authority
- Required skills
- Performance expectations
- Key relationships

## 5. Job Descriptions

Material roles should have current job descriptions and be reviewed when responsibilities materially change.

## 6. Hiring Principles

Hiring should evaluate both technical capability and alignment with company values.

Recruitment decisions should be based on job-related criteria and applicable law.

## 7. Recruitment Process

A controlled recruitment process should generally include:

**Requirement → Sourcing → Screening → Interview → Assessment → Decision → Offer → Onboarding**

## 8. Candidate Evaluation

Candidates should be evaluated consistently against defined role requirements where practical.

## 9. Background Checks

Where appropriate and legally permitted, background verification may be performed for roles where the risk justifies it.

## 10. Offer Management

Employment offers should clearly document material terms and receive appropriate authorization.

## 11. Employee Records

Employee records should be maintained securely with access limited to authorized personnel.

## 12. Employee Privacy

Employee personal information should be handled according to applicable privacy requirements and DEC-0055.

## 13. Onboarding

New employees should receive an onboarding program covering:

- Role expectations
- Company values
- Policies
- Security
- Privacy
- Customer standards
- Safety
- Tools and systems

## 14. First-Day Readiness

Before or at the start of employment, the company should provide required access, equipment and role information according to the position.

## 15. Probation / Initial Review

Where applicable, new employees should receive structured early feedback and a clear review before completion of the initial employment period.

## 16. Training Philosophy

Training should combine:

- Product knowledge
- Process knowledge
- Practical skills
- Customer handling
- Systems training
- Compliance
- Safety

## 17. Role-Based Training

Training requirements should differ by role.

Examples include:

- Sales
- Customer service
- Technical service
- Procurement
- Finance
- Store operations
- Management

## 18. Product Training

Customer-facing employees should understand the products they sell well enough to make accurate recommendations.

## 19. Service Training

Service employees should receive appropriate technical and customer-handling training.

## 20. Security Training

Employees should receive security awareness training consistent with DEC-0055.

## 21. Privacy Training

Employees handling customer or employee information should understand privacy and data-handling responsibilities.

## 22. Safety Training

Store and facilities employees should receive training appropriate to physical workplace risks.

## 23. Compliance Training

Employees should receive role-appropriate training on material legal, ethical and company requirements.

## 24. Training Records

Completion of required training should be recorded.

## 25. Knowledge Assessment

Important training may use quizzes, demonstrations or competency checks to confirm understanding.

## 26. Certification

Technical or higher-risk roles may require certification or documented competency before independent work.

## 27. Continuous Learning

Training should not end at onboarding. Product, technology, process and regulatory changes should trigger relevant refreshers.

## 28. Performance Management

Performance management should create clarity rather than fear.

Employees should understand:

- What success means
- How it is measured
- What support is available
- What improvement is required

## 29. Goals

Goals should be specific enough to guide behavior and connected to business priorities.

## 30. Balanced Performance

Performance should consider both results and how those results were achieved.

Short-term sales should not excuse misconduct, customer abuse, fraud or policy violations.

## 31. Key Performance Indicators

Role-specific measures may include:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Customer satisfaction
- Service resolution
- Inventory accuracy
- Productivity
- Quality
- Attendance
- Training completion

## 32. Performance Reviews

Employees should receive periodic performance feedback appropriate to their role and level.

## 33. Continuous Feedback

Managers should not wait for an annual review to address important performance issues.

## 34. Coaching

Managers should use coaching to improve skills, judgment and performance before problems become systemic where practical.

## 35. Performance Improvement

Where performance is materially below expectations, the company should use a documented and fair improvement process consistent with applicable law.

## 36. Recognition

The company should recognize behaviors and achievements that strengthen customer trust, operational quality, teamwork and long-term performance.

## 37. Compensation Philosophy

Compensation should be designed to attract and retain capable people while remaining economically sustainable.

## 38. Pay Governance

Compensation decisions should follow approved authority levels and applicable employment requirements.

## 39. Incentives

Incentive plans should avoid encouraging:

- Mis-selling
- Excessive discounting
- Warranty abuse
- Unsafe behavior
- Fraud
- Customer manipulation

## 40. Sales Incentives

Sales incentives should balance revenue with margin, customer experience, compliance and quality where appropriate.

## 41. Service Incentives

Service incentives should reward quality and resolution rather than simply maximizing case volume.

## 42. Attendance and Scheduling

Work schedules should reflect customer and operational requirements while following applicable employment rules.

## 43. Workforce Scheduling

Store schedules should balance:

- Customer traffic
- Sales demand
- Service demand
- Employee availability
- Labor requirements

## 44. Overtime

Overtime should be managed according to applicable requirements and internal approval processes.

## 45. Leave

Employee leave should be administered consistently with applicable law and company policy.

## 46. Attendance Management

Attendance issues should be handled consistently, fairly and with appropriate documentation.

## 47. Employee Conduct

Employees should follow standards concerning:

- Honesty
- Respect
- Customer treatment
- Confidentiality
- Security
- Conflicts of interest
- Company assets

## 48. Code of Conduct

A practical employee code of conduct should support DEC-0051 and DEC-0055 requirements.

## 49. Anti-Harassment

The company should maintain a workplace free from harassment, intimidation and unlawful discrimination, consistent with applicable requirements.

## 50. Respectful Workplace

Managers and employees should treat one another professionally regardless of role or seniority.

## 51. Speak-Up

Employees should have safe channels to raise concerns about:

- Fraud
- Harassment
- Safety
- Security
- Compliance
- Misconduct

## 52. Non-Retaliation

Good-faith reporting should not result in retaliation.

## 53. Conflict of Interest

Employees should disclose material conflicts that could affect business decisions.

## 54. Gifts and Hospitality

Employee acceptance of gifts or benefits from suppliers or customers should follow company rules and DEC-0051.

## 55. Company Assets

Employees are responsible for appropriate use and protection of company property assigned to them.

## 56. Information Security Responsibility

Employees must follow security controls and promptly report suspected incidents under DEC-0055.

## 57. Customer Experience Responsibility

Every customer-facing employee contributes to the customer experience, not only sales and service teams.

## 58. Manager Standards

Managers are responsible for:

- Clear direction
- Fair treatment
- Coaching
- Performance management
- Team safety
- Escalation
- Policy adherence

## 59. Leadership Development

High-potential employees may receive structured opportunities to develop:

- Decision-making
- People management
- Commercial judgment
- Operational discipline

## 60. Succession Planning

Critical roles should have reasonable succession or contingency plans as the company grows.

## 61. Internal Mobility

Where practical, employees should be able to apply for expanded responsibilities or internal roles based on capability and business need.

## 62. Career Development

Managers should discuss development opportunities with employees and identify practical skill-building actions.

## 63. Training Budget

Training investment should be prioritized toward capabilities with measurable operational or strategic value.

## 64. Learning Management

As scale justifies it, K&S should use a controlled learning-management system for:

- Training assignments
- Completion tracking
- Certifications
- Assessments
- Learning history

## 65. Knowledge Management

Important operating knowledge should be documented in K&S OS rather than remaining dependent on individual employees.

## 66. SOP Ownership

Operational procedures should have owners responsible for keeping them accurate and current.

## 67. Policy Acknowledgment

Employees should acknowledge material policies where appropriate and be able to access the current versions.

## 68. Employee Communications

Important organizational changes should be communicated clearly and through appropriate channels.

## 69. Change Management

Major process or technology changes should include sufficient employee communication, training and transition support.

## 70. Employee Feedback

The company should create practical channels for employees to provide operational feedback and improvement ideas.

## 71. Engagement

Management should monitor employee engagement signals without turning engagement measurement into bureaucracy.

## 72. Employee Experience

The company should periodically evaluate whether employees have the tools, information, training and support required to perform their jobs.

## 73. Workforce Analytics

Management may analyze:

- Headcount
- Turnover
- Hiring time
- Absence
- Training completion
- Productivity
- Performance
- Labor cost

Analytics should respect applicable privacy requirements.

## 74. Turnover Analysis

Material turnover should be investigated for patterns involving:

- Management
- Compensation
- Workload
- Training
- Scheduling
- Career opportunity

## 75. Recruitment Metrics

Potential metrics include:

- Time to hire
- Offer acceptance
- Hiring quality
- New-hire retention
- Cost per hire

## 76. Workforce Cost

Management should understand labor cost relative to revenue, service demand and operational requirements.

## 77. Staffing Efficiency

Efficiency should not be pursued by understaffing to the point that customer service, safety or employee wellbeing deteriorates.

## 78. Temporary / Contract Workers

Temporary or contract workers should receive appropriate onboarding, access controls and role-specific training.

## 79. Contractor Access

External workers should receive only the physical and digital access required for their approved work.

## 80. Offboarding

Employee departure should include appropriate:

- Access removal
- Asset return
- Record closure
- Knowledge transfer
- Final administrative processing

## 81. Exit Feedback

Where practical, exit feedback may be collected to identify systemic workforce issues.

## 82. Final Access Review

Security-sensitive access should be removed promptly when employment ends, consistent with DEC-0055.

## 83. Workforce Continuity

Critical operations should identify backup coverage for key roles.

## 84. Crisis Staffing

Business-continuity planning should identify minimum staffing and role substitution requirements for major disruptions.

## 85. Health and Safety

The company should maintain workplace safety practices appropriate to its locations and activities.

## 86. Incident Reporting

Workplace injuries, safety incidents and significant hazards should be reported and managed promptly.

## 87. Workplace Equipment

Employees should receive appropriate equipment and instruction for their roles.

## 88. Remote / Hybrid Work

Where remote or hybrid work is permitted, the company should define expectations for productivity, security, communication and equipment.

## 89. AI and Workforce Management

AI may assist with:

- Training recommendations
- Workforce forecasting
- Scheduling support
- Knowledge retrieval
- Performance trend analysis

AI should not independently make high-impact employment decisions without appropriate human oversight, review and applicable legal safeguards.

## 90. HR Data Security

HR systems should receive strong access controls because employee information is highly sensitive.

## 91. HR Vendor Management

External HR, payroll, recruitment and learning vendors should be assessed according to the sensitivity of information and access involved.

## 92. Fairness

Employment processes should be designed to reduce arbitrary or discriminatory decision-making.

## 93. Documentation

Material employment decisions should have sufficient documentation to support consistency, accountability and legal compliance.

## 94. Employee Relations

Employee concerns should be handled promptly, respectfully and consistently.

## 95. Grievance Handling

The company should provide a documented route for employees to raise workplace grievances.

## 96. Disciplinary Process

Disciplinary action should be proportionate, documented and consistent with applicable law and company policy.

## 97. Recognition of Long-Term Value

People decisions should consider long-term capability and retention, not only immediate cost.

## 98. Culture Metrics

Potential culture indicators include:

- Voluntary turnover
- Training completion
- Internal promotion
- Employee feedback
- Safety incidents
- Policy violations
- Absenteeism

## 99. People Dashboard

Management should eventually have a workforce dashboard covering:

- Headcount
- Open positions
- Hiring pipeline
- Turnover
- Training
- Labor cost
- Performance
- Safety
- Employee feedback

## 100. Risks

Major people and workforce risks include:

- Poor hiring
- High turnover
- Skill gaps
- Weak management
- Workplace misconduct
- Safety incidents
- Payroll errors
- Unauthorized access after departure
- Employee data exposure
- Misaligned incentives
- Workforce shortages

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish role architecture and workforce ownership.
2. Standardize hiring and onboarding.
3. Define role-based training and required certifications.
4. Implement employee records and access controls.
5. Establish performance, coaching and feedback processes.
6. Define compensation and incentive governance.
7. Establish conduct, speak-up and grievance processes.
8. Build workforce planning and scheduling practices.
9. Introduce learning and workforce analytics as scale justifies.
10. Develop leadership and succession capabilities.
11. Integrate HR, security, training and operational systems.
12. Continuously improve culture, capability and workforce resilience.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **people, HR, culture, training and workforce-management framework**. Specific employment policies, compensation structures, benefits, training programs and local legal requirements should be implemented through approved HR and legal processes.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document workforce, financial, legal, operational and culture implications.
