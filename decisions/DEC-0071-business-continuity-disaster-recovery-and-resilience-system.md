# DEC-0071 — Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery & Resilience System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0071  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will prepare for material operational disruptions by identifying critical processes, dependencies, recovery priorities and practical continuity actions.

> **Stay operational when conditions are not normal.**

## Core Controls

- Critical-process identification
- Business-impact assessment
- Recovery priorities
- Backup and restoration
- Alternate suppliers and channels
- Communications plans
- Incident roles
- Manual fallback procedures
- Disaster recovery testing
- Lessons learned

## Critical Dependencies

Continuity planning should consider stores, technology, payments, inventory, suppliers, customer service, finance, workforce and communications.

## Recovery

Critical systems should have documented recovery objectives proportionate to business impact. Important data should be backed up and restoration should be tested periodically.

## Supplier Resilience

Material single points of failure should be identified, with alternate sources or mitigation plans where justified.

## Crisis Management

Material incidents should use clear command, escalation and communication structures coordinated with DEC-0051 and DEC-0062.

## Testing

Plans should be exercised through practical scenarios rather than treated as documents only.

## Success Standard

The company knows what must be restored first, who is responsible, how customers and employees will be informed, and how operations can continue during significant disruption.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Identify critical processes and dependencies.
2. Define impact and recovery priorities.
3. Document continuity and manual fallback procedures.
4. Establish backup and restoration controls.
5. Build incident communications plans.
6. Test scenarios and close identified gaps.
