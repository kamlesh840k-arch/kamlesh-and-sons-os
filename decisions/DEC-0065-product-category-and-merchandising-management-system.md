# DEC-0065 — Product, Category & Merchandising Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0065  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Product, Category and Merchandising Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS
**Related Research:** [Consumer demand segments and catchment](../docs/research/western-nepal-device-demand-segments-and-catchment.md), [Integrated market size, assortment and flagship defensibility](../docs/research/integrated-market-size-assortment-and-flagship-defensibility.md)

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will manage its product assortment and merchandising as a disciplined commercial system that balances customer usefulness, product quality, availability, margin, inventory risk and brand positioning.

> **Curate what customers need, present it clearly, and earn healthy economics from every category.**

## 1. Assortment Principles

The assortment should be customer-relevant, differentiated, commercially viable and operationally manageable.

## 2. Category Architecture

Products should be organized into a clear hierarchy of categories, subcategories and product families.

## 3. Product Master

Every active product should have a controlled master record with an authoritative identifier.

## 4. Product Attributes

Important attributes may include:

- Brand
- Model
- Specification
- Size
- Variant
- Cost
- Price
- Category
- Supplier
- Warranty

## 5. Product Information Quality

Customer-facing product information should be accurate, complete and consistent across channels.

## 6. Product Source of Truth

Approved product specifications should come from authoritative supplier, manufacturer or internally verified sources.

## 7. Product Onboarding

New products should pass defined commercial, supply, compliance and content checks before sale.

## 8. Product Compliance

Regulated or safety-sensitive products require appropriate compliance review under DEC-0058.

## 9. Product Economics

Products should be evaluated on purchase cost, landed cost, selling price, gross margin and inventory burden.

## 10. Margin Targets

Category and product targets should reflect market conditions, strategic role and customer value.

## 11. Good-Better-Best

Where appropriate, assortment architecture may provide clear choices across price and capability levels.

## 12. Hero Products

Selected products may receive greater merchandising attention when they materially support customer value and commercial goals.

## 13. Traffic Products

Low-margin products may have strategic value when they attract customers, provided their economics are understood.

## 14. Profit Products

Higher-margin products should be managed without sacrificing customer trust or fair value.

## 15. Attachment Products

Complementary products should be identified where they genuinely improve customer outcomes.

## 16. Bundling

Bundles should provide clear customer value and transparent pricing.

## 17. Cross-Sell

Cross-sell recommendations should be relevant rather than purely maximizing transaction size.

## 18. Up-Sell

Up-sell suggestions should be based on meaningful additional customer benefit.

## 19. Assortment Breadth

Breadth should be balanced against inventory complexity and capital requirements.

## 20. Assortment Depth

Depth should reflect demand predictability and customer importance.

## 21. SKU Rationalization

Low-value or redundant SKUs should be periodically reviewed for removal.

## 22. New Product Gate

New product decisions should consider:

- Customer demand
- Supplier reliability
- Margin
- Availability
- Compliance
- Warranty
- Inventory risk

## 23. Product Testing

Where product quality is uncertain, samples or controlled pilots should precede broad purchase commitments.

## 24. Supplier Samples

Samples should be documented and evaluated against specifications where material.

## 25. Quality Feedback

Customer complaints, returns and warranty data should feed back into assortment decisions.

## 26. Product Reviews

Product performance should be reviewed periodically using sales, margin, returns, service and customer feedback.

## 27. Product Lifecycle

Products should be managed across introduction, growth, maturity, decline and exit.

## 28. Launch Planning

Product launches should coordinate inventory, content, pricing, marketing, sales and service readiness.

## 29. Launch Inventory

Initial stock should reflect demand uncertainty and cash constraints rather than optimistic assumptions alone.

## 30. Pricing Coordination

Product pricing should align with DEC-0057 and approved commercial policies.

## 31. Competitive Positioning

Competitive comparisons should use reliable information and comply with DEC-0058.

## 32. Price Architecture

Pricing should create understandable relationships between products and variants.

## 33. Promotional Pricing

Promotional prices should have defined objectives, timing and margin implications.

## 34. Markdown Management

Markdowns should be used to manage inventory and demand while protecting brand value.

## 35. Clearance

Clearance processes should define authorization, pricing, customer communication and inventory accounting.

## 36. Seasonal Products

Seasonal products should have explicit buy, sell-through and exit plans.

## 37. Long-Tail Products

Long-tail products should be retained only when their customer or strategic value justifies inventory and management complexity.

## 38. Private Label

Any private-label initiative should receive additional quality, legal, supply and brand review.

## 39. Exclusive Products

Exclusive products should have clear commercial rationale and appropriate supplier agreements.

## 40. Brand Portfolio

Brand selection should balance customer trust, availability, economics and category relevance.

## 41. Supplier Brand Rights

Required brand authorization or distribution rights should be verified where applicable.

## 42. Authenticity

The company should maintain controls against counterfeit or unauthorized products.

## 43. Product Traceability

Material product batches, serials or identifiers should be tracked where required or commercially useful.

## 44. Warranty Mapping

Product records should identify applicable warranty responsibilities and service routes.

## 45. Returns Mapping

Return conditions should connect product categories to customer-service processes.

## 46. Serviceability

Products requiring installation, repair or specialized support should be evaluated for service capacity before expansion.

## 47. Installation Products

Installation-dependent products should include clear readiness, scheduling and support requirements.

## 48. Compatibility

Products with compatibility requirements should clearly state relevant dependencies.

## 49. Product Content

Content should help customers make informed decisions, including important limitations.

## 50. Images and Media

Product media should accurately represent products and variants.

## 51. Search and Navigation

Digital product categorization should support intuitive discovery and filtering.

## 52. Merchandising Rules

Merchandising should define placement, visibility, sequencing and promotional logic.

## 53. Store Merchandising

Physical displays should balance discovery, accessibility, safety and inventory practicality.

## 54. Digital Merchandising

Online merchandising should balance relevance, availability, margin and customer intent.

## 55. Recommendations

Recommendation systems should prioritize relevance and customer usefulness.

## 56. Personalization

Personalized merchandising must respect privacy and customer preferences under DEC-0055.

## 57. Category Reviews

Each material category should have periodic review of demand, margin, competition and inventory performance.

## 58. Category Strategy

Category strategies should define the intended role of each category in the overall portfolio.

## 59. Category Growth

Growth plans should identify demand drivers, supply constraints and required investment.

## 60. Category Profitability

Category profitability should consider direct margin and relevant operational costs.

## 61. Inventory Productivity

Merchandising decisions should consider stock turns, sell-through and capital efficiency.

## 62. Space Productivity

Physical merchandising should consider sales and margin relative to space consumed.

## 63. Digital Space

Online placement should be evaluated for customer engagement and commercial performance.

## 64. Supplier Collaboration

Suppliers may support assortment development, training and merchandising within approved agreements.

## 65. Vendor Funding

Supplier-funded placement or promotions should be documented and reconciled.

## 66. Category Conflicts

Supplier incentives should not override customer value or company assortment strategy.

## 67. Product Substitution

When products are unavailable, substitutes should be selected using clear compatibility and value rules.

## 68. Out-of-Stock Experience

Customers should receive accurate availability information and useful alternatives when possible.

## 69. Product Availability

Critical products should have defined availability expectations linked to DEC-0064.

## 70. Demand Signals

Product decisions should use analytics from DEC-0060 and market feedback from DEC-0059.

## 71. Customer Feedback

Reviews, complaints, returns and service cases should influence product decisions.

## 72. Product Defects

Material defect patterns should trigger supplier corrective action and potential assortment review.

## 73. Product Safety

Safety concerns require immediate escalation and appropriate containment.

## 74. Product Recall

Recall processes should coordinate legal, supplier, inventory, customer service and communications functions.

## 75. Regulatory Change

Category managers should monitor material regulatory changes affecting products.

## 76. AI Merchandising

AI may assist with recommendations, assortment analysis, demand signals and content generation under DEC-0063.

## 77. AI Human Review

Material assortment and pricing recommendations should remain subject to accountable commercial review.

## 78. Merchandising Experiments

New layouts, bundles, recommendations and offers may be tested using controlled experiments.

## 79. Experiment Measurement

Merchandising experiments should evaluate incremental sales, margin, customer experience and inventory impact.

## 80. Product Portfolio Dashboard

Management should monitor:

- Sales
- Margin
- Sell-through
- Stock turns
- Returns
- Warranty
- Availability
- Customer satisfaction

## 81. Product Performance Tiers

Products may be classified by strategic and commercial performance to focus management attention.

## 82. Underperformers

Persistent underperformance should trigger root-cause analysis rather than automatic discounting.

## 83. Product Exit

Exit decisions should consider remaining stock, supplier commitments, customer need and financial impact.

## 84. Supplier Exit

Poor supplier performance may require corrective action, reduced allocation or replacement.

## 85. Category Exit

Categories may be exited when strategic value and economics no longer justify complexity.

## 86. New Category Entry

New category entry requires demand, supply, compliance, service and economic assessment.

## 87. Geographic Assortment

Different stores or regions may carry different assortments when local demand justifies it.

## 88. Assortment Governance

Material assortment changes should have defined approval authority.

## 89. Documentation

Category decisions should document rationale, expected outcomes and major assumptions.

## 90. Review Cadence

Product and category reviews should occur at frequencies appropriate to category volatility.

## 91. Data Quality

Merchandising decisions depend on accurate product, sales and inventory data under DEC-0060.

## 92. Supply Coordination

Merchandising should coordinate with demand planning and procurement under DEC-0064.

## 93. Finance Coordination

Product decisions should incorporate cash, margin and working-capital considerations under DEC-0057.

## 94. Marketing Coordination

Merchandising and marketing should align offers, content and customer messaging under DEC-0059.

## 95. Legal Coordination

Product claims, warranties and regulated categories should align with DEC-0058.

## 96. Continuous Improvement

Assortment and merchandising should evolve based on customer behavior, product performance and market evidence.

## 97. Key Risks

Major product and merchandising risks include:

- Poor assortment fit
- Overstock
- Stockouts
- Counterfeit goods
- Margin erosion
- Product defects
- Regulatory non-compliance
- Inaccurate content
- Excessive SKU complexity
- Supplier dependency

## 98. Governance

Material product and category risks should be included in management and enterprise risk reviews.

## 99. Customer Value

Commercial decisions should preserve a clear connection between product selection and customer benefit.

## 100. Success Standard

The merchandising system is successful when leadership can answer:

- What should we sell?
- Why should we sell it?
- Who is it for?
- What does it earn?
- How reliably can we source it?
- How much inventory should we hold?
- When should we promote or exit it?

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish product and category master-data standards.
2. Define category roles, assortment rules and approval authority.
3. Build product economics and performance dashboards.
4. Integrate merchandising with demand planning, procurement and marketing.
5. Standardize product onboarding, content, warranty and compliance checks.
6. Introduce structured category reviews and SKU rationalization.
7. Pilot recommendation and merchandising analytics.
8. Introduce controlled AI assistance for assortment and content workflows.
9. Continuously optimize the portfolio based on customer value and economics.
