## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will be built with the discipline of a technology company: decisions should be documented, processes should be systemized, ideas should be recorded, and important changes should remain traceable over time.

The company should optimize for long-term trust, capability, and institutional knowledge rather than short-term convenience.

## Founder Principle

> **We are not building a phone store. We are building Nepal's most trusted premium technology brand.**

This principle is the benchmark for future decisions across branding, retail, hiring, customer service, technology, AI, operations, finance, and expansion.

## Operating Principles

### 1. Customer First

Build around genuine customer needs and long-term relationships rather than short-term transactions.

### 2. Experience Over Discounts

Compete through trust, knowledge, service, convenience, and experience—not only price.

### 3. Documentation Creates Institutional Memory

Important knowledge should not exist only in conversations or in individual employees' memories. The K&S OS repository should preserve decisions, standards, research, and operating knowledge.

### 4. Decisions Must Be Traceable

Material decisions should have a decision ID, rationale, status, related documents, and an understandable history of change.

### 5. Ideas Should Become Discoverable Assets

Promising ideas should be captured in the Innovation Vault and connected to decisions, experiments, research, or roadmap items where appropriate.

### 6. Research Before Major Commitment

Significant assumptions should be tested with evidence whenever practical. Uncertainty should be stated rather than hidden.

### 7. Build Systems, Not Heroics

The company should reduce dependence on individual memory and improvisation by creating repeatable processes, SOPs, training, and technology.

### 8. Technology With Purpose

Technology should solve meaningful customer or operational problems and should not be adopted simply because it is new.

### 9. Human Judgment Matters

AI and automation should support people, not remove the human qualities that create trust, empathy, accountability, and good judgment.

### 10. Think Long Term

Brand, systems, customer trust, people, and technology should be built to remain valuable as the company grows.

## Decision-Making Standard

When evaluating a significant idea, the company should consider:

1. Does it support the vision?
2. Does it improve customer value?
3. Does it strengthen the premium position?
4. Is there evidence supporting the assumption?
5. What are the risks and trade-offs?
6. What systems or processes will it affect?
7. What decisions or documents must change?
8. Can the approach scale beyond one store?
9. What should be tested before full commitment?

This is a decision framework, not a requirement that every minor operational choice receive formal executive documentation.

## Documentation Discipline

The K&S OS should progressively maintain:

- Founder's Playbook
- Brand System
- Store Design standards
- Customer Experience standards
- AI and Technology architecture
- Operations and SOPs
- Marketing standards
- Financial models
- Expansion plans
- Research
- Legal and compliance records
- Decision Log
- Innovation Vault
- Meeting records

Cross-references should be used so that a future employee can understand not only **what** the company decided, but **why**.

## Source-of-Truth Hierarchy

For company knowledge, the intended hierarchy is:

**Approved GitHub documentation → Approved operational systems → AI interfaces and summaries → Informal discussion**

Chat and meetings may generate ideas and discussion, but important outcomes should be transferred into the official repository.

AI-generated summaries must not silently override approved documentation.

## Innovation Discipline

Innovation should be encouraged but structured.

A new idea should be evaluated for:

- Customer value
- Strategic fit
- Feasibility
- Cost
- Risk
- Differentiation
- Scalability
- Evidence

Promising concepts can then move through discovery, research, experiment, validation, decision, and implementation.

## Learning Culture

A failed experiment should not automatically be treated as failure of the people involved. The company should capture what was learned, update assumptions, and improve the system.

The goal is continuous improvement without normalizing avoidable mistakes.

## Leadership Standard

Leadership should model the behavior expected from the organization:

- Document important decisions.
- Admit uncertainty.
- Seek evidence.
- Listen to customers.
- Protect trust.
- Improve systems.
- Develop people.
- Challenge weak assumptions respectfully.
- Take responsibility for outcomes.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **foundational operating philosophy** for KAMLESH & SONS. It does not replace detailed policies, SOPs, employment rules, financial controls, legal requirements, or technical standards.

Those documents should implement these principles at the appropriate level of detail.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. As K&S OS matures, more specific policies may be added beneath these principles.

Any material change to the founder philosophy should be treated as a significant strategic decision and should document the rationale and consequences carefully.
