# DEC-0043 — Finance, Accounting & Financial Control System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0043  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Finance, Accounting and Financial Control System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md), [Trade-in and ownership-support economics](../docs/research/trade-in-and-ownership-support-economics.md)
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0036, DEC-0038, DEC-0039, DEC-0040, DEC-0041, DEC-0042

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will establish disciplined financial management as a core operating capability of K&S OS.

The finance system must protect cash, preserve accurate records, support responsible growth, and make financial performance understandable to leadership.

> **Growth without financial discipline is not a strategy.**

## 1. Finance Philosophy

Finance should enable the company to:

- Know where money comes from and where it goes
- Protect cash
- Understand profitability
- Control risk
- Make informed investments
- Price products responsibly
- Plan expansion
- Meet applicable obligations

Financial discipline should support the premium customer experience rather than undermine it.

## 2. Accounting System

KAMLESH & SONS should maintain accurate accounting records through an approved accounting system and appropriate professional support.

The system should support, as applicable:

- Sales
- Purchases
- Expenses
- Receivables
- Payables
- Inventory accounting
- Fixed assets
- Taxes
- Bank transactions
- Payroll
- Financial reporting

## 3. Chart of Accounts

A structured chart of accounts should be established before meaningful financial reporting begins.

It should distinguish major categories of:

- Revenue
- Cost of goods sold
- Operating expenses
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Equity

The chart should be designed to support future multi-location reporting.

## 4. Financial Periods

The company should establish controlled accounting periods and closing procedures.

Period-end processes should prevent unauthorized changes to finalized records without appropriate approval and audit trail.

## 5. Revenue Recognition

Sales should be recorded consistently with applicable accounting practices and the actual commercial transaction.

Revenue reporting should distinguish, where relevant:

- Product sales
- Service revenue
- Corporate sales
- Other operating revenue

Specific accounting treatment should be validated with the company's accounting professional.

## 6. Cost of Goods Sold

Management should understand the cost associated with products sold.

Gross margin should be visible by meaningful dimensions where systems support it, potentially including:

- Product category
- Brand
- Store
- Sales channel
- Customer segment

## 7. Gross Margin Discipline

KAMLESH & SONS should not evaluate sales only through revenue.

Important commercial decisions should consider:

- Selling price
- Product cost
- Discounts
- Financing costs
- Payment fees
- Warranty/service implications
- Marketing costs where relevant

## 8. Pricing Governance

Pricing should follow controlled processes.

Changes should consider:

- Supplier cost
- Market conditions
- Taxes
- Currency movement where relevant
- Competitor positioning
- Margin requirements
- Customer value

Unauthorized price manipulation should not be permitted.

## 9. Discount Governance

Discounts should have clear authority levels.

The company should define:

- Standard pricing
- Promotional pricing
- Maximum discretionary discount
- Approval requirements
- Documentation requirements

Discounts should not become an uncontrolled leakage of margin.

## 10. Cash Management

Cash is a critical operating asset.

Management should maintain visibility into:

- Cash on hand
- Bank balances
- Expected receipts
- Expected payments
- Upcoming obligations
- Inventory commitments

## 11. Bank Controls

Company bank accounts should be controlled and reconciled regularly.

Bank access should be limited according to role.

Payment authorization should be separated from ordinary operational access where practical.

## 12. Bank Reconciliation

Bank accounts should be reconciled regularly to accounting records.

Unexplained differences should be investigated rather than carried indefinitely.

## 13. Petty Cash

If petty cash is used, it should have:

- Defined custodian
- Approved limits
- Supporting receipts
- Reconciliation
- Periodic review

## 14. Accounts Receivable

Customer receivables, especially corporate or financed arrangements where applicable, should be tracked systematically.

The company should monitor:

- Outstanding balance
- Due date
- Aging
- Collection status
- Credit exposure

## 15. Accounts Payable

Supplier obligations should be recorded and tracked.

Management should know upcoming payment commitments to protect cash flow and supplier relationships.

## 16. Procurement-to-Payment

Purchases should follow a controlled flow:

**Need → Approval → Purchase Order/Authorization → Receipt → Invoice → Verification → Payment**

The exact workflow may vary by transaction size.

## 17. Expense Approval

Company expenses should have appropriate authorization based on defined thresholds.

Employees should not approve their own unsupported expenses.

## 18. Segregation of Duties

Where practical, critical financial activities should be separated among different people.

For example:

- Request
- Approval
- Receipt/verification
- Payment
- Reconciliation

Small teams may require compensating controls.

## 19. Inventory and Finance

Inventory is both a commercial and financial asset.

Finance and operations should reconcile inventory records and investigate material differences.

## 20. Inventory Valuation

Inventory valuation should follow a consistent accounting method appropriate to the business and applicable accounting requirements.

The method should be documented and applied consistently.

## 21. Shrinkage and Loss

Inventory losses may arise from:

- Theft
- Damage
- Miscounts
- Administrative errors
- Returns
- Warranty/service handling

Material variances should be investigated and documented.

## 22. Fixed Assets

Material company assets should be recorded appropriately.

Potential assets include:

- Store fixtures
- Computers
- POS equipment
- Network equipment
- Furniture
- Tools

Capital expenditure should be distinguished from ordinary operating expense according to applicable accounting treatment.

## 23. Capital Expenditure

Major investments should have a business case covering, where appropriate:

- Purpose
- Cost
- Expected benefit
- Cash-flow impact
- Risks
- Approval

## 24. Store Economics

Each store should eventually have visibility into:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- Inventory investment
- Cash performance
- Customer metrics
- Store contribution

This becomes increasingly important during expansion.

## 25. Budgeting

Management should establish annual and periodic budgets covering:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Payroll
- Marketing
- Rent
- Technology
- Operations
- Capital expenditure
- Cash flow

Budgets are planning tools, not guarantees.

## 26. Forecasting

Forecasts should be updated as actual business performance changes.

Management should compare:

**Plan → Actual → Variance → Explanation → Action**

## 27. Cash-Flow Forecast

A rolling cash-flow forecast should become a core management tool.

It should consider:

- Expected sales receipts
- Supplier payments
- Payroll
- Rent
- Taxes
- Financing payments
- Capital expenditure
- Inventory purchases

## 28. Financial Reporting

Leadership should receive regular management reporting appropriate to the company's stage.

Potential reports include:

- Profit and loss
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow
- Sales summary
- Gross margin
- Inventory
- Receivables
- Payables
- Budget variance

## 29. Management Dashboard

The future Founder Dashboard may provide summarized financial indicators.

However, dashboards must reflect the controlled accounting source rather than becoming an independent unofficial financial system.

## 30. Tax and Statutory Compliance

KAMLESH & SONS must comply with applicable Nepalese tax, accounting and statutory requirements.

Tax registrations, invoicing, filings and records should be managed with qualified professional support where appropriate.

This decision is not legal or tax advice.

## 31. VAT and Invoicing

Customer invoicing should be accurate and consistent with applicable requirements.

The company should maintain appropriate records for VAT and other applicable taxes.

Claims about VAT treatment should be verified before being communicated to customers.

## 32. EMI and Financing

If KAMLESH & SONS offers EMI or financing arrangements, the financial model should distinguish:

- Customer purchase value
- Financing provider
- Merchant fees
- Settlement timing
- Customer obligations
- Refund/cancellation implications

Commercial terms should be documented before launch.

## 33. Refunds and Returns

Refunds and returns should follow approved customer policies and financial controls.

Transactions should preserve the link between:

- Original sale
- Customer
- Product/serial information where applicable
- Payment
- Refund/credit

## 34. Trade-In Accounting

Trade-in programs require controlled valuation and accounting.

The system should distinguish:

- Customer trade-in value
- Purchase transaction
- Acquired used device
- Resale/disposition value
- Loss or gain

Specific accounting treatment should be established before launch.

## 35. Fraud Controls

Finance should work with operations, security and service teams to detect and prevent:

- Fake refunds
- Unauthorized discounts
- Payment fraud
- Inventory manipulation
- Expense fraud
- Supplier fraud
- Trade-in fraud
- Cash theft

## 36. Audit Trail

Financially significant transactions should be traceable to supporting records and authorized actions.

Systems should preserve sufficient history for investigation and reconciliation.

## 37. Financial Document Retention

Invoices, receipts, contracts, payment records and other financial documents should be retained according to applicable requirements and company policy.

## 38. Supplier Financial Controls

Important suppliers should have verified commercial terms covering, as applicable:

- Pricing
- Credit terms
- Payment timing
- Returns
- Warranty
- Rebates/incentives
- Delivery

## 39. Related-Party Transactions

Transactions involving owners, family members, employees or related entities should be identified and handled transparently according to applicable accounting and legal requirements.

## 40. Financial Decision-Making

Major decisions should consider:

- Customer impact
- Strategic fit
- Revenue potential
- Gross margin
- Cash requirement
- Return on investment
- Downside risk
- Operational capacity

A large revenue opportunity is not automatically a good business decision.

## 41. Expansion Financial Gate

A new store should not open solely because the brand is ready.

Expansion should consider:

- Required capital
- Working capital
- Expected revenue
- Expected margin
- Fixed costs
- Break-even expectations
- Cash runway
- Management capacity
- Risk scenarios

## 42. Financial Controls for Growth

As the company expands, controls should become stronger rather than remaining informal.

Multi-location operations should support consolidated and location-level reporting.

## 43. Finance Technology

Finance systems should integrate appropriately with:

- POS
- Inventory
- CRM where financially relevant
- Procurement
- Banking/payment systems
- Payroll
- Reporting

Integrations should preserve data integrity and clear ownership of source records.

## 44. Internal AI and Finance

Internal AI may assist with:

- Report summaries
- Variance explanations
- Forecast analysis
- Document retrieval
- Management questions

AI-generated financial conclusions should be verified against controlled financial records before important decisions.

## 45. Financial Security

Financial systems require appropriate access controls, authentication, audit logs and backup procedures consistent with DEC-0042.

## 46. Financial KPIs

Management may eventually monitor:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Operating margin
- Cash balance
- Cash conversion
- Inventory turnover
- Days of inventory
- Receivables aging
- Payables aging
- Average transaction value
- Discount rate
- Store contribution
- Customer acquisition economics

The exact KPI set should evolve with business maturity.

## 47. Financial Review Cadence

A practical management cadence should eventually include:

- Daily operational sales/cash visibility
- Weekly cash and trading review
- Monthly management accounts
- Quarterly strategic financial review
- Annual planning and budget cycle

Exact cadence may change with scale.

## 48. Risks

Major financial risks include:

- Cash-flow shortage
- Excess inventory
- Margin erosion
- Unauthorized discounts
- Fraud
- Tax/compliance failure
- Supplier concentration
- Customer credit exposure
- Poor forecasting
- Uncontrolled expansion
- Weak financial records

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish accounting ownership and professional support.
2. Define chart of accounts.
3. Establish banking and payment controls.
4. Integrate sales and inventory records.
5. Establish expense and procurement approvals.
6. Implement reconciliation procedures.
7. Establish budgeting and cash-flow forecasting.
8. Build monthly management reporting.
9. Establish fraud and audit controls.
10. Integrate finance into store and expansion decisions.
11. Build Founder Dashboard financial views from controlled sources.
12. Strengthen controls as the company expands.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **finance, accounting and financial-control direction** for K&S OS. It does not finalize accounting methods, tax positions, banking partners, financing contracts, compensation amounts, or statutory compliance procedures.

Those require current financial data, applicable Nepalese requirements and appropriate professional accounting/tax/legal review.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material financial policy changes should document the rationale, economic impact, risk, affected systems and related decision IDs.
