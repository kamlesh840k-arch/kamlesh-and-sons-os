## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build a documented **Store Operations and Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Management System** so that important store processes are consistent, trainable, measurable, auditable, and continuously improvable.

The objective is not to make employees read endless manuals. The objective is to turn the company's best operating practices into clear, usable systems while preserving human judgment.

> **Standardize what should be consistent. Empower people where judgment matters.**

## 1. Purpose

The Store Operations System should provide a reliable operating foundation for:

- Opening and closing stores
- Customer welcome
- Product display
- Sales consultation
- Billing and payment
- EMI and financing workflows
- Device handover
- Setup and data transfer
- Inventory handling
- Stock movement
- Warranty assistance
- Service intake
- Complaints and escalation
- Cash and financial controls
- Store security
- Cleaning and maintenance
- Staff handovers
- Incident management

Exact SOPs will be developed progressively as operations mature.

## 2. SOP Hierarchy

Future operating documentation should use a clear hierarchy:

**Policy → Standard → SOP → Work Instruction → Checklist → Record**

### Policy
Defines a company-level rule or principle.

### Standard
Defines the expected operating outcome or quality level.

### SOP
Defines the repeatable process for achieving the standard.

### Work Instruction
Explains a specific task in greater operational detail where needed.

### Checklist
Provides a practical execution aid.

### Record
Provides evidence that an activity occurred.

This hierarchy should prevent policies, procedures, and checklists from becoming confused with one another.

## 3. SOP Metadata Standard

Each formal SOP should contain, where applicable:

- SOP ID
- Title
- Version
- Status
- Owner
- Approver
- Effective date
- Review date
- Purpose
- Scope
- Preconditions
- Responsibilities
- Required tools or systems
- Procedure
- Exceptions
- Escalation path
- Safety/security requirements
- Records generated
- Related documents
- Related decisions
- Change log

## 4. SOP IDs

Formal SOPs should use stable identifiers such as:

- `SOP-STORE-001`
- `SOP-SALES-001`
- `SOP-INVENTORY-001`
- `SOP-SERVICE-001`
- `SOP-CUSTOMER-001`

IDs should not be reused after retirement.

## 5. Store Opening and Closing

Opening and closing procedures should eventually cover, as applicable:

### Opening

- Security and access check
- Store condition
- Lighting and environment
- Demo-device readiness
- POS/system readiness
- Inventory exceptions
- Cash controls
- Staff readiness
- Customer-facing cleanliness

### Closing

- Sales and payment reconciliation
- Inventory/security checks
- Device and equipment checks
- Store condition
- Sensitive information protection
- System logout/lock procedures
- Physical security
- Incident handover

Exact checklists require operational testing before approval.

## 6. Customer-Facing Operations

Store SOPs must support the CX principles established in DEC-0028.

Processes should ensure customers receive:

- Respectful welcome
- Knowledgeable assistance
- Clear information
- Transparent terms
- Appropriate demonstrations
- Professional purchase handling
- Reliable post-purchase support

Operational efficiency must not become an excuse for poor hospitality.

## 7. Sales Operations

Sales SOPs should define the process without turning employees into scripts.

A standard sales journey may include:

**Welcome → Discover Needs → Demonstrate → Compare → Recommend → Explain Terms → Confirm → Complete Purchase → Handover → Follow-up**

Employees should be trained to adapt the conversation naturally to each customer.

## 8. Pricing, Discounts, and Financing

Pricing and discount authority should be clearly documented.

Employees should know:

- Approved prices
- Authorized discounts
- Approval thresholds
- Financing/EMI procedures
- Required customer disclosures
- Escalation paths

Employees should never invent discounts, financing terms, or commitments to close a sale.

## 9. Inventory Operations

Inventory SOPs should eventually cover:

- Receiving
- Verification
- Serial/IMEI capture where applicable
- Storage
- Display allocation
- Transfers
- Reservations
- Sales deduction
- Returns
- Damaged stock
- Missing stock
- Cycle counts
- Reconciliation

Inventory discrepancies should be investigated rather than silently adjusted.

## 10. Product Handover

A premium handover should verify, where applicable:

- Correct product
- Correct variant
- Accessories
- Invoice
- Warranty information
- Device condition
- Setup requirements
- Customer understanding
- Relevant service options

The goal is for the customer to leave confident rather than confused.

## 11. Device Setup and Data Transfer

Setup services should have clear consent, privacy, and responsibility boundaries.

Staff should not access personal customer information unnecessarily.

Where data transfer is performed, SOPs should address:

- Customer authorization
- Device compatibility
- Backup considerations
- Account credentials
- Data privacy
- Transfer verification
- Completion confirmation
- Handling of failed transfers

Customers should retain control of their credentials and sensitive information.

## 12. Service Intake

Service intake should create a clear record of:

- Customer identity where required
- Device
- Reported issue
- Physical condition
- Accessories received
- Warranty status
- Customer expectations
- Estimated next step
- Handover record

Customers should receive clear information about what happens after intake.

## 13. Complaint and Escalation

Operational SOPs should define escalation levels for issues that frontline employees cannot resolve.

Escalation should be based on factors such as:

- Customer impact
- Financial impact
- Safety
- Legal/compliance concerns
- Security
- Reputational risk
- Repeated failure

Escalation should transfer accountability, not simply transfer the customer from person to person.

## 14. Cash and Financial Controls

Store financial procedures should establish separation of responsibilities and appropriate reconciliation for:

- Cash
- Card payments
- Digital payments
- Refunds
- Discounts
- Financing transactions
- Daily sales reconciliation

Specific financial controls should be developed with Finance and legal/accounting requirements in mind.

## 15. Security and Loss Prevention

Store SOPs should address:

- Physical access
- High-value product security
- Inventory controls
- Customer privacy
- Employee access
- Incident reporting
- CCTV/access procedures where applicable
- Emergency response

Security procedures must comply with applicable law and should not create an unnecessarily hostile customer environment.

## 16. Employee Training

Employees should not be expected to memorize every SOP.

Training should combine:

- Initial onboarding
- Product training
- Process training
- Demonstration
- Supervised practice
- Assessment where appropriate
- Refresher training
- Change notifications

Critical SOPs should have evidence that relevant employees have been trained.

## 17. SOP Change Management

SOPs must have controlled versions.

A change should identify:

- What changed
- Why it changed
- Who approved it
- Effective date
- Affected roles
- Training requirement
- Related documents

Obsolete SOPs should be clearly marked or archived so employees do not accidentally follow retired instructions.

## 18. Exception Management

A good SOP must identify where exceptions can occur.

Employees should know:

- What they may decide themselves
- When supervisor approval is required
- When another department must be involved
- When a process must stop

Exceptions should be recorded when they reveal a weakness or recurring ambiguity in the standard process.

## 19. Operational Records

Important processes should produce appropriate records, such as:

- Sales records
- Inventory movements
- Service intake records
- Refund records
- Incident records
- Training records
- Opening/closing checklists
- Reconciliation records

Records should have appropriate retention and access controls.

## 20. AI-Assisted Operations

Future Internal AI may help employees find SOPs, answer process questions, summarize changes, or guide workflows.

AI must not override approved procedures silently.

Where an AI response conflicts with the current approved SOP, the SOP is authoritative and the discrepancy should be investigated.

AI-generated operational instructions should be clearly distinguishable from approved company procedures until reviewed and adopted.

## 21. SOP Quality Standard

A useful SOP should be:

- Clear
- Correct
- Current
- Practical
- Searchable
- Trainable
- Measurable
- Appropriately detailed

If employees repeatedly need verbal explanation to understand an SOP, the document should be improved.

## 22. Continuous Improvement

Store operations should use the loop:

**Observe → Record → Analyze → Improve → Test → Approve → Train → Standardize**

Operational incidents, customer complaints, employee feedback, audit findings, and new technology should all be potential sources of improvement.

## 23. Multi-Store Scalability

SOPs should be designed with future expansion in mind.

The goal is to establish a **core operating standard** that can be reused across stores while allowing controlled local variations where necessary.

A future multi-store model should distinguish:

- Company-wide standards
- Regional requirements
- Store-specific procedures

Local variation should be documented rather than becoming informal tribal knowledge.

## 24. Risks

Major operational risks include:

- Outdated SOPs
- Excessive bureaucracy
- Employees ignoring impractical procedures
- Inconsistent training
- Hidden local processes
- Poor inventory controls
- Unclear authority
- Over-standardization
- Under-documentation
- Conflicting versions

The system should optimize for reliable execution, not documentation volume.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Map critical store processes.
2. Identify high-risk and high-frequency workflows.
3. Define SOP hierarchy and IDs.
4. Draft priority SOPs.
5. Test them in real operations.
6. Train relevant employees.
7. Measure compliance and outcomes.
8. Improve unclear procedures.
9. Establish version control and review cycles.
10. Expand the operating library.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **Store Operations and SOP Management System direction**. It does not approve individual SOPs or create final operational rules for every process.

Priority SOPs should be developed, tested, approved, and stored in the appropriate `docs/06-operations/` or future operations structure, with cross-references to relevant decisions and CX standards.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. The operating system should mature as the first flagship store, service operation, technology stack, and future locations provide real-world evidence.

Any material change to SOP governance should document the rationale, operational impact, affected roles, training implications, and related repository documents.
