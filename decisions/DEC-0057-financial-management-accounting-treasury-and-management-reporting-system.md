# DEC-0057 — Financial Management, Accounting, Treasury & Management Reporting System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0057  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Financial Management, Accounting, Treasury and Management Reporting System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0045, DEC-0051, DEC-0052, DEC-0054, DEC-0056

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will operate finance as a disciplined management system covering accounting integrity, cash, working capital, profitability, budgeting, controls and decision support.

Financial information should be timely, accurate, understandable and traceable to underlying transactions.

> **Know where the money goes, know what creates value, and protect the cash that keeps the business alive.**

## 1. Finance Principles

Finance will prioritize:

- Accuracy
- Timeliness
- Transparency
- Cash discipline
- Margin protection
- Proper authorization
- Auditability
- Useful management insight

## 2. Accounting Basis

The company should maintain books using an appropriate accounting basis and chart of accounts suited to its jurisdiction, size and reporting requirements.

## 3. Chart of Accounts

The chart of accounts should support useful reporting across:

- Revenue
- Cost of goods sold
- Gross margin
- Operating expenses
- Assets
- Liabilities
- Equity
- Cash flows

## 4. Accounting Ownership

Material accounting processes should have clear owners and review responsibilities.

## 5. Transaction Integrity

Financial transactions should be supported by appropriate source records and approvals.

## 6. Segregation of Duties

Where practical, initiation, approval, execution, recording and reconciliation should not all be controlled by one person.

## 7. General Ledger

The general ledger should remain the authoritative accounting record for financial reporting.

## 8. Subledgers

Operational systems such as inventory, sales, receivables, payables and fixed assets should reconcile to financial records where applicable.

## 9. Bank Accounts

Bank accounts should be approved, documented and periodically reviewed.

## 10. Bank Reconciliation

Bank accounts should be reconciled regularly, with discrepancies investigated and resolved.

## 11. Cash Management

Management should maintain visibility into available cash, committed cash and expected inflows/outflows.

## 12. Cash Forecasting

Rolling cash forecasts should consider:

- Sales collections
- Supplier payments
- Payroll
- Taxes
- Rent
- Capital expenditure
- Debt obligations
- Other material commitments

## 13. Liquidity Buffer

The company should maintain an appropriate liquidity buffer based on operating volatility, obligations and risk.

## 14. Payment Authorization

Payments should follow defined approval thresholds and authorized payment processes.

## 15. Supplier Payments

Supplier payments should be supported by appropriate purchasing and receiving evidence, consistent with DEC-0052.

## 16. Accounts Payable

Accounts payable should track:

- Supplier
- Invoice
- Due date
- Approval
- Payment status
- Exceptions

## 17. Three-Way Matching

Where practical, supplier invoices should be matched against purchase orders and receiving evidence before payment.

## 18. Duplicate Invoice Controls

Systems and processes should identify potential duplicate invoices before payment.

## 19. Accounts Receivable

Customer receivables should be monitored where the business provides credit or invoiced services.

## 20. Credit Policy

Customer credit should be offered only under approved commercial and risk criteria.

## 21. Collections

Overdue receivables should have defined ownership and follow-up procedures.

## 22. Revenue Recognition

Revenue should be recorded according to the applicable accounting framework and the underlying transaction terms.

## 23. Sales Reconciliation

Sales recorded in commerce/POS systems should reconcile to financial records and payment settlements.

## 24. Refunds

Refunds should be traceable to the underlying transaction and authorized according to policy.

## 25. Discounts

Material discounts should have defined authority and be visible in profitability reporting.

## 26. Gross Margin

Management should monitor gross margin by relevant dimensions such as:

- Product
- Category
- Brand
- Store
- Channel
- Supplier

## 27. Landed Cost

Where relevant, product economics should include appropriate acquisition, freight, duties, handling and other landed costs.

## 28. Inventory Accounting

Inventory accounting should reconcile operational inventory records with financial records.

## 29. Inventory Valuation

Inventory should be valued using an appropriate accounting method consistently applied.

## 30. Shrinkage

Material inventory shrinkage should be reflected appropriately and investigated according to DEC-0053.

## 31. Obsolescence

Slow-moving, obsolete or damaged inventory should be identified for appropriate accounting treatment and commercial action.

## 32. Purchase Commitments

Material purchase commitments should be visible to management for cash and working-capital planning.

## 33. Working Capital

Management should monitor:

- Inventory days
- Receivable days
- Payable days
- Cash conversion cycle

## 34. Budgeting

The company should maintain an annual operating budget once scale makes formal budgeting useful.

## 35. Forecasting

Forecasts should be updated when material assumptions change rather than treated as static documents.

## 36. Budget vs Actual

Material variances between budget and actual performance should be visible and investigated.

## 37. Rolling Forecast

As the company grows, a rolling forecast should complement the annual budget.

## 38. Scenario Planning

Management should model relevant scenarios such as:

- Demand decline
- Supplier disruption
- Margin compression
- Major expansion
- Currency movement
- Significant capital expenditure

## 39. Cost Management

Costs should be understood by purpose, owner and business impact.

## 40. Operating Expense Controls

Material recurring expenses should have owners and approved budgets or spending limits.

## 41. Procurement-Finance Integration

Finance should connect with procurement so commitments are visible before cash is spent.

## 42. Capital Expenditure

Material capital expenditure should have business justification, approval and post-investment review where appropriate.

## 43. Fixed Assets

Material fixed assets should be recorded, depreciated where applicable and reconciled to the asset register under DEC-0053.

## 44. Asset Disposal

Asset disposals should be authorized, documented and reflected correctly in financial records.

## 45. Payroll Accounting

Payroll costs and liabilities should reconcile to approved payroll records.

## 46. Tax Management

The company should maintain processes for identifying, calculating, filing and paying applicable taxes and statutory obligations.

## 47. Tax Calendar

Material tax and statutory deadlines should have responsible owners and visibility.

## 48. Tax Documentation

Supporting records should be retained according to applicable requirements and company retention policy.

## 49. Foreign Currency

Where imports or foreign transactions occur, FX exposure should be identified and reflected appropriately in financial planning and accounting.

## 50. FX Risk

Material currency exposure should be monitored and managed using proportionate controls.

## 51. Treasury

Treasury management should cover:

- Cash positioning
- Bank relationships
- Payments
- Liquidity
- FX
- Financing

## 52. Banking Relationships

Banking relationships should be periodically reviewed for service, reliability, fees, security and strategic fit.

## 53. Financing

Debt or other financing should be evaluated against repayment capacity, cost, risk and strategic need.

## 54. Debt Controls

Material borrowing should have approved terms, ownership and monitoring.

## 55. Financial Reserves

Management should determine appropriate reserves for operating volatility, taxes, repairs and other foreseeable obligations.

## 56. Fraud Prevention

Finance controls should address:

- Fake suppliers
- Duplicate invoices
- Unauthorized payments
- Refund fraud
- Expense fraud
- Payroll fraud
- Account manipulation

## 57. Payment Security

Payment instructions should be independently verified when changes or unusual requests create material fraud risk.

## 58. Vendor Bank Changes

Supplier bank-account changes should use controlled verification procedures.

## 59. Expense Management

Employee expenses should follow documented approval and evidence requirements.

## 60. Petty Cash

Where petty cash exists, it should have defined custody, limits and reconciliation.

## 61. Month-End Close

The company should establish a repeatable month-end close process covering material:

- Accruals
- Reconciliations
- Inventory
- Payables
- Receivables
- Payroll
- Fixed assets
- Taxes

## 62. Close Calendar

Close activities should have owners, deadlines and review checkpoints.

## 63. Reconciliations

Material balance-sheet accounts should be reconciled regularly.

## 64. Journal Entries

Material or unusual journal entries should have supporting documentation and appropriate review.

## 65. Accounting Estimates

Material estimates should have documented assumptions and appropriate review.

## 66. Financial Statements

Management reporting should include appropriate statements such as:

- Profit and loss
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow

## 67. Management Reporting

Reports should emphasize decisions and trends rather than simply reproducing accounting data.

## 68. Management Dashboard

Management should eventually monitor:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Operating profit
- Cash
- Working capital
- Inventory
- Receivables
- Payables
- Budget variance

## 69. Store Profitability

Where meaningful, management should evaluate profitability by store using consistent allocation methods.

## 70. Channel Profitability

Online, store and other channels should be evaluated using appropriate revenue and cost attribution.

## 71. Product Profitability

Product economics should consider selling price, discounts, landed cost, warranty/service burden and other material costs where available.

## 72. Customer Economics

Where data quality permits, management may evaluate customer-level or segment-level economics without compromising privacy requirements.

## 73. Contribution Margin

Contribution margin should be used where it improves decisions about pricing, promotions, channels or products.

## 74. Break-Even Analysis

Material investments and initiatives should be evaluated using appropriate break-even and payback analysis.

## 75. Investment Decisions

Investment proposals should consider:

- Expected return
- Cash requirement
- Risk
- Strategic value
- Downside case

## 76. Pricing Finance

Finance should support pricing decisions with margin and cost visibility, while commercial teams retain appropriate market judgment.

## 77. Promotion Economics

Promotions should evaluate incremental demand against margin cost and operational impact.

## 78. Rebate Accounting

Supplier rebates and incentives should be documented, reconciled and recognized according to the applicable accounting treatment.

## 79. Warranty Economics

Warranty and after-sales costs should be monitored by product and supplier where useful, consistent with DEC-0054.

## 80. Service Economics

Management should understand service revenue, cost, parts consumption and recovery.

## 81. Audit Trail

Financial records should maintain sufficient traceability from reported figures to source transactions.

## 82. Document Retention

Financial records should be retained according to applicable legal, tax and business requirements.

## 83. External Audit

If an external audit or review is required or strategically appropriate, the company should maintain readiness through organized records and reconciliations.

## 84. Internal Review

Management should periodically review important controls and financial processes even where no formal internal-audit function exists.

## 85. Financial Close Quality

Close quality should be measured by accuracy, timeliness and unresolved exceptions rather than speed alone.

## 86. Finance Technology

Finance systems should integrate appropriately with:

- POS/commerce
- Inventory
- Procurement
- Payroll
- Banking
- CRM where useful

## 87. Data Controls

Financial interfaces should use reconciliation and exception reporting to detect missing or duplicated transactions.

## 88. Finance Access

Financial systems should use role-based access and strong authentication consistent with DEC-0055.

## 89. Finance Segregation

Users who can create suppliers, approve payments and reconcile bank accounts should be separated where practical.

## 90. AI in Finance

AI may assist with:

- Report drafting
- Variance analysis
- Forecast support
- Transaction classification suggestions
- Anomaly detection
- Cash forecasting

Material accounting conclusions and payment decisions require appropriate human review.

## 91. AI Data Controls

Sensitive financial information should only be provided to approved AI systems consistent with DEC-0055.

## 92. Financial Data Quality

Management should monitor completeness, accuracy and timeliness of financial data feeds.

## 93. Exceptions

Unreconciled or unusual financial exceptions should be assigned, investigated and closed.

## 94. Financial Risk Register

Material financial risks should connect to the enterprise risk framework under DEC-0051.

## 95. Key Financial Risks

Major risks include:

- Liquidity shortage
- Margin erosion
- Inventory overinvestment
- Bad debts
- Fraud
- Tax non-compliance
- FX volatility
- Supplier concentration
- Financing stress
- Accounting errors

## 96. Financial Resilience

Management should test whether the business can withstand plausible adverse scenarios without unacceptable disruption.

## 97. Decision Support

Finance should act as a business partner by translating financial data into useful operational choices.

## 98. Financial Governance

Material changes to accounting policies, financing, banking, payment controls or reporting standards should follow appropriate approval.

## 99. Continuous Improvement

Finance processes should be simplified and automated where doing so improves control, accuracy or decision speed without weakening accountability.

## 100. Success Standard

The finance system is successful when leadership can answer, with reasonable confidence:

- How much cash do we have?
- What is profitable?
- What is consuming cash?
- What do we owe?
- What are customers owed to us?
- Where are margins changing?
- What risks require action?

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish chart of accounts and finance ownership.
2. Standardize banking, payment and reconciliation controls.
3. Connect sales, inventory, procurement and payroll to accounting.
4. Establish month-end close and management reporting.
5. Implement budgeting, forecasting and variance analysis.
6. Strengthen working-capital and cash forecasting.
7. Establish tax, fixed-asset and financial-document controls.
8. Build store, channel and product profitability reporting.
9. Introduce treasury, scenario planning and financing governance as scale requires.
10. Introduce controlled AI assistance and automated anomaly detection.
11. Continuously improve financial controls and decision support.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **financial management, accounting, treasury and management-reporting framework**. Specific accounting policies, tax treatments, banking arrangements, reporting standards and statutory obligations should be implemented with qualified finance/accounting and legal professionals as appropriate.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document financial, legal, operational and strategic implications.
