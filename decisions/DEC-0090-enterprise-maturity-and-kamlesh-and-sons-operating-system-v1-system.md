# DEC-0090 — Enterprise Maturity & KAMLESH & SONS Operating System v1 System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0090  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat the accumulated decision framework as an evolving operating system, using maturity assessments, periodic reviews and versioned improvements to move from founder-led execution toward a scalable, integrated enterprise.

> **Build the system so K&S can scale without losing control, speed, or identity.**

## Maturity Model

Capabilities should progress through practical stages such as:

**Ad hoc → Defined → Managed → Measured → Optimized → Intelligent**

Not every capability must mature at the same speed; investment should follow business importance and risk.

## Operating-System Governance

The K&S operating system should maintain:

- Decision records
- Policies and standards
- Process ownership
- KPI definitions
- Risk controls
- Technology architecture
- Organizational capability
- Improvement backlogs

## Versioning

Material changes to the operating system should be documented and versioned so leadership can understand what changed and why.

## Maturity Review

Periodic reviews should assess whether important capabilities are actually operating as designed, not merely documented.

## Integration

The operating system should connect strategy, finance, customers, people, operations, technology, data, risk and growth into a coherent management model.

## Scale Readiness

Before major expansion, leadership should assess whether the operating capabilities required for the next stage are sufficiently mature.

## Continuous Improvement

Lessons from incidents, customer feedback, performance data, experiments and strategic reviews should feed future operating-system updates.

## Success Standard

K&S can grow in complexity while retaining clear ownership, reliable information, disciplined execution, customer trust and the ability to improve continuously.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish a maturity baseline across major capabilities.
2. Identify the highest-value maturity gaps.
3. Assign improvement owners and priorities.
4. Review operating-system performance periodically.
5. Version material changes and preserve decision history.
6. Use maturity evidence to determine readiness for each major growth stage.
