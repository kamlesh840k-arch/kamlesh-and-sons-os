# DEC-0052 — Procurement, Supply Chain & Vendor Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0052  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Procurement, Supply Chain and Vendor Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0043, DEC-0045, DEC-0050, DEC-0051

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will establish a disciplined procurement and supply-chain system that protects product availability, margin, quality, cash flow and customer trust.

Procurement should be commercially competitive without sacrificing authenticity, reliability, compliance or long-term supplier relationships.

> **Buy intelligently, receive reliably, protect margin, and never compromise authenticity.**

## 1. Procurement Philosophy

Procurement decisions should balance:

- Availability
- Cost
- Gross margin
- Product authenticity
- Supplier reliability
- Payment terms
- Lead time
- Warranty support
- Compliance
- Strategic importance

Lowest purchase price alone is not the objective.

## 2. Procurement Governance

Material purchasing should follow documented authority levels consistent with DEC-0051.

Purchases should be approved according to financial and operational risk.

## 3. Supplier Master

The company should maintain a controlled supplier master containing appropriate information such as:

- Legal name
- Contact information
- Supplier category
- Authorized products
- Commercial terms
- Payment terms
- Account owner
- Compliance status
- Risk classification

## 4. Supplier Due Diligence

Important suppliers should be evaluated for:

- Legal standing
- Authenticity of products
- Commercial reliability
- Warranty capability
- Financial stability where relevant
- Data/security practices where applicable
- Regulatory exposure
- Reputation

## 5. Authorized Supply

Products should be sourced through legitimate channels appropriate to the product and market.

The company should avoid knowingly purchasing counterfeit, stolen or otherwise improperly sourced goods.

## 6. Supplier Segmentation

Suppliers may be classified by:

- Strategic importance
- Spend
- Product criticality
- Risk
- Availability alternatives

Higher-risk or more strategic suppliers should receive stronger oversight.

## 7. Purchase Requisition

Material purchases should begin with a documented requirement identifying:

- Product/SKU
- Quantity
- Required date
- Store/location
- Business reason
- Expected commercial impact

## 8. Purchase Order

Where appropriate, purchase orders should establish the agreed:

- Product
- Quantity
- Price
- Delivery terms
- Payment terms
- Warranty/support expectations

## 9. Three-Way Matching

Where practical, finance and operations should reconcile:

**Purchase Order → Receipt → Invoice**

Exceptions should be investigated before payment.

## 10. Supplier Quotes

Significant purchases should be competitively evaluated where practical.

Competitive quotes are not mandatory when a legitimate strategic or authorized-source reason exists, but the exception should be understandable and documented.

## 11. Price Negotiation

Negotiation should consider total commercial value rather than headline unit price alone.

Relevant factors include:

- Payment terms
- Freight
- Returns
- Warranty support
- Rebates
- Volume benefits
- Lead time
- Availability

## 12. Payment Terms

Supplier payment terms should be evaluated alongside cash-flow requirements.

Longer payment terms may be valuable, but should not justify uneconomic purchasing.

## 13. Working Capital

Procurement should coordinate with finance to avoid excessive inventory investment.

Purchasing should not be driven solely by supplier pressure or short-term promotional opportunities.

## 14. Demand Planning

Procurement should use demand signals such as:

- Historical sales
- Current pipeline
- Seasonal patterns
- Product launches
- Store expansion
- Marketing campaigns
- Supplier lead times

Forecasts should be treated as estimates, not certainty.

## 15. Inventory Planning

Inventory policy should distinguish between:

- Core products
- Fast movers
- Slow movers
- New launches
- Seasonal products
- Service stock
- Strategic display stock

## 16. Reorder Logic

Reorder systems should eventually consider:

- Current stock
- Reserved stock
- Expected demand
- Lead time
- Safety stock
- Supplier reliability

## 17. Stockout Management

Critical stockouts should be visible to procurement, sales and management.

Customer-facing teams should not promise availability that procurement cannot support.

## 18. Overstock Management

Slow-moving and excess inventory should be identified early.

Possible responses include:

- Promotion
- Transfer
- Bundling
- Supplier return where allowed
- Alternative sales channel
- Controlled markdown

## 19. Product Lifecycle

Procurement should monitor product lifecycle stages:

**Launch → Growth → Mature → Decline → End-of-Life**

Purchasing should adjust accordingly.

## 20. New Product Launch Procurement

New launches should coordinate:

- Demand estimates
- Initial allocation
- Marketing timing
- Store readiness
- Demonstration stock
- Accessories
- Service capacity

## 21. Authenticity Controls

High-risk products should have appropriate controls for:

- Serial/IMEI verification
- Packaging
- Supplier documentation
- Warranty eligibility
- Source records

## 22. Receiving Process

Goods receiving should verify:

- Quantity
- Product identity
- Condition
- Packaging
- Serial/IMEI where applicable
- Purchase order match
- Visible damage

## 23. Receiving Exceptions

Discrepancies should be recorded and resolved before inventory is treated as fully available where appropriate.

## 24. Inventory Traceability

Where applicable, serial-numbered products should remain traceable from receipt through sale, service, return or other disposition.

## 25. Warehouse Controls

Warehouse operations should maintain:

- Controlled access
- Organized storage
- Inventory records
- Cycle counts
- Damage controls
- Security

## 26. Inter-Store Transfers

Transfers should be authorized and recorded.

The source and destination locations should reconcile the movement.

## 27. Inventory Counts

Inventory should be periodically counted according to risk and value.

High-value or high-risk inventory should receive stronger counting controls.

## 28. Inventory Variance

Significant differences between physical and system inventory should be investigated rather than simply adjusted away.

## 29. Supplier Performance

Supplier performance should eventually be measured through factors such as:

- On-time delivery
- Fill rate
- Product quality
- Authenticity
- Price stability
- Warranty support
- Return handling
- Responsiveness

## 30. Supplier Scorecards

Strategic suppliers may receive periodic scorecards and business reviews.

## 31. Supplier Concentration Risk

The company should understand dependence on single suppliers, distributors or channels for critical products.

Where practical, alternatives should be evaluated.

## 32. Supply Disruption

Critical supply disruptions should have contingency plans where feasible.

Potential responses include:

- Alternate supplier
- Alternate product
- Store transfer
- Customer reservation
- Revised demand plan

## 33. Supplier Contracts

Material supplier relationships should have appropriate contractual documentation covering relevant commercial and operational terms.

## 34. Rebates and Incentives

Supplier rebates, incentives and marketing support should be documented and reconciled with finance.

## 35. Vendor Marketing Funds

Supplier-funded marketing should follow approved marketing and financial controls.

It should not compromise independent customer recommendations.

## 36. Returns to Supplier

Supplier returns should have controlled authorization, documentation and inventory reconciliation.

## 37. Defective Goods

Defective goods should be segregated where appropriate and tracked through resolution.

## 38. Warranty Supply Chain

Procurement should coordinate with service teams so warranty obligations can be supported with appropriate supplier relationships and spare parts where applicable.

## 39. Service Parts

Service inventory should be planned separately from ordinary retail inventory where appropriate.

Critical parts should have defined replenishment logic.

## 40. Accessories

Accessory procurement should consider attachment rates, compatibility, quality and customer value rather than simply maximizing SKU count.

## 41. Bundling

Bundles should have clear economics and genuine customer value.

Bundle inventory should remain traceable.

## 42. Procurement Data

Procurement systems should capture enough information to analyze:

- Purchase price
- Quantity
- Supplier
- Lead time
- Payment terms
- Product movement
- Margin

## 43. Purchase Price Variance

Material differences between expected and actual purchase prices should be visible and investigated.

## 44. Landed Cost

Where relevant, purchasing decisions should account for the total landed cost, including freight, duties, taxes and other applicable costs.

## 45. Gross Margin Protection

Procurement should understand the relationship between purchase cost, selling price, discounting and gross margin.

## 46. Cash Conversion

Inventory investment should be managed with attention to how quickly products convert into cash.

## 47. Procurement and Sales Alignment

Sales forecasts, promotions and major campaigns should be shared with procurement early enough to support demand.

## 48. Procurement and Marketing Alignment

Marketing campaigns involving specific products should be coordinated with confirmed supply.

## 49. Procurement and Finance Alignment

Finance should have visibility into material purchasing commitments, payment schedules and inventory exposure.

## 50. Procurement and Service Alignment

Service teams should communicate expected parts and warranty requirements to procurement.

## 51. Vendor Access

Supplier access to company systems or data should be limited to what is necessary and governed according to DEC-0050 and DEC-0051.

## 52. Supplier Data Protection

Supplier and commercial information should be protected according to its sensitivity.

## 53. Ethical Procurement

Procurement should avoid:

- Bribery
- Kickbacks
- Hidden personal benefits
- Conflicts of interest
- Supplier favoritism without legitimate reason

## 54. Conflict Disclosure

Employees involved in procurement should disclose material personal relationships or interests that could influence supplier decisions.

## 55. Procurement Fraud Controls

Controls should address risks including:

- Fake suppliers
- Duplicate invoices
- Unauthorized purchases
- Price manipulation
- Quantity manipulation
- False receiving
- Kickbacks

## 56. Vendor Onboarding

New suppliers should have documented onboarding requirements appropriate to their risk level.

## 57. Vendor Offboarding

When a supplier relationship ends, the company should close open obligations, remove unnecessary access and preserve required records.

## 58. Vendor Reviews

Strategic supplier relationships should be reviewed periodically for commercial, operational and risk performance.

## 59. Procurement Technology

Future procurement technology should integrate with:

- Inventory
- Finance
- Supplier records
- Purchase orders
- Receiving
- Reporting

## 60. Automation

Low-risk procurement workflows may be automated, such as:

- Reorder alerts
- Approval routing
- Supplier reminders
- Report generation

High-value purchasing decisions should retain appropriate human oversight.

## 61. AI Procurement Assistance

AI may assist with:

- Demand analysis
- Supplier comparisons
- Purchase summaries
- Anomaly detection
- Forecasting support

AI should not independently commit the company to material purchases unless explicitly authorized through controlled automation.

## 62. Procurement Dashboard

Management should eventually monitor:

- Inventory value
- Stockouts
- Overstock
- Purchase commitments
- Supplier performance
- Purchase price trends
- Inventory turnover
- Days of inventory
- Gross-margin exposure

## 63. Procurement KPIs

Potential KPIs include:

- Supplier on-time delivery
- Fill rate
- Purchase price variance
- Stockout rate
- Inventory turnover
- Excess inventory
- Receiving accuracy
- Supplier return rate
- Procurement savings/value

## 64. Strategic Sourcing

As the company scales, major categories should be reviewed for strategic sourcing opportunities.

## 65. National Scale

Procurement should evolve from store-level buying toward coordinated network purchasing as the store footprint grows.

## 66. Centralization vs Local Buying

Central procurement should be used where scale, consistency or control creates value.

Local purchasing may remain appropriate where local availability, urgency or market conditions justify it.

## 67. Import / Cross-Border Risk

If direct importing is introduced, the company should establish appropriate controls for:

- Customs
- Duties/taxes
- Documentation
- Currency exposure
- Transit insurance
- Regulatory requirements
- Product compliance

## 68. Foreign Exchange Exposure

Material foreign-currency procurement should be evaluated for currency risk and appropriate financial controls.

## 69. Supply Chain Resilience

Critical categories should be assessed for:

- Single-source dependency
- Long lead times
- Geographic concentration
- Regulatory disruption
- Logistics disruption

## 70. Sustainability

Where commercially practical, procurement may consider responsible sourcing, packaging reduction, product lifecycle and supplier environmental practices.

## 71. Risks

Major procurement and supply-chain risks include:

- Counterfeit products
- Supplier failure
- Stockouts
- Overstock
- Cash trapped in inventory
- Price volatility
- Fraud
- Supply disruption
- Poor receiving controls
- Vendor concentration
- Unauthorized purchasing

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish supplier and procurement master data.
2. Define purchasing authority levels.
3. Standardize purchase requisitions and purchase orders.
4. Establish receiving and inventory reconciliation controls.
5. Build supplier performance tracking.
6. Connect procurement with finance, inventory, sales and service.
7. Establish demand and inventory planning.
8. Introduce supplier scorecards and risk segmentation.
9. Build procurement dashboards.
10. Introduce controlled automation and AI assistance.
11. Develop centralized procurement capabilities for multi-store scale.
12. Continuously optimize working capital, availability and supplier resilience.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **procurement, supply-chain and vendor-management framework**. Specific suppliers, purchasing contracts, import arrangements, procurement software and category strategies should be evaluated and approved separately.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document commercial rationale, inventory impact, supplier risk, cash-flow implications and related decision IDs.
