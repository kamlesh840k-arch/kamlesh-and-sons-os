# DEC-0038 — Store Operations, SOP & Daily Management System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0038  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Store Operations, SOP and Daily Management System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0028, DEC-0030, DEC-0031, DEC-0032, DEC-0034, DEC-0035, DEC-0036, DEC-0037

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will operate each store through **clear, documented, measurable and continuously improving operating procedures**.

The store should feel effortless to the customer because the underlying operation is disciplined.

> **Premium experience requires operational excellence behind the scenes.**

## 1. Store Operating Philosophy

Store operations should consistently deliver:

- Readiness
- Cleanliness
- Product availability
- Product knowledge
- Hospitality
- Accuracy
- Security
- Speed
- Accountability

Processes should reduce errors without making the customer experience feel bureaucratic.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedures

Every recurring critical process should eventually have an SOP with:

- SOP ID
- Purpose
- Scope
- Owner
- Preconditions
- Step-by-step procedure
- Exceptions
- Safety/control requirements
- Related systems
- Related decisions
- Version
- Review date
- Change history

## 3. Store Opening

A store-opening checklist should eventually verify, as appropriate:

- Physical readiness
- Cleanliness
- Lighting
- Displays
- Demo devices
- Product security
- POS/system availability
- Internet/connectivity
- Cash/payment readiness
- Staff readiness
- Customer-facing information

Opening exceptions should be recorded and assigned when material.

## 4. Store Closing

A closing procedure should verify, as appropriate:

- Sales reconciliation
- Cash/payment reconciliation
- Inventory/security checks
- Device charging
- Demo equipment
- Store cleanliness
- Customer/service cases
- Systems shutdown or secure state
- Physical security

Critical discrepancies should be escalated rather than ignored.

## 5. Daily Management System

Store management should have a daily operating rhythm covering:

**Prepare → Trade → Review → Improve**

Daily management should focus on customer experience, sales execution, inventory, service, people, and exceptions.

## 6. Daily Huddle

A short team huddle may cover:

- Yesterday's results
- Today's priorities
- Product launches/promotions
- Stock constraints
- Customer/service appointments
- Important customer cases
- Staff assignments
- Safety/security reminders

The huddle should be brief and action-oriented.

## 7. Customer Greeting and Hospitality

Customers should be welcomed naturally and respectfully.

Staff should avoid aggressive selling or immediately overwhelming visitors with product information.

The objective is to understand the customer's need and create confidence.

## 8. Customer Discovery

Staff should learn, where relevant:

- Intended use
- Budget
- Existing device/ecosystem
- Required features
- Time horizon
- Financing preference
- Accessories/service needs

Questions should be useful rather than intrusive.

## 9. Product Demonstration

Products should be demonstrated in a clear, hands-on manner.

Staff should explain relevant benefits rather than reciting specifications without context.

Customers should be encouraged to experience products where safe and practical.

## 10. Recommendations

Recommendations should be based on customer needs, product suitability, availability, and approved commercial information.

Employees should not recommend unsuitable products solely because of higher margin or sales targets.

## 11. Pricing and Promotions

Staff must use approved current pricing and promotion information.

Unauthorized discounts, misleading comparisons, or unapproved promises are not permitted.

Promotional terms should be clear to customers.

## 12. Financing

Where EMI/financing is offered, staff should explain applicable terms accurately and avoid guaranteeing approval.

Customer financing information should be handled according to approved privacy and financial controls.

## 13. Checkout

Checkout should maintain accuracy across:

**Customer → Product/SKU → Price → Discount → Tax → Payment/Finance → Invoice → Inventory**

The process should minimize manual errors and maintain an audit trail.

## 14. Handover and Setup

The customer handover should be treated as part of the product experience.

Where purchased services include setup, staff may assist with:

- Device setup
- Data transfer
- Account configuration
- Basic education
- Accessories
- Warranty information

Customer credentials and sensitive information must be handled securely.

## 15. Warranty and Service Intake

Service cases should capture sufficient information to support accurate tracking.

Potential fields include:

- Customer identity
- Device/SKU
- Serial/IMEI
- Purchase reference
- Problem description
- Condition
- Date received
- Expected next step
- Status
- Responsible owner

Customers should receive realistic expectations rather than unsupported promises.

## 16. Returns and Exchanges

Returns and exchanges must follow approved policy and applicable requirements.

Exceptions should have documented authorization.

Returned products should be inspected and correctly classified before re-entering inventory.

## 17. Inventory Discipline

Store teams should maintain inventory accuracy through controlled processes for:

- Receiving
- Transfers
- Sales
- Reservations
- Returns
- Damages
- Adjustments
- Cycle counts

Inventory discrepancies should be investigated and not simply written off without authorization.

## 18. Cycle Counting

The store should use risk-based cycle counting rather than relying only on infrequent full stock counts.

Higher-value and higher-risk items should receive appropriate attention.

## 19. Demo Device Management

Demo devices should have controlled ownership and status.

Records should identify, where appropriate:

- Device
- Serial/IMEI
- Demo location
- Condition
- Assigned accessories
- Responsible owner

Demo devices should be protected while remaining easy for customers to experience.

## 20. Store Cleanliness and Visual Standards

The store should maintain the approved premium environment throughout operating hours.

Checks should cover:

- Floors
- Surfaces
- Displays
- Cables
- Product presentation
- Lighting
- Signage
- Customer areas

Visual standards should be documented separately under the Store Design and Brand Systems.

## 21. Security

Store operations should include controls for:

- High-value products
- Cash/payment systems
- Customer information
- Keys/access
- Staff credentials
- Closing/opening
- Suspicious activity

Security procedures should protect people first and assets second.

## 22. Incident Handling

Store incidents should follow:

**Identify → Stabilize → Escalate → Record → Resolve → Learn**

Material incidents should enter the company's incident-management system under DEC-0036.

## 23. Customer Complaints

Complaints should be handled calmly and respectfully.

Staff should:

1. Listen.
2. Clarify the problem.
3. Take ownership where appropriate.
4. Explain available options.
5. Escalate when necessary.
6. Record material cases.
7. Follow up.

Complaint trends should feed continuous improvement.

## 24. Staff Scheduling

Scheduling should balance:

- Expected customer traffic
- Product launches
- Appointments
- Service demand
- Employee capability
- Legal/HR requirements

Schedules should not create avoidable service gaps.

## 25. Training Readiness

Employees should be prepared before being responsible for customer-facing processes.

Training should cover:

- Product knowledge
- Customer experience
- POS/processes
- Inventory
- Financing
- Privacy/security
- Service
- Escalation
- Brand standards

Training and certification should connect to DEC-0032.

## 26. Store Metrics

Management should eventually monitor a balanced set of metrics including:

### Customer
- Customer satisfaction
- Complaint rate
- Service turnaround
- Repeat visits

### Commercial
- Sales
- Gross margin
- Conversion where measurable
- Average transaction value
- Category mix

### Operations
- Inventory accuracy
- Stockouts
- Shrinkage
- Service backlog
- Opening/closing compliance

### People
- Training completion
- Productivity
- Attendance
- Employee development

Metrics should support decisions rather than encourage unhealthy behavior.

## 27. Daily Review

Store management should review major exceptions and results at the end of the operating period.

Questions should include:

- What went well?
- What failed?
- What affected customers?
- What inventory issues occurred?
- What needs escalation?
- What should change tomorrow?

## 28. Weekly Operating Review

A weekly review should consolidate:

- Sales
- Margin
- Inventory
- Service
- Customer feedback
- Staff issues
- Marketing activity
- Supplier issues
- Operational incidents

Recurring problems should be assigned owners and deadlines.

## 29. SOP Improvement

SOPs are living documents.

When a process repeatedly fails, management should ask whether the problem is:

- Training
- Process design
- System design
- Staffing
- Supplier performance
- Policy
- Measurement

The answer should drive the corrective action.

## 30. Exception Management

Not every unusual case requires a new rule.

Exceptions should be classified as:

- One-time exception
- Known exception
- Repeated problem
- Systemic issue

Repeated exceptions should trigger process review.

## 31. Store + CRM

Customer interactions that create legitimate follow-up should be captured in the CRM with appropriate consent and access controls.

Staff should avoid maintaining critical customer information only in personal devices or informal chat threads.

## 32. Store + AI Concierge

The AI Concierge should complement staff rather than replace human hospitality.

Staff should be able to intervene when:

- Customer needs are complex
- AI information is uncertain
- A purchase decision is high-value
- Service escalation is required
- The customer requests a person

AI interactions should remain within approved knowledge and access boundaries.

## 33. Store + Finance

Daily operations should support the financial controls in DEC-0034 through accurate:

- Sales records
- Payment records
- Refunds
- Discounts
- Cash handling
- Reconciliation

## 34. Store + Procurement

Store teams should provide procurement with structured feedback about:

- Stockouts
- Customer demand
- Product defects
- Slow-moving inventory
- Supplier issues

Store requests should not bypass procurement controls.

## 35. Store + Marketing

Store execution must match published marketing.

Before important campaigns, management should verify:

- Stock
- Price
- Promotion terms
- Staff knowledge
- Display readiness
- Service capacity

## 36. Store + Technology

Critical store systems should have documented fallback procedures for outages.

Employees should know what to do when:

- Internet fails
- POS is unavailable
- Payment systems fail
- Inventory systems are unavailable
- AI Concierge is unavailable

Fallback processes should protect transaction accuracy and customer trust.

## 37. Store Manager Responsibilities

The Store Manager should be accountable for the overall store operating standard, including:

- Customer experience
- People readiness
- Daily execution
- Inventory discipline
- Sales performance
- Financial controls
- Security
- Escalations
- SOP compliance
- Continuous improvement

Specific authority should be defined through the delegation framework.

## 38. Store Documentation

Each store should eventually have a controlled operational pack containing:

- Store profile
- Opening/closing checklist
- Daily checklist
- Emergency contacts
- SOP index
- Staff roles
- Inventory procedures
- Service procedures
- Security procedures
- Escalation matrix
- Vendor contacts
- Technology fallback procedures

## 39. Risks

Major store-operation risks include:

- Poor customer experience
- Inventory loss
- Incorrect pricing
- Payment errors
- Unauthorized discounts
- Security incidents
- Service delays
- Staff knowledge gaps
- System outages
- SOP non-compliance
- Operational inconsistency

Controls should be practical and proportionate.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Map the customer and store operating journeys.
2. Identify critical recurring processes.
3. Create the SOP index and templates.
4. Build opening/closing procedures.
5. Document sales and checkout processes.
6. Document inventory and receiving procedures.
7. Document service/returns procedures.
8. Establish daily and weekly management routines.
9. Train and certify employees.
10. Measure exceptions and customer outcomes.
11. Improve SOPs continuously.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **store operations, SOP and daily management direction**. It does not define every final SOP, checklist, staff schedule, approval threshold, security procedure, or store layout.

Those should be developed as controlled operating documents and specialized decisions as the flagship store requirements become concrete.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Store operations should evolve as the flagship store is tested and as additional locations are opened.

Material changes should document the rationale, customer/operational impact, affected SOPs, and related decision IDs.
