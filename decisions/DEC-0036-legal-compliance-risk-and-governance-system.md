# DEC-0036 — Legal, Compliance, Risk & Governance System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0036  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Legal, Compliance, Risk and Governance System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [Founding Charter](../docs/founders-playbook/FOUNDING-CHARTER.md), [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0031, DEC-0032, DEC-0034, DEC-0035

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat **legal compliance, risk management, internal controls, and governance as operating infrastructure**, not as paperwork performed only after a problem occurs.

The company will build a practical governance system that protects customers, employees, owners, suppliers, assets, data, and the long-term brand.

> **Do the right thing. Know the rules. Control the risks. Document the decisions.**

## 1. Governance Philosophy

Governance should enable responsible growth without creating unnecessary bureaucracy.

The company should prioritize:

- Integrity
- Accountability
- Compliance
- Transparency
- Appropriate authorization
- Traceability
- Risk awareness
- Customer protection
- Data protection
- Business continuity

## 2. Legal Structure

The company's final legal structure, registrations, ownership arrangements, and statutory obligations should be established and maintained with qualified Nepalese legal/accounting professionals.

The repository may document decisions and governance requirements but does not replace professional legal advice or statutory records.

## 3. Compliance Register

KAMLESH & SONS should maintain a controlled compliance register identifying applicable obligations.

Potential categories include:

- Corporate/statutory requirements
- Tax and VAT
- Employment
- Consumer protection
- Product requirements
- Advertising
- Data/privacy
- Electronic transactions
- Financial/payment activities
- Health and safety
- Store/building requirements
- Supplier/brand agreements

Specific requirements should be verified against current Nepalese law and documented with an owner and review date.

## 4. Licenses and Registrations

Required licenses, registrations, certificates, and permits should have:

- Responsible owner
- Issuing authority
- Effective date
- Expiry/renewal date where applicable
- Supporting documentation
- Renewal status

The company should not rely on memory for critical compliance dates.

## 5. Consumer Protection

Customer-facing practices should be designed to support fair dealing and transparent communication.

This includes, where applicable:

- Accurate product descriptions
- Clear pricing
- Clear financing terms
- Honest promotions
- Appropriate warranty information
- Documented returns/exchange rules
- Complaint handling

Employees should not promise legal rights, warranties, refunds, or exceptions beyond approved policy.

## 6. Product Authenticity and Supplier Compliance

KAMLESH & SONS should maintain appropriate controls to reduce risks associated with counterfeit, unauthorized, damaged, or improperly sourced products.

Supplier relationships should include appropriate documentation for:

- Product source
- Purchase records
- Warranty arrangements
- Commercial terms
- Returns
- Brand authorization where relevant

Specific authorized-retailer relationships require separate commercial and legal verification.

## 7. Brand and Intellectual Property

The company should protect its own intellectual property and respect third-party rights.

Relevant areas include:

- Company name
- Logo
- Brand assets
- Website content
- Photography
- Software
- Marketing materials
- Product trademarks
- Licensed content

Use of third-party intellectual property should have appropriate permission or licensing where required.

## 8. Contracts

Material relationships should use appropriate written agreements where practical.

Potential contract categories include:

- Suppliers
- Banks/financing partners
- Employees
- Landlords
- Service providers
- Technology vendors
- Marketing partners
- Corporate customers
- Logistics providers

Contracts should identify responsibilities, payment terms, termination, liability, confidentiality, data handling, and other material risks as appropriate.

## 9. Contract Register

The company should maintain a controlled contract register containing, as appropriate:

- Contract ID
- Parties
- Purpose
- Owner
- Effective date
- Renewal/expiry date
- Key commercial terms
- Termination provisions
- Data/security obligations
- Document location

Access should be restricted according to sensitivity.

## 10. Employment Compliance

Employment practices should comply with applicable Nepalese requirements and approved company policies.

Relevant areas may include:

- Employment agreements
- Compensation
- Working hours
- Leave
- Workplace conduct
- Health and safety
- Termination
- Employee records

Detailed employment policy should be developed separately with professional guidance.

## 11. Data Protection and Privacy

Customer and employee data should be handled according to applicable legal requirements and company privacy policy.

The company should define:

- What data is collected
- Why it is collected
- Legal/consent basis where applicable
- Who can access it
- How long it is retained
- How it is protected
- How customers can exercise applicable rights

Privacy requirements must be reflected in system design under DEC-0035.

## 12. AI Governance

AI systems require additional governance because they may process customer, employee, product, financial, and internal company information.

The company should define:

- Approved AI systems
- Permitted data
- Restricted data
- Human oversight
- Accuracy requirements
- Auditability
- Incident reporting
- Model/vendor review
- Access controls

Customer AI and Internal AI must remain appropriately separated.

## 13. AI Accountability

AI must not be treated as an independent legal or managerial authority.

Human owners remain accountable for important customer, financial, employment, security, and operational decisions.

AI-generated information should be verified when the consequences of error are material.

## 14. Risk Management

The company should maintain a structured risk register.

Each material risk should identify, where appropriate:

- Risk ID
- Description
- Cause
- Impact
- Likelihood
- Severity
- Owner
- Existing controls
- Mitigation
- Review date
- Status

## 15. Risk Categories

The risk system should consider at least:

- Strategic
- Financial
- Operational
- Legal/compliance
- Cybersecurity
- Privacy
- Supplier
- Inventory
- Customer experience
- Reputation
- People
- Technology
- Business continuity

## 16. Risk Appetite

Management should eventually define acceptable levels of risk for major categories.

Not every risk can be eliminated.

The objective is to understand and deliberately manage risk rather than pretend that zero risk is achievable.

## 17. Internal Controls

Critical processes should include appropriate controls such as:

- Authorization
- Verification
- Reconciliation
- Separation of duties
- Access control
- Audit trails
- Exception review

Controls should be proportionate to the value and risk of the activity.

## 18. Delegation of Authority

The company should establish clear approval limits for material decisions.

Potential categories include:

- Purchases
- Discounts
- Refunds
- Payments
- Hiring
- Contracts
- Capital expenditure
- Inventory adjustments
- Customer exceptions

Specific monetary thresholds should be approved separately after financial and operational analysis.

## 19. Incident Management

Material incidents should follow a defined process:

**Detect → Contain → Assess → Escalate → Resolve → Document → Learn**

Incident categories may include:

- Data breach
- Cybersecurity event
- Fraud
- Financial discrepancy
- Customer safety issue
- Product authenticity issue
- Major system outage
- Serious employee conduct issue
- Reputation crisis

## 20. Whistleblowing and Ethical Reporting

Employees should have a safe mechanism to report serious concerns such as:

- Fraud
- Theft
- Corruption
- Harassment
- Safety violations
- Privacy violations
- Deliberate customer deception
- Serious policy breaches

Good-faith reporting should be protected from retaliation consistent with applicable law and company policy.

## 21. Records Management

Important business records should have defined ownership, retention, and access requirements.

Potential records include:

- Contracts
- Invoices
- Financial records
- Customer records
- Employee records
- Compliance documents
- Incident records
- Decisions
- Policies
- SOPs

GitHub should remain the source of truth for documented company decisions and operating knowledge, while legally/statutorily authoritative records remain in their required systems.

## 22. Auditability

Material transactions and decisions should be traceable to appropriate records.

The company should be able to answer:

- What happened?
- Who authorized it?
- When did it happen?
- What information supported it?
- What changed?
- What was the outcome?

This principle should influence both process and technology design.

## 23. Insurance and Asset Protection

Management should evaluate appropriate insurance and risk-transfer mechanisms as the business grows.

Potential areas may include:

- Property
- Inventory
- Liability
- Business interruption
- Cyber risk
- Employee-related risks

Specific insurance requirements require professional risk assessment.

## 24. Business Continuity

Critical operations should have continuity plans for events such as:

- Major power/network failure
- Store damage
- Cyber incident
- Critical supplier disruption
- Payment system outage
- Natural disaster
- Key-person unavailability

Plans should identify essential functions, alternatives, owners, and recovery priorities.

## 25. Crisis Communication

Material incidents should have a controlled communication process.

Only authorized people should speak publicly on behalf of the company regarding significant legal, security, financial, or reputational incidents.

Communication should be truthful, timely, and appropriately limited to verified information.

## 26. Governance of Major Decisions

Major decisions should be recorded through the Decision Log.

A significant decision should capture:

- Decision ID
- Context
- Options considered
- Decision
- Rationale
- Risks
- Owner
- Effective date
- Review date
- Related documents

This keeps the company's institutional memory traceable.

## 27. Policy Lifecycle

Policies should follow a controlled lifecycle:

**Draft → Review → Approve → Publish → Train → Monitor → Review → Supersede/Retire**

Policies should have owners and review dates.

## 28. Legal and Compliance Training

Employees whose roles create material compliance exposure should receive appropriate training.

Training may cover:

- Customer rights
- Privacy
- Security
- Financial controls
- Anti-fraud practices
- Workplace conduct
- Product handling
- Approved communications

## 29. Governance + Technology

Technology should support governance through:

- Access controls
- Audit logs
- Approval workflows
- Version history
- Automated reminders
- Compliance dashboards
- Secure document storage

Manual controls should be replaced with reliable automation when justified.

## 30. Governance + AI

Internal AI may assist with:

- Policy discovery
- Compliance checklists
- Risk summaries
- Decision retrieval
- Incident documentation
- Control monitoring

AI should not provide definitive legal advice unless the company has explicitly established an appropriate professional/legal review workflow.

## 31. Risk Review Cadence

The company should eventually conduct:

- Operational risk checks
- Monthly/periodic control reviews
- Quarterly risk review
- Annual governance review
- Event-driven reviews after major incidents

The cadence should scale with business complexity.

## 32. Risks of Governance Itself

Governance can fail through:

- Excessive bureaucracy
- Unclear ownership
- Outdated policies
- False compliance
- Unused controls
- Poor documentation
- Lack of employee understanding
- Treating checklists as substitutes for judgment

The objective is effective governance, not paperwork volume.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Identify legal and regulatory obligations.
2. Establish compliance register.
3. Establish contract register.
4. Define key internal controls.
5. Create delegation-of-authority framework.
6. Establish risk register.
7. Define incident-management process.
8. Establish privacy and AI governance.
9. Develop business continuity plans.
10. Establish governance review cadence.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **legal, compliance, risk and governance direction**. It does not provide legal advice or finalize specific statutory registrations, tax interpretations, employment policies, contracts, insurance policies, or regulatory procedures.

Those matters require verification with qualified professionals and current Nepalese requirements before implementation.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Governance should mature as KAMLESH & SONS adds employees, stores, financial complexity, customer data, technology systems, and external partners.

Material changes should document the rationale, legal/compliance impact, risk implications, affected controls, and related decision IDs.
