# DEC-0045 — Store Operations, SOP & Daily Execution System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0045  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Store Operations, SOP and Daily Execution System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0038, DEC-0039, DEC-0041, DEC-0042, DEC-0043, DEC-0044

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will operate stores through a documented, repeatable and measurable operating system.

The goal is not to make employees robotic. The goal is to make the **important things reliably excellent** while leaving room for human judgment and hospitality.

> **Premium experience requires operational consistency.**

## 1. Store Operating Philosophy

Every store should be:

- Clean
- Ready
- Secure
- Fully operational
- Product knowledgeable
- Customer focused
- Calm
- Consistent

The customer should experience the same fundamental KAMLESH & SONS standards regardless of employee or location.

## 2. Standard Operating Procedures

Important recurring activities should have documented SOPs.

An SOP should normally contain:

- Purpose
- Scope
- Owner
- Preconditions
- Steps
- Quality standard
- Exceptions
- Escalation path
- Required records
- Review date

## 3. Daily Operating Rhythm

A flagship store should eventually operate through a daily rhythm:

**Open → Prepare → Trade → Review → Close → Reconcile → Secure**

The exact checklist should be adapted to store format and staffing.

## 4. Opening Procedure

Before opening, authorized staff should verify, as appropriate:

- Store cleanliness
- Lighting
- Music/ambient environment where applicable
- Displays
- Demo devices
- Product availability
- POS systems
- Network connectivity
- Payment systems
- Security systems
- Service area readiness
- Staff readiness

Customer-facing areas should be ready before doors open.

## 5. Store Presentation

Store presentation should follow the Brand System and Store Design standards.

Staff should continuously prevent:

- Clutter
- Empty display areas
- Unorganized accessories
- Damaged signage
- Unclean surfaces
- Uncharged demo devices

## 6. Demo Device Readiness

Every live demo should be:

- Functional
- Charged
- Connected where appropriate
- Updated according to approved process
- Clean
- Configured for demonstration
- Free of inappropriate customer data

## 7. Customer Greeting

Customers should be acknowledged promptly and respectfully without aggressive sales pressure.

The goal is to create comfort and invite discovery.

## 8. Customer Discovery

Staff should understand the customer's need before recommending a product.

Useful discovery areas may include:

- Current device
- Primary use
- Budget
- Preferences
- Work/study needs
- Camera needs
- Gaming needs
- Ecosystem preferences
- Storage requirements
- Financing needs
- Accessories

## 9. Product Demonstration

Whenever practical, customers should be allowed to experience products directly.

Demonstrations should focus on relevant benefits rather than overwhelming customers with specifications.

## 10. Recommendation Standard

Recommendations should be:

- Honest
- Relevant
- Understandable
- Based on customer needs
- Transparent about trade-offs

Employees should not recommend a more expensive product solely because it produces more revenue.

## 11. Comparison Standard

When customers compare products, staff should clearly explain:

- Important differences
- Similarities
- Practical implications
- Price difference
- Best-fit customer

The goal is confident decision-making, not confusion.

## 12. Pricing Communication

Prices should be communicated accurately.

If taxes, financing fees, promotions or other conditions affect the final price, they should be explained clearly.

## 13. EMI / Financing Process

Where financing is offered, employees should follow approved financing procedures and clearly distinguish:

- Product price
- Financing terms
- Fees
- Eligibility
- Documentation
- Customer obligations

Employees must not promise approval when approval is controlled by a financing provider.

## 14. Checkout Procedure

Checkout should verify, as appropriate:

- Customer/product selection
- Price
- Applicable discount
- Payment method
- Serial/IMEI
- Invoice information
- Warranty information
- Required customer consent

## 15. Invoice and Documentation

Customers should receive appropriate transaction documentation consistent with company policy and applicable requirements.

Records should connect the sale to the product and payment.

## 16. Customer Handover

Before a customer leaves with a new device, staff should offer appropriate assistance with:

- Basic setup
- Account sign-in
- Data transfer where purchased/authorized
- Device protection
- Accessories
- Warranty explanation
- Care guidance

The goal is a confident handover rather than a rushed transaction.

## 17. Device Setup

Setup services should follow a documented process that protects customer privacy and device integrity.

Employees should never retain customer credentials.

## 18. Data Transfer

Data-transfer services must follow approved privacy and security procedures.

Customers should remain informed about what is being transferred and what limitations may exist.

## 19. Screen Protector / Installation Services

Installation services should use approved products, tools and procedures.

Any damage or exception should be documented and escalated according to policy.

## 20. Service Intake

Customer devices entering service should receive a controlled intake record covering, as appropriate:

- Customer identity
- Device
- Serial/IMEI
- Condition
- Reported issue
- Accessories received
- Authorization
- Expected next step

## 21. Service Handover

Before returning a serviced device, staff should verify:

- Correct customer
- Correct device
- Service status
- Required documentation
- Physical condition
- Payment status where applicable

## 22. Returns and Exchanges

Returns and exchanges should follow approved policy.

Employees should not create exceptions informally when the exception creates financial, legal or customer-trust risk.

## 23. Trade-In Procedure

Trade-ins should use a documented assessment process.

The process should cover:

- Device identity
- Condition
- Functionality
- Ownership/eligibility checks where applicable
- Valuation
- Customer approval
- Transaction record
- Final inventory disposition

## 24. Customer Complaints

Complaints should be handled calmly and respectfully.

The first objective is to understand the issue, then determine the appropriate resolution within policy.

Serious or unresolved issues should escalate to store leadership.

## 25. Escalation Matrix

Stores should maintain clear escalation paths for:

- Customer complaints
- Pricing exceptions
- Security incidents
- Suspected fraud
- Inventory discrepancies
- Service disputes
- Payment issues
- Staff conduct issues
- Legal/compliance concerns

## 26. Store Security

Store security should protect:

- Customers
- Employees
- Inventory
- Cash
- Devices
- Company information
- Customer information

Security controls should align with DEC-0042.

## 27. High-Value Product Handling

High-value products should receive appropriate controls for:

- Storage
- Display
- Movement
- Counting
- Sale
- Return
- Service

## 28. Cash Handling

Where cash is accepted, employees should follow controlled cash procedures covering:

- Starting cash
- Transaction handling
- Change
- Cash drops where applicable
- End-of-day count
- Reconciliation
- Variance escalation

## 29. Payment Handling

Employees should use approved payment systems and must not request or record sensitive payment credentials outside approved processes.

## 30. End-of-Day Procedure

Closing should include, as appropriate:

- Sales reconciliation
- Payment reconciliation
- Cash count
- Inventory exceptions
- Service status review
- Store cleanup
- Demo readiness for next day
- Security check
- System shutdown/lock procedures

## 31. Daily Huddle

A short daily team huddle should align staff around:

- Today's priorities
- Product focus
- Promotions
- Inventory constraints
- Service workload
- Customer experience focus
- Staffing
- Risks or alerts

The huddle should be brief and action-oriented.

## 32. Daily Management Board

The store may maintain a visible operational board or digital equivalent containing key priorities and metrics.

Sensitive information should not be displayed publicly.

## 33. Shift Handover

When responsibility moves between employees or shifts, important open items should be handed over clearly.

Potential items include:

- Customer follow-ups
- Service cases
- Reservations
- Pending payments
- Inventory discrepancies
- Supplier deliveries
- Escalations

## 34. Staff Conduct on Floor

Employees should remain professional and attentive.

Avoid:

- Excessive personal phone use
- Internal arguments in customer areas
- Ignoring customers
- Aggressive selling
- Negative comments about competitors
- Unprofessional language

## 35. Customer Privacy on Floor

Customer information should not be unnecessarily discussed where other customers can hear it.

Printed records and screens should be positioned and handled appropriately.

## 36. Cleanliness Standard

Cleanliness is part of the premium experience.

The store should have defined standards for:

- Floors
- Counters
- Displays
- Devices
- Glass
- Staff areas
- Service areas
- Restrooms where applicable

## 37. Maintenance

Store defects should be logged and tracked.

Examples include:

- Lighting failures
- Display damage
- Network problems
- POS failures
- Air-conditioning issues
- Furniture damage

## 38. Technology Outage Procedure

If a critical system fails, employees should follow a documented fallback procedure.

Potential outages include:

- POS
- Internet
- Payment system
- CRM
- Inventory system
- Customer Portal

Manual fallback processes should preserve enough information for later reconciliation.

## 39. Emergency Procedures

Stores should maintain procedures for relevant emergencies, including:

- Fire
- Medical emergency
- Security threat
- Power failure
- Major technology outage
- Natural hazards relevant to location

Employees should know evacuation and escalation procedures.

## 40. Opening/Closing Accountability

Opening and closing tasks should have clear ownership.

Critical security tasks should not rely on informal memory alone.

## 41. SOP Governance

Every important SOP should have:

- Document ID
- Owner
- Version
- Effective date
- Review date
- Change history

Obsolete SOPs should not remain mixed with current operating instructions.

## 42. SOP Improvement

Employees should be encouraged to identify process problems.

Improvement suggestions should be captured in K&S OS and evaluated rather than disappearing into informal conversations.

## 43. Operational Audits

Management should periodically review store execution against key SOPs.

Audits should focus on improving reliability rather than creating a culture of fear.

## 44. Store KPI System

Management may eventually monitor:

- Sales
- Gross margin
- Conversion
- Average transaction value
- Customer satisfaction
- Service turnaround
- Inventory accuracy
- Shrinkage
- Attach rate
- Financing adoption
- Return rate
- Complaint resolution

Metrics should be interpreted together rather than optimized independently.

## 45. Customer Experience Audit

Store leadership should periodically assess whether customers actually experience the intended KAMLESH & SONS philosophy:

- Calm
- Helpful
- Professional
- Respectful
- Interactive
- Trustworthy

## 46. Operational Excellence

Operational excellence means reducing avoidable friction.

Examples include:

- Finding products quickly
- Accurate prices
- Fast checkout
- Clear service updates
- Reliable inventory information
- Consistent follow-up

## 47. Store Manager Operating System

The Store Manager should maintain visibility over:

- People
- Sales
- Inventory
- Service
- Customer issues
- Store condition
- Security
- Daily priorities

## 48. Founder / Leadership Visibility

Leadership should be able to understand store health without constantly interrupting store teams.

Future K&S OS dashboards should provide summarized operational visibility.

## 49. Internal AI Assistance

Internal AI may eventually help employees retrieve:

- SOPs
- Product information
- Policies
- Escalation procedures
- Training material
- Inventory information

AI must respect role-based access and the security boundaries in DEC-0042.

## 50. Continuous Improvement

Every significant operational problem should create an opportunity to ask:

**What happened? → Why? → What changes? → Who owns it? → How do we verify improvement?**

## 51. Risks

Major operational risks include:

- Inconsistent customer experience
- SOP non-compliance
- Inventory errors
- Cash/payment errors
- Security incidents
- Poor handovers
- Service delays
- Staff dependency
- Technology outages
- Weak escalation
- Store deterioration

## Implementation Sequence

1. Identify critical store workflows.
2. Document opening and closing checklists.
3. Document customer journey standards.
4. Establish checkout, payment and handover procedures.
5. Establish service, return and trade-in SOPs.
6. Establish security and emergency procedures.
7. Establish daily huddles and shift handovers.
8. Establish operational KPIs.
9. Begin periodic operational audits.
10. Capture improvement ideas in K&S OS.
11. Connect Internal AI to approved SOP and knowledge sources.
12. Replicate the operating model for future stores.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **store operations and daily execution framework**. Individual SOPs, checklists, emergency plans and store-specific procedures should be created as controlled documents under this framework.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material operating-model changes should document rationale, customer impact, operational risk, affected SOPs and related decision IDs.
