# DEC-0039 — Customer Service, Warranty & After-Sales Experience System

**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Customer Service, Warranty and After-Sales Experience System

**Version:** 0.1.0

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat the customer relationship as continuing **after the sale**. Warranty support, setup, service, repairs, returns, communication and follow-up are part of the premium customer experience rather than separate administrative functions.

> **The sale begins the relationship; great after-sales service earns the next visit.**

## 1. After-Sales Philosophy

After-sales service should be:

- Respectful
- Clear
- Convenient
- Traceable
- Timely
- Honest
- Technically competent
- Customer-first

Customers should understand what is happening with their device and what they can reasonably expect next.

## 2. Service Lifecycle

The standard service journey should eventually follow:

**Request → Intake → Diagnose → Approve → Repair/Resolve → Quality Check → Notify → Handover → Follow-up → Close**

The exact flow may differ by product, warranty provider and service type.

## 3. Service Intake

Every material service case should capture sufficient information, including where appropriate:

- Customer identity
- Contact information
- Device/product
- Serial/IMEI
- Purchase reference
- Warranty status
- Reported issue
- Device condition
- Accessories received
- Date/time received
- Assigned owner
- Current status

Sensitive customer information should be collected only when necessary.

## 4. Digital Service Record

The long-term goal is a digital service record connected to the customer's account where appropriate.

Customers may eventually see:

- Service request
- Current status
- Diagnostic result where appropriate
- Estimated next step
- Appointment
- Completion notice
- Service history

Access must be limited to the authenticated customer and authorized employees.

## 5. Warranty Verification

Warranty claims should be verified using appropriate evidence, which may include:

- Invoice
- Serial/IMEI
- Purchase date
- Warranty terms
- Manufacturer/service-provider records
- Device condition

Staff should never guarantee warranty approval when the decision belongs to a manufacturer or authorized service provider.

## 6. Warranty Communication

Customers should receive clear explanations of:

- What is covered
- What is not covered
- Who makes the final warranty determination
- Expected next steps
- Expected timelines where known
- Any customer-approved charges

If the outcome is uncertain, staff should say so rather than create false certainty.

## 7. Non-Warranty Service

For paid service, customers should receive appropriate information about:

- Diagnosis
- Estimated cost
- Scope of work
- Approval requirement
- Expected timeline
- Parts where relevant
- Warranty on the service where applicable

Work beyond approved scope should require appropriate customer authorization.

## 8. Repair Authorization

Material repair work should not proceed beyond approved scope without appropriate authorization.

The authorization should be traceable to the customer and service case.

## 9. Device Condition at Intake

The store/service team should document relevant condition information to reduce disputes.

For appropriate devices, this may include:

- Physical condition
- Existing damage
- Accessories received
- Serial/IMEI
- Functional observations
- Customer-reported symptoms

Images may be used where justified and with appropriate privacy controls.

## 10. Data and Privacy During Service

Customer devices may contain highly sensitive information.

Employees must not access customer content unnecessarily.

Service procedures should minimize exposure to:

- Photos
- Messages
- Contacts
- Passwords
- Financial information
- Authentication credentials

Customers should be advised of appropriate backup and account-security considerations where relevant.

## 11. Password and Credential Handling

Customer passwords, OTPs, recovery codes and other authentication secrets should not be stored as ordinary service records.

Where technical assistance requires authentication, the process should use the minimum necessary access and approved safeguards.

## 12. Data Transfer and Setup

Where KAMLESH & SONS provides setup/data-transfer services, the process should prioritize:

- Customer consent
- Data privacy
- Backup awareness
- Transfer verification
- Account security
- Clear completion confirmation

Employees should not assume responsibility for data loss beyond the approved service scope.

## 13. Repair Tracking

Each service case should have a visible status such as:

- Received
- Diagnosing
- Awaiting approval
- Awaiting parts
- Under repair
- Awaiting service-provider decision
- Quality check
- Ready for collection
- Completed
- Closed

Customers should receive meaningful updates when the status materially changes.

## 14. Service-Level Expectations

The company should establish realistic service targets by category.

Targets may differ for:

- In-store assistance
- Warranty intake
- Manufacturer repair
- Parts-dependent repairs
- Data transfer
- Trade-in evaluation

Targets should be measured and revised using actual performance.

## 15. Delays

When a service case is delayed, the customer should be informed rather than left to repeatedly chase the store.

Communication should explain the known reason and next expected update without inventing information.

## 16. Escalation

Cases should have clear escalation paths for:

- Repeated delays
- Warranty disputes
- High-value devices
- Customer vulnerability
- Safety concerns
- Lost/damaged devices
- Serious complaints
- Suspected fraud
- Data/privacy incidents

Escalation authority should be documented separately.

## 17. Service Quality Check

Before handover, appropriate devices should undergo a quality check covering the agreed repair/service scope.

The objective is to prevent avoidable repeat visits caused by incomplete work.

## 18. Customer Handover

The handover should include, where relevant:

- Confirmation of completed work
- Device condition
- Accessories returned
- Charges/invoice
- Warranty/service information
- Basic usage guidance
- Customer confirmation

## 19. Service Handover Security

Before returning a device, staff should verify the appropriate customer identity or authorization.

The company should avoid releasing customer devices to unauthorized people.

## 20. Returns and Exchanges

Returns and exchanges should follow approved policy and applicable requirements.

The process should distinguish among:

- Change-of-mind cases
- Defective products
- Warranty cases
- Incorrect product supplied
- Transit/receiving damage
- Authorized exceptions

Each category may require a different operational response.

## 21. Refund Controls

Refunds should be processed through controlled financial workflows.

Staff should not promise an immediate refund when processing depends on payment providers, banks or other systems.

Refund records should reconcile with the original transaction.

## 22. Trade-In Support

Trade-in services should have a documented evaluation and approval process.

Where appropriate, the record should include:

- Device identity
- Condition
- Evaluation result
- Offered value
- Customer acceptance
- Data-erasure confirmation
- Final disposition

Trade-in values should not be represented as guaranteed until the required evaluation is complete.

## 23. Customer Communication

Communication channels may eventually include:

- In-store
- Phone
- SMS
- WhatsApp or other approved messaging
- Email
- Customer portal
- AI-assisted notifications

The company should use approved channels and maintain appropriate records for material service communications.

## 24. Proactive Updates

The company should prefer proactive communication over forcing customers to request updates repeatedly.

Examples include:

- Service received
- Diagnosis completed
- Approval required
- Part delayed
- Repair completed
- Ready for collection
- Follow-up reminder

## 25. Customer Support Knowledge Base

Support teams should have access to current approved knowledge covering:

- Warranty policies
- Product support
- Service processes
- Returns
- Escalations
- Contact information
- Approved customer communications

The Internal AI may assist with knowledge retrieval under DEC-0035 and DEC-0036.

## 26. AI in After-Sales

Customer AI may assist customers with:

- Service status
- Basic troubleshooting
- Appointment information
- Warranty-information retrieval
- Product guidance
- Next-step explanations

AI should not invent service status, warranty decisions, repair estimates, or policy exceptions.

## 27. Human Escalation

Customers should be able to reach a human when AI cannot adequately resolve the issue.

High-impact or ambiguous cases should be routed to trained employees.

## 28. Customer Feedback

After meaningful service interactions, the company may request feedback.

Feedback should measure:

- Ease
- Courtesy
- Clarity
- Timeliness
- Resolution
- Overall satisfaction

Feedback should be used for improvement rather than only marketing.

## 29. Service Metrics

Management should eventually track:

- Number of service cases
- First-contact resolution where applicable
- Turnaround time
- Aging cases
- Repeat repairs
- Warranty approval rate
- Customer satisfaction
- Complaint rate
- Parts delays
- Escalations

Metrics should be segmented by product/service category where useful.

## 30. Repeat Problems

Repeated failures should trigger root-cause analysis.

Possible causes include:

- Product issue
- Supplier issue
- Training gap
- Process failure
- Parts availability
- Incorrect diagnosis
- Communication failure

The goal is to remove recurring causes rather than repeatedly resolve symptoms.

## 31. Service Inventory and Parts

Where the company holds service parts or accessories, those items should be controlled through inventory procedures.

Service parts should not disappear from inventory simply because they were used during repair.

## 32. Third-Party Service Providers

Where repair is performed by an external provider, the relationship should define appropriate:

- Responsibilities
- Handover records
- Tracking
- Data/security expectations
- Turnaround expectations
- Warranty responsibility
- Escalation
- Liability

Material providers should be evaluated under DEC-0037 and DEC-0036.

## 33. Lost or Damaged Customer Devices

Any incident involving a customer device while under company control should be escalated and documented immediately.

The response should prioritize:

1. Customer safety/trust.
2. Device protection.
3. Accurate facts.
4. Incident documentation.
5. Appropriate management/legal escalation.

## 34. Service Fraud Controls

Controls should reduce risks such as:

- False warranty claims
- Device substitution
- Unauthorized parts
- Fake receipts
- Manipulated service records
- Unauthorized refunds

Controls should be proportionate to risk and should not make legitimate customers feel suspected by default.

## 35. After-Sales + CRM

Material service interactions should be linked to the customer record where appropriate.

This creates a long-term customer history that can support better service without collecting unnecessary information.

## 36. After-Sales + Customer Portal

The future portal should allow customers to track service and warranty information without needing to call the store for every update.

## 37. After-Sales + Finance

Service charges, refunds, deposits and warranty-related financial transactions should be recorded and reconciled through approved financial controls.

## 38. After-Sales + Brand Experience

The quality of after-sales service should match the premium positioning of KAMLESH & SONS.

A customer should not receive an excellent purchase experience followed by confusing, dismissive or untraceable service.

## 39. Risks

Major after-sales risks include:

- Warranty misunderstanding
- Service delays
- Device loss/damage
- Data/privacy exposure
- Incorrect diagnosis
- Poor communication
- Unauthorized refunds
- Fraud
- Repeat failures
- Third-party service failure
- Customer dissatisfaction

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Define service categories.
2. Create service intake and tracking records.
3. Establish warranty-verification procedures.
4. Document returns/exchange process.
5. Establish repair authorization and quality checks.
6. Establish customer communication standards.
7. Define escalation paths.
8. Integrate service with CRM and customer identity.
9. Integrate financial reconciliation.
10. Launch service metrics and feedback.
11. Introduce customer-portal tracking.
12. Add controlled AI assistance.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **customer service, warranty and after-sales direction**. It does not finalize manufacturer warranty policies, repair pricing, service-provider contracts, statutory consumer-rights interpretations, or technical repair procedures.

Those requirements should be verified with applicable manufacturers, authorized service providers, legal professionals and current regulations before implementation.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. After-sales processes should evolve based on customer feedback, service data, supplier performance and store experience.

Material changes should document the rationale, customer impact, affected SOPs, risks, and related decision IDs.
