# DEC-0062 — Information Security, Cybersecurity & Privacy Protection System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0062  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Information Security, Cybersecurity and Privacy Protection System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0051, DEC-0055, DEC-0058, DEC-0060, DEC-0061

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will protect company systems, customer information, employee information, financial records, intellectual property and operational data through a risk-based security and privacy management system.

Security will be treated as an operating responsibility, not merely an IT function. Privacy will be built into processes and products from the beginning.

> **Protect the business, protect the customer, and make secure behavior the default.**

## 1. Security Principles

The system will prioritize:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Least privilege
- Defense in depth
- Secure defaults
- Accountability
- Continuous improvement

## 2. Security Ownership

Material security and privacy responsibilities will have named owners with clear escalation paths.

## 3. Asset Inventory

The company should maintain an inventory of important:

- Devices
- Applications
- Cloud services
- Accounts
- Data stores
- Network infrastructure
- Critical vendors

## 4. Data Classification

Information should be classified according to sensitivity and business impact, for example:

- Public
- Internal
- Confidential
- Restricted

## 5. Access Control

Access should follow least privilege and need-to-know principles.

## 6. Identity Management

User identities should be unique, attributable and centrally managed where practical.

## 7. Authentication

Strong authentication should be used for important systems, with multi-factor authentication prioritized for privileged, financial, administrative and remote access.

## 8. Privileged Access

Administrative privileges should be limited, monitored and periodically reviewed.

## 9. Joiner / Mover / Leaver Controls

Access should be provisioned, changed and revoked promptly when people join, change roles or leave.

## 10. Password Management

The company should use strong credential practices and approved password-management mechanisms where practical.

## 11. Endpoint Security

Company-managed endpoints should use appropriate security controls such as supported software, screen locking, malware protection and encryption where appropriate.

## 12. Device Management

Business devices should be identifiable, maintained and remotely manageable where justified by risk.

## 13. Patch Management

Critical security updates should be prioritized and tracked.

## 14. Vulnerability Management

Material vulnerabilities should be identified, prioritized and remediated according to risk.

## 15. Secure Configuration

Systems should use hardened, documented configurations appropriate to their role.

## 16. Network Security

Networks should be segmented and protected according to business and technical risk.

## 17. Wireless Security

Business wireless networks should use appropriate authentication and encryption and should be separated from guest access where practical.

## 18. Remote Access

Remote access to company systems should use approved secure mechanisms and appropriate authentication.

## 19. Cloud Security

Cloud services should have defined owners, controlled access, secure configuration and appropriate monitoring.

## 20. Application Security

Applications should address authentication, authorization, input validation, secrets management and secure error handling.

## 21. Secure Development

Software developed for K&S should use appropriate secure-development practices and review proportional to risk.

## 22. Secrets Management

Passwords, API keys, tokens and certificates should not be embedded insecurely in source code or shared documents.

## 23. Encryption

Sensitive information should be encrypted in transit and, where appropriate, at rest.

## 24. Backup Security

Backups should be protected from unauthorized access and destructive events.

## 25. Backup Strategy

Critical data should have backups appropriate to recovery requirements, including consideration of offline or logically isolated copies where warranted.

## 26. Recovery Testing

Important backups should be tested periodically to confirm that restoration actually works.

## 27. Logging

Important systems should generate sufficient logs to support security investigation and operational troubleshooting.

## 28. Monitoring

Material security events should be monitored using proportionate detection capabilities.

## 29. Security Alerts

Alerts should have defined ownership, severity and escalation expectations.

## 30. Incident Response

The company should maintain a documented process for detecting, containing, investigating, recovering from and learning from security incidents.

## 31. Incident Severity

Incidents should be prioritized according to factors such as:

- Data exposure
- Business disruption
- Financial impact
- Customer impact
- Regulatory implications
- Scope

## 32. Incident Escalation

Material incidents should be escalated promptly to appropriate management and professional advisers where required.

## 33. Evidence Preservation

Potentially relevant security evidence should be preserved during significant investigations.

## 34. Breach Assessment

Potential personal-data incidents should be assessed for notification and other obligations under applicable law and DEC-0058.

## 35. Business Continuity

Critical technology services should have continuity arrangements consistent with DEC-0051 and DEC-0061.

## 36. Disaster Recovery

Recovery priorities and acceptable downtime should be defined for important systems.

## 37. Recovery Objectives

Critical systems should have documented recovery-time and recovery-point expectations where justified.

## 38. Third-Party Security

Material technology and service providers should be evaluated for security and privacy risk.

## 39. Vendor Access

Third parties should receive only the access necessary for their contracted work, for the shortest practical period.

## 40. Vendor Offboarding

Third-party access and data-handling arrangements should be reviewed when contracts end.

## 41. Privacy Governance

Privacy responsibilities should be assigned and integrated into business processes.

## 42. Privacy by Design

New products, services and material process changes should consider privacy requirements before implementation.

## 43. Data Minimization

The company should collect and retain only information reasonably necessary for legitimate purposes.

## 44. Purpose Limitation

Personal information should be used consistently with its communicated and lawful purposes.

## 45. Transparency

Customers and employees should receive appropriate information about relevant personal-data practices.

## 46. Consent and Preferences

Where consent is the applicable basis for processing, systems should capture and respect consent and withdrawal appropriately.

## 47. Individual Rights

The company should maintain processes for handling applicable privacy requests and rights.

## 48. Privacy Requests

Privacy requests should have an owner, verification process, deadline tracking and documented resolution.

## 49. Sensitive Information

Sensitive personal information should receive additional controls based on applicable requirements and risk.

## 50. Employee Privacy

Employee information should be protected with appropriate access and retention controls.

## 51. Customer Privacy

Customer data should be accessible only to authorized people and systems with legitimate business need.

## 52. Marketing Privacy

Marketing data practices should connect to DEC-0059 and applicable privacy requirements.

## 53. Financial Data Protection

Financial and payment-related information should receive appropriate security controls and restricted access.

## 54. Payment Security

Payment information should be handled through appropriately secured and approved payment providers and processes.

## 55. Data Retention

Retention periods should reflect legal, operational and business requirements rather than indefinite storage.

## 56. Secure Disposal

Information and devices should be securely disposed of when no longer required, appropriate to sensitivity.

## 57. Data Location

Important data-processing and storage locations should be understood where jurisdictional or contractual requirements make location relevant.

## 58. Cross-Border Data

Cross-border personal-data transfers should be assessed for applicable legal and contractual requirements.

## 59. Privacy Impact Assessment

Material new uses of personal data should receive proportionate privacy-risk assessment before launch.

## 60. Security Risk Register

Material security and privacy risks should be integrated with the enterprise risk register under DEC-0051.

## 61. Security Policies

Material security policies should have owners, approval, effective dates, versions and review dates.

## 62. Security Awareness

Employees should receive practical security training appropriate to their roles.

## 63. Phishing Awareness

Employees should be trained to recognize suspicious messages, links, attachments and impersonation attempts.

## 64. Social Engineering

Staff should understand that attackers may manipulate people rather than only technical systems.

## 65. Security Reporting

Employees should have a simple route to report suspicious activity or potential incidents.

## 66. Acceptable Use

Company technology should have clear acceptable-use expectations appropriate to the business.

## 67. Personal Devices

Use of personal devices for business information should be controlled according to security and privacy risk.

## 68. Removable Media

Sensitive data on removable media should be restricted and protected appropriately.

## 69. Email Security

Business email should use appropriate authentication, filtering and anti-phishing protections.

## 70. Domain Protection

Important company domains should use appropriate controls against spoofing and unauthorized changes.

## 71. Web Security

Customer-facing websites should use secure transport, supported software and appropriate monitoring.

## 72. API Security

APIs should authenticate and authorize requests, validate inputs and protect sensitive operations.

## 73. Security Testing

Material systems should undergo proportionate security testing before significant production use and after major changes where appropriate.

## 74. Penetration Testing

Higher-risk internet-facing systems may require independent penetration testing based on risk.

## 75. Vulnerability Disclosure

Where appropriate, the company may provide a responsible channel for reporting security vulnerabilities.

## 76. Fraud Coordination

Security controls should coordinate with financial and operational fraud controls under DEC-0057.

## 77. Physical Security

Physical access to offices, stores, equipment and sensitive areas should be controlled according to risk.

## 78. CCTV and Monitoring

Physical monitoring should respect applicable privacy requirements and have defined access and retention rules.

## 79. Store Security

Store technology and operational systems should connect to the security controls established under DEC-0053.

## 80. Service Security

Customer-service tools should protect customer records and restrict access according to role under DEC-0054.

## 81. Data in AI Systems

Confidential or personal information should only be entered into approved AI systems with appropriate security and privacy controls.

## 82. AI Security

AI systems should be assessed for risks including unauthorized disclosure, prompt manipulation, insecure integrations and excessive permissions.

## 83. AI Output Validation

Security-sensitive AI recommendations or generated actions should receive appropriate human validation before execution.

## 84. Security Automation

Automated controls may be used for patching, alerting, access reviews, backups and compliance checks where reliability is sufficient.

## 85. Change Management

Material security-sensitive changes should be reviewed, tested and documented according to risk.

## 86. Configuration Management

Important security configurations should be documented and protected from unauthorized modification.

## 87. Access Reviews

Privileged and sensitive-system access should be reviewed periodically.

## 88. Security Metrics

Management should monitor useful measures such as:

- Critical vulnerabilities overdue
- MFA coverage
- Backup success
- Security incidents
- Phishing reports
- Access-review completion
- Patch compliance

## 89. Privacy Metrics

Privacy reporting may include:

- Open privacy requests
- Request completion times
- Material privacy incidents
- Data-retention exceptions
- Privacy assessments completed

## 90. Compliance Alignment

Security and privacy controls should support applicable contractual, legal and regulatory requirements under DEC-0058.

## 91. Security Exceptions

Exceptions should be documented, risk-assessed, approved by an appropriate authority and given an expiry or review date where possible.

## 92. Independent Review

Material security and privacy controls should receive independent review when business scale or risk justifies it.

## 93. Security Budget

Security investment should be prioritized according to risk, business criticality and expected loss reduction.

## 94. Security Architecture

Technology architecture under DEC-0061 should incorporate security boundaries, identity, resilience and monitoring from the design stage.

## 95. Security by Default

New systems should begin with the most secure practical configuration rather than requiring security to be added after deployment.

## 96. Continuous Improvement

Incidents, audits, tests and operational experience should feed improvements into the security and privacy program.

## 97. Key Risks

Major risks include:

- Account compromise
- Ransomware
- Data leakage
- Phishing
- Insider misuse
- Vulnerable software
- Cloud misconfiguration
- Vendor compromise
- Privacy violations
- Inadequate recovery

## 98. Management Reporting

Material security and privacy risks should be visible to leadership with clear owners and actions.

## 99. Security Culture

Every employee should understand that protecting information and systems is part of their job.

## 100. Success Standard

The security and privacy system is successful when K&S can answer:

- What information and systems are critical?
- Who can access them and why?
- Are important systems protected and recoverable?
- Would we detect a serious incident?
- Could we respond quickly?
- Are privacy obligations built into our operations?

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish security and privacy ownership and risk registers.
2. Inventory critical assets, systems, data and vendors.
3. Implement strong identity, MFA and privileged-access controls.
4. Establish endpoint, patching, backup and vulnerability management.
5. Formalize incident response, business continuity and recovery testing.
6. Establish privacy governance, data classification and retention controls.
7. Implement security awareness and reporting processes.
8. Introduce proportionate security testing and third-party reviews.
9. Establish AI security and privacy controls.
10. Build management dashboards and periodic control reviews.
11. Continuously improve the program based on incidents, testing, business growth and regulatory change.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection framework**. Specific technical controls, legal obligations, standards and implementation priorities should be selected according to the company's risk profile, applicable jurisdiction and technology architecture.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document security, privacy, technology, operational and legal implications.
