# DEC-0028 — Customer Experience & Service Excellence System

**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat **customer experience as a core operating system**, not merely as a store-design or marketing activity.

The company will deliberately design, document, measure, and continuously improve the customer journey across discovery, store visit, consultation, purchase, setup, ownership, service, and future interactions.

> **Experience > Discounts.**

Price competitiveness matters, but KAMLESH & SONS will primarily differentiate through trust, knowledge, service, convenience, and the quality of the overall experience.

## 1. Customer Experience Promise

Customers should leave KAMLESH & SONS feeling:

- Respected
- Comfortable
- Informed
- Confident
- Properly assisted
- Proud of their purchase
- Interested in returning

The intended reaction is not simply:

> "I bought a phone."

It is:

> **"I visited KAMLESH & SONS."**

## 2. Customer Journey

The future experience framework should cover the full relationship:

**Discover → Visit → Welcome → Explore → Consult → Decide → Purchase → Setup → Own → Service → Return → Recommend**

The journey should not end when payment is completed.

## 3. Store Experience Principles

Stores should aim to be:

- Minimal
- Spacious
- Bright
- Elegant
- Premium
- Calm
- Interactive
- Welcoming

Customers should be able to experience products rather than feel that they are viewing locked inventory.

## 4. Hospitality Standard

Every customer should receive respectful and professional treatment regardless of:

- Budget
- Product preference
- Age
- Background
- Previous purchase history
- Whether they purchase immediately

Premium service must not become elitist service.

## 5. Consultation Philosophy

Staff should act as knowledgeable technology advisors rather than aggressive salespeople.

Consultation should begin by understanding the customer's needs, including where relevant:

- Intended use
- Budget
- Existing devices
- Preferences
- Required features
- Compatibility
- Future needs

Recommendations should explain trade-offs rather than simply push the highest-priced product.

## 6. Product Experience

Where operationally and securely possible, customers should be encouraged to:

- Hold devices
- Explore interfaces
- Compare products
- Test relevant features
- Ask questions
- Understand accessories and compatibility

Live displays should be maintained to a reliable standard so that the premium experience is not undermined by broken, outdated, or poorly configured demo units.

## 7. Purchase Experience

The purchase process should be:

- Clear
- Accurate
- Efficient
- Transparent
- Respectful

Customers should understand relevant pricing, billing, warranty, financing, and service terms before completing a purchase.

EMI and financing should be presented as useful options where appropriate, not as pressure mechanisms.

## 8. Post-Purchase Experience

The relationship continues after purchase.

Future premium services may include:

- Device setup
- Data transfer
- Screen protector installation
- Warranty assistance
- Service support
- Trade-in
- Personal consultation
- Workshops
- Membership

These services should be designed around genuine customer value and operational capability.

## 9. Service Excellence

Service interactions should emphasize:

- Clear communication
- Accurate diagnosis
- Honest expectations
- Status visibility
- Respectful handling
- Appropriate escalation
- Timely updates
- Proper closure

If a problem cannot be solved immediately, the customer should still receive clarity about what is happening next.

## 10. Complaint and Recovery System

A customer complaint is both a service event and a learning opportunity.

The company should eventually classify complaints by cause, severity, recurrence, and resolution.

Service recovery should focus on:

1. Listening
2. Understanding the problem
3. Taking ownership
4. Explaining the next step
5. Resolving where possible
6. Following up
7. Recording lessons

Staff should not make promises outside their authority merely to end a difficult conversation.

## 11. Customer Feedback

KAMLESH & SONS should create structured feedback mechanisms covering relevant journey stages.

Feedback should be analyzed for patterns rather than treated only as individual ratings.

Useful signals may include:

- Satisfaction
- Effort
- Service resolution
- Recommendation intent
- Product confidence
- Wait time
- Consultation quality
- Repeat visits

Specific metrics and targets require later research and operational decisions.

## 12. Customer Experience Metrics

Future CX dashboards may measure:

- Customer satisfaction
- Net Promoter Score or an appropriate alternative
- Service resolution time
- First-contact resolution
- Complaint rate
- Repeat visits
- Repeat purchases
- Referral behavior
- Setup-service usage
- Appointment completion

Metrics should drive learning, not employee gaming.

## 13. Employee Role in Experience

Customer experience is created by people as much as by systems and interiors.

Employees should therefore receive training in:

- Product knowledge
- Consultation
- Communication
- Service recovery
- Privacy
- Security
- Store standards
- AI tools
- Escalation procedures

Training should be continuously updated as products and processes change.

## 14. AI Concierge Integration

The AI Concierge should complement human staff rather than create a wall between customers and employees.

Customers should be able to move naturally between:

**Self-service → AI assistance → Human consultation**

Staff should be able to intervene when a customer wants personal help or when the situation requires human judgment.

## 15. Customer Continuity

DEC-0024 and DEC-0025 establish the future Customer Digital Identity, Experience Passport, and Customer Portal directions.

CX systems should use those capabilities to reduce unnecessary repetition, for example by allowing authorized customers to continue relevant conversations, access documents, or track service without starting from zero.

## 16. Accessibility and Respect

The premium experience should remain accessible and respectful to customers with different abilities, technical familiarity, and communication needs.

Accessibility should be considered in:

- Physical store design
- Signage
- Digital interfaces
- AI interaction
- Staff communication
- Service processes

## 17. Privacy and Trust

Customer experience must not be improved by violating customer trust.

Customer information should be collected and used according to the principles established by DEC-0024 and DEC-0026.

Staff should never disclose customer information simply because another person claims to know the customer.

## 18. Experience Standards

Future SOPs should convert this decision into observable standards such as:

- How customers are welcomed
- How product demonstrations are maintained
- How consultations are conducted
- How financing is explained
- How purchases are handed over
- How setup services are delivered
- How complaints are escalated
- How follow-up is performed

The standards should be specific enough to train and measure, while leaving employees room to act naturally rather than sounding scripted.

## 19. Continuous Improvement

Customer experience should operate as a continuous improvement loop:

**Observe → Measure → Learn → Improve → Test → Standardize → Repeat**

Changes should be supported by customer feedback, operational evidence, research, and experimentation where appropriate.

## 20. Risks

Major CX risks include:

- Premium branding without premium service
- Excessive sales pressure
- Inconsistent employee behavior
- Broken demo products
- Poor complaint handling
- Hidden fees or unclear terms
- Overpromising
- Unnecessary customer data collection
- Digital complexity
- Measuring the wrong things

The company should treat trust as a long-term asset that can be damaged by a single poor experience.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Map the end-to-end customer journey.
2. Identify critical moments of truth.
3. Research customer expectations and pain points.
4. Define service principles.
5. Create store and service SOPs.
6. Train staff.
7. Establish baseline CX metrics.
8. Pilot improvements.
9. Review customer and operational evidence.
10. Standardize successful practices.
11. Continue improving.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **customer experience and service excellence direction**. It does not yet define every SOP, staffing model, service-level target, compensation policy, customer survey, or store operating procedure.

Those should be developed progressively and cross-referenced to this decision.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Customer experience standards should evolve with customer expectations, store maturity, product categories, digital capabilities, and operational evidence.

Any material change to the customer experience philosophy should document the rationale, customer impact, alternatives considered, and affected repository documents.
