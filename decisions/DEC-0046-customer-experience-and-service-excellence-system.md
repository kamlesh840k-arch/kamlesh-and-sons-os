# DEC-0046 — Customer Experience & Service Excellence System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0046  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Customer Experience and Service Excellence System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0039, DEC-0041, DEC-0042, DEC-0045

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat customer experience as a core product of the company, not merely a consequence of selling technology.

The customer should leave feeling respected, informed, confident and proud of the decision to visit KAMLESH & SONS.

> **We are not only selling technology. We are designing the experience around it.**

## 1. Experience Philosophy

The experience should consistently communicate:

- Trust
- Calm
- Helpfulness
- Professionalism
- Expertise
- Respect
- Convenience
- Premium quality

## 2. Experience Over Price

KAMLESH & SONS will not build its primary competitive position around discounting.

The company should compete through:

- Trust
- Product knowledge
- Demonstration
- Service
- Convenience
- Follow-through

Price remains important, but it should not define the entire customer relationship.

## 3. Customer Journey

The target journey is:

**Discover → Visit → Welcome → Understand → Explore → Compare → Decide → Purchase → Setup → Support → Return**

Each stage should eventually have measurable standards.

## 4. First Impression

The first moments in the store should communicate that the customer has entered a premium technology environment.

Store presentation, employee behavior and product readiness must reinforce the same message.

## 5. Welcome Standard

Every customer should be acknowledged appropriately.

Employees should avoid both extremes:

- Ignoring customers
- Aggressively pursuing customers

The goal is to make customers comfortable enough to explore.

## 6. Discovery Before Recommendation

Employees should understand the customer's actual needs before recommending products.

Discovery should focus on the customer's life, work and technology needs rather than immediately pushing a specific model.

## 7. Customer Respect

Customers should be treated respectfully regardless of:

- Budget
- Product category
- Age
- Appearance
- Technical knowledge
- Whether they purchase immediately

Every visitor is a potential long-term relationship.

## 8. Product Experience

Where possible, customers should be able to physically and practically experience products before purchasing.

Live demonstrations should answer the customer's real questions.

## 9. Consultation Model

For complex purchases, staff should behave more like technology consultants than traditional salespeople.

The objective is to help the customer choose well.

## 10. Honest Recommendation

Employees must explain meaningful trade-offs.

If a less expensive product is genuinely better suited to the customer's needs, recommending it is consistent with KAMLESH & SONS values.

## 11. Avoiding Pressure Selling

Employees should not create artificial urgency, make unsupported claims, or pressure customers into purchases they do not understand.

Short-term sales should never destroy long-term trust.

## 12. Comparison Experience

The store should make product comparison easy.

Comparison may include:

- Performance
- Camera
- Battery
- Display
- Software
- Ecosystem
- Storage
- Durability
- Accessories
- Price
- Financing

## 13. AI Concierge Experience

The flagship AI Concierge should support customers without replacing human hospitality.

It may help customers:

- Compare products
- Ask questions
- Find specifications
- Explore accessories
- Receive recommendations
- Continue conversations digitally

## 14. Human + AI Model

The intended experience is:

**AI for information and continuity + Human for empathy, judgment and final guidance.**

Customers should be able to move between AI and employees without losing context where appropriate.

## 15. QR Conversation Continuation

The AI Concierge should eventually allow customers to continue a conversation on their phone through an approved QR-based experience.

Privacy and account controls must follow DEC-0042.

## 16. Customer Account

A customer account should make future interactions easier rather than create unnecessary friction.

Potential benefits include:

- Warranty access
- Invoices
- Device history
- Service status
- Wishlist
- Appointments
- AI history
- Recommendations

## 17. Personalization

Personalization should be useful and proportionate.

The company should not collect information simply because it is technically possible to collect it.

## 18. Customer Privacy

Customer information must be handled according to approved privacy and security controls.

Employees should only access information necessary for their role.

## 19. Purchase Experience

Checkout should feel:

- Clear
- Accurate
- Efficient
- Professional
- Unpressured

The customer should understand what they are paying for.

## 20. Financing Experience

Financing should be explained transparently.

Customers should understand relevant:

- Price
- Fees
- Terms
- Eligibility
- Payment obligations

Employees should not make promises controlled by financing providers.

## 21. Handover Experience

The purchase should not end when payment is completed.

The handover should help the customer become comfortable with the new device.

## 22. Setup Service

Where offered, setup should include appropriate guidance for:

- Accounts
- Basic settings
- Security
- Data transfer
- Essential applications

Customer credentials should never be retained by staff.

## 23. Post-Purchase Support

Customers should know how to obtain help after purchase.

Support information should be easy to find and consistent across channels.

## 24. Warranty Assistance

KAMLESH & SONS should help customers understand the appropriate warranty path and assist with documentation or routing where the service is offered.

Employees must not make unsupported warranty promises.

## 25. Service Experience

Service interactions should be:

- Clear
- Traceable
- Respectful
- Timely
- Honest about uncertainty

Customers should receive realistic expectations rather than false certainty.

## 26. Service Communication

Where a service case takes time, customers should receive meaningful status updates according to the service policy.

Silence should not be the default communication strategy.

## 27. Complaint Handling

Complaints should be treated as opportunities to recover trust and identify system failures.

Employees should:

1. Listen
2. Clarify
3. Acknowledge
4. Investigate
5. Explain
6. Resolve or escalate
7. Record important lessons

## 28. Service Recovery

When the company makes a mistake, the response should focus on:

- Owning the issue
- Communicating clearly
- Correcting the problem where possible
- Learning from the failure

Compensation or exceptions should follow approved authority levels.

## 29. Escalation

Customers should have a clear path when frontline staff cannot resolve an issue.

Escalation should be fast enough to prevent avoidable frustration while protecting operational controls.

## 30. Customer Communication Standards

Communication should be:

- Clear
- Polite
- Concise
- Accurate
- Human

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

## 31. Digital Experience

The website and future Customer Portal should extend the store experience rather than feel like unrelated systems.

Customers should recognize the same KAMLESH & SONS identity across physical and digital channels.

## 32. Omnichannel Continuity

Where practical, customers should be able to move between:

- Store
- Website
- AI Concierge
- Customer Portal
- Phone/support channels

without repeatedly explaining the same information.

## 33. Customer Follow-Up

Appropriate follow-up may include:

- Setup support
- Service updates
- Warranty information
- Appointment reminders
- Customer-requested information

Follow-up should be useful rather than spammy.

## 34. Marketing vs Service Communication

Operational customer communication should be distinguishable from optional marketing communication.

Marketing preferences should be respected according to applicable requirements and company policy.

## 35. Loyalty

Future loyalty programs should reward long-term relationships without creating unnecessary complexity.

Potential benefits may include:

- Service benefits
- Early access
- Workshops
- Offers
- Personalized recommendations

Specific loyalty economics require separate decisions.

## 36. Experience Passport

The future Experience Passport may provide a continuity layer across the customer lifecycle.

Potential information could include:

- Devices
- Warranty
- Service
- Preferences
- Appointments
- Membership

It must be designed with privacy and customer control at its center.

## 37. Workshops and Education

KAMLESH & SONS may offer workshops that help customers get more value from technology.

Potential subjects include:

- Smartphone skills
- Photography
- Productivity
- AI literacy
- Device security
- Smart home

Education strengthens the relationship beyond the transaction.

## 38. Corporate Experience

Corporate customers may require a different journey involving:

- Consultation
- Volume purchasing
- Device standards
- Financing
- Deployment
- Support
- Account management

The experience should remain premium while adapting to organizational needs.

## 39. Customer Feedback

Feedback should be collected through appropriate channels.

Potential methods include:

- Post-purchase feedback
- Service feedback
- QR surveys
- Customer interviews
- Review monitoring

Feedback should be analyzed for patterns rather than treated as isolated comments.

## 40. Customer Experience Metrics

Management may eventually monitor:

- Customer satisfaction
- Recommendation likelihood
- Complaint rate
- Complaint resolution time
- Service turnaround
- Repeat visits
- Repeat purchases
- Conversion
- Average transaction value
- Setup-service adoption
- Loyalty engagement

No single metric should define customer experience quality.

## 41. Mystery Shopping / Experience Audits

Periodic mystery shopping or structured experience audits may be used to evaluate whether the intended experience is actually delivered.

Results should be used for coaching and improvement rather than punishment alone.

## 42. Employee Experience Connection

Customer experience and employee experience are connected.

Employees cannot consistently deliver calm, knowledgeable service if systems, training or leadership create unnecessary chaos.

## 43. Training

Customer-facing employees should receive recurring training in:

- Product knowledge
- Discovery
- Demonstration
- Communication
- Service recovery
- Privacy
- AI tools
- Financing explanations
- Conflict management

## 44. Customer Experience and AI Governance

AI should never be allowed to invent product availability, pricing, warranty promises or policy exceptions.

High-impact customer information should come from controlled sources and have clear escalation paths.

## 45. Accessibility and Inclusion

The company should seek to make its customer experience usable and respectful for a broad range of customers.

Store and digital accessibility requirements should be incorporated as the business and facilities develop.

## 46. Customer Trust Rules

The following should be treated as non-negotiable principles:

- Do not mislead
- Do not hide important conditions
- Do not fabricate information
- Do not misuse customer data
- Do not pressure unnecessarily
- Do not promise what the company cannot deliver

## 47. Experience Failure Review

Material customer failures should be reviewed using:

**What happened? → Customer impact? → Root cause? → Immediate recovery? → System change? → Owner?**

## 48. Service Excellence Culture

Service excellence should become a behavior system rather than a slogan.

Managers should recognize employees who consistently demonstrate:

- Patience
- Ownership
- Knowledge
- Empathy
- Accuracy
- Follow-through

## 49. Future Experience Dashboard

The Founder Dashboard may eventually combine experience signals from:

- Sales
- Service
- Feedback
- Complaints
- Customer accounts
- Loyalty
- AI interactions

The dashboard should surface patterns, not encourage intrusive surveillance.

## 50. Risks

Major customer-experience risks include:

- Aggressive selling
- Inconsistent service
- Misleading recommendations
- Poor complaint handling
- Long service delays
- Privacy failures
- AI misinformation
- Broken digital continuity
- Unclear financing terms
- Unreliable follow-up

## Implementation Sequence

1. Define customer journey stages and standards.
2. Establish greeting and discovery training.
3. Standardize product demonstration and comparison.
4. Improve checkout and handover.
5. Establish service and complaint standards.
6. Establish customer feedback mechanisms.
7. Train staff in service recovery.
8. Launch AI Concierge with controlled knowledge sources.
9. Build customer account and continuity capabilities.
10. Establish customer-experience metrics.
11. Introduce workshops and loyalty capabilities.
12. Continuously improve the experience using customer evidence.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **customer experience and service-excellence framework**. Specific service policies, loyalty economics, customer-data practices, AI capabilities and compensation/exception rules require separate controlled decisions and implementation documents.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document customer impact, business rationale, risk, affected processes and related decision IDs.
