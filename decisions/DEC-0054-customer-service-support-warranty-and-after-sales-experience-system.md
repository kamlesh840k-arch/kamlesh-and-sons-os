# DEC-0054 — Customer Service, Support, Warranty & After-Sales Experience System

**Decision ID:** DEC-0054  
**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Customer Service, Support, Warranty and After-Sales Experience System  
**Status:** Accepted  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Decision Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Owner:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Related Documents:** [ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md)  
**Related Decisions:** DEC-0045, DEC-0046, DEC-0048, DEC-0050, DEC-0051, DEC-0053

---

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will treat after-sales service as a core part of the product and customer experience rather than as a separate repair function.

Every service interaction should strengthen trust, provide clear ownership and move the customer toward a fair and timely resolution.

> **The sale starts the relationship; excellent after-sales service earns the next one.**

## 1. Service Philosophy

Customer service should be:

- Respectful
- Fast where possible
- Transparent
- Fair
- Traceable
- Technically competent
- Consistent
- Human when situations are difficult

## 2. Customer Ownership

Every customer issue should have a clear owner or queue.

Customers should not be forced to repeatedly explain the same problem because internal teams failed to transfer context.

## 3. Omnichannel Support

Support should eventually operate across approved channels such as:

- Store
- Phone
- Email
- Messaging
- Customer Portal
- AI Concierge

Customer context should remain connected where technically and legally appropriate.

## 4. Case Management

Service cases should have a unique identifier and track:

- Customer
- Product/device
- Purchase reference
- Issue
- Status
- Owner
- Dates
- Actions
- Resolution

## 5. Case Statuses

A standard lifecycle should be used where appropriate:

**New → Triaged → In Progress → Waiting → Resolved → Closed**

Additional statuses may be introduced when required.

## 6. Service Intake

At intake, teams should capture enough information to understand:

- Customer need
- Product/device
- Purchase history where available
- Warranty status
- Symptoms
- Customer expectation

## 7. Identity Verification

Appropriate verification should be used before exposing sensitive customer information or authorizing protected actions.

## 8. Device Identification

For devices with serial numbers or IMEI, service records should capture the correct identifier where relevant.

## 9. Warranty Verification

Warranty status should be determined using authoritative records and applicable manufacturer/company terms.

Staff should not promise coverage they cannot verify.

## 10. Warranty Communication

Customers should receive clear explanations of:

- Coverage
- Exclusions
- Required documentation
- Expected timelines
- Next steps

## 11. Warranty Boundaries

Where a manufacturer or third party controls warranty obligations, KAMLESH & SONS should clearly distinguish its role from the manufacturer's obligations.

## 12. Non-Warranty Service

Out-of-warranty cases should provide transparent information about:

- Diagnosis
- Estimated cost
- Parts
- Labor
- Timing
- Approval requirements

## 13. Repair Authorization

Material repair costs should receive customer authorization before work proceeds, except where an approved policy explicitly provides otherwise.

## 14. Diagnosis

Technical diagnosis should be documented sufficiently to support:

- Repair decisions
- Customer communication
- Warranty claims
- Quality analysis

## 15. Repair Quality

Repairs should follow approved technical procedures and use appropriate parts and tools.

## 16. Parts Traceability

Material replacement parts should be traceable where practical, particularly for high-value or warranty-sensitive repairs.

## 17. Repair Status

Customers should be able to understand where their case stands without repeatedly contacting the company.

## 18. Service-Level Expectations

Service categories should have target response and resolution times appropriate to their urgency and complexity.

Targets should be measured as service commitments, not unrealistic promises.

## 19. Priority Handling

Cases may be prioritized based on:

- Safety
- Customer impact
- Business impact
- Warranty deadlines
- Product criticality
- Escalation status

## 20. Communication Standards

Customer communications should be clear, factual and respectful.

Teams should avoid technical jargon when it does not help the customer.

## 21. Proactive Updates

When resolution takes time, customers should receive useful updates rather than being left to chase the company.

## 22. Delay Management

When service is delayed, teams should explain:

- What happened
- Current status
- Expected next step
- Revised timing where known

## 23. Escalation

Escalation should be available for:

- Repeated unresolved issues
- Serious complaints
- Safety concerns
- Significant financial impact
- Vulnerable situations
- Management exceptions

## 24. Service Recovery

When KAMLESH & SONS makes a meaningful service failure, appropriate recovery may include:

- Apology
- Corrective action
- Priority handling
- Approved goodwill gesture

Any financial compensation should follow authority controls.

## 25. Complaint Management

Complaints should be recorded, categorized and analyzed rather than treated only as individual conversations.

## 26. Root Cause Analysis

Repeated complaints should trigger investigation into systemic causes such as:

- Product quality
- Staff training
- Supplier issues
- Process failures
- Communication gaps
- Technology failures

## 27. Customer Feedback

The company should collect feedback at appropriate moments without creating survey fatigue.

## 28. Customer Satisfaction

Potential measures include:

- Customer satisfaction
- Resolution rate
- First-contact resolution
- Repeat contact rate
- Complaint rate
- Resolution time

## 29. First-Contact Resolution

Where practical, teams should resolve straightforward issues during the first meaningful interaction.

## 30. Repeat Contacts

Repeated customer contacts for the same unresolved issue should be visible as a quality signal.

## 31. Service Knowledge Base

Support teams should use a controlled knowledge base containing approved:

- Troubleshooting guidance
- Warranty rules
- Product information
- Service procedures
- Escalation rules

## 32. Knowledge Governance

Service knowledge should have owners and version control.

Outdated instructions should be removed or clearly marked.

## 33. AI Concierge

The AI Concierge may assist with:

- Basic troubleshooting
- Warranty information
- Service-case status
- Appointment guidance
- Product support
- FAQ resolution

AI responses should use approved knowledge sources.

## 34. AI Escalation

The AI Concierge should escalate when:

- Information is uncertain
- A customer requests an exception
- A safety issue is involved
- A financial commitment is required
- The case is emotionally or operationally sensitive
- The AI lacks authoritative information

## 35. Human Service

AI should support employees rather than eliminate appropriate human judgment in complex customer situations.

## 36. Customer Data

Service records should follow DEC-0042 and DEC-0050 requirements for privacy, security and access.

## 37. Data Minimization

Service teams should collect only information necessary for the service purpose.

## 38. Service Access Controls

Employees should access customer service information according to role and legitimate business need.

## 39. Service Fraud Controls

Controls should address risks including:

- Fake warranty claims
- Unauthorized repairs
- Parts theft
- False service completion
- Duplicate claims
- Fraudulent refunds

## 40. Service Parts Inventory

Service parts should be managed according to DEC-0052 and tracked separately where operationally useful.

## 41. Service Vendor Management

Third-party repair or service providers should be evaluated for:

- Technical capability
- Reliability
- Quality
- Security
- Customer handling
- Warranty compliance

## 42. Authorized Service Network

Where external service partners are used, the company should maintain clear standards for authorization, quality and escalation.

## 43. Quality Assurance

Completed service work should receive appropriate quality checks based on risk.

## 44. Repair Rework

Repeat repairs for the same issue should be tracked as a quality metric.

## 45. Turnaround Management

Service operations should monitor:

- Intake-to-diagnosis time
- Diagnosis-to-approval time
- Approval-to-repair time
- Repair-to-return time

## 46. Loaner / Temporary Solutions

Where appropriate and economically justified, the company may offer temporary solutions for customers experiencing extended service interruptions.

## 47. Pickup and Delivery

If service pickup/delivery is offered, chain-of-custody and customer communication should be controlled.

## 48. Chain of Custody

Customer devices and valuable products should be traceable while in company or partner custody.

## 49. Customer Property

Customer property should be protected from loss, damage and unauthorized access while under company control.

## 50. Data on Customer Devices

Service processes involving customer devices should include appropriate procedures for protecting customer data and avoiding unnecessary access.

## 51. Device Reset / Data Loss

Customers should be informed where service may require data reset or create data-loss risk, subject to applicable law and manufacturer requirements.

## 52. Unclaimed Items

Unclaimed repaired products should follow a documented process consistent with applicable law and company policy.

## 53. Returns and Exchanges

Returns and exchanges should follow approved policies with clear authorization and inventory controls.

## 54. Refunds

Refunds should be linked to the original transaction where practical and follow finance controls.

## 55. Warranty Claims to Suppliers

Supplier/manufacturer warranty claims should be documented and reconciled with service outcomes.

## 56. Manufacturer Escalation

Cases requiring manufacturer intervention should have clear ownership so customers are not unnecessarily bounced between organizations.

## 57. Service Documentation

A completed service case should contain enough information for future reference, warranty evidence and quality analysis.

## 58. Customer Communication Templates

Standard templates may be used for common communications, but employees should adapt them when a situation requires human judgment.

## 59. Service Tone

Service language should be:

- Respectful
- Direct
- Non-defensive
- Honest about uncertainty
- Focused on solutions

## 60. Difficult Customers

Employees should remain professional when customers are frustrated.

Abusive or threatening behavior should be handled under approved escalation and safety procedures.

## 61. Employee Empowerment

Frontline employees should have defined authority to resolve common issues without unnecessary management escalation.

## 62. Service Authority Matrix

The company should define approval limits for:

- Refunds
- Discounts
- Goodwill gestures
- Replacement
- Repair exceptions
- Warranty exceptions

## 63. Service Training

Customer-service and technical staff should receive role-appropriate training in:

- Product knowledge
- Service process
- Communication
- Warranty
- Privacy
- Security
- Escalation

## 64. Certification

Technical or high-risk service roles should use competency or certification requirements appropriate to the work.

## 65. Service Scheduling

Appointments should capture enough information to prepare the team and customer.

## 66. Capacity Planning

Service capacity should be planned against expected demand, product mix and seasonal peaks.

## 67. Queue Management

Service queues should make waiting work visible and prioritize appropriately.

## 68. Service Dashboard

Management should eventually see:

- Open cases
- Aging cases
- Resolution time
- First-contact resolution
- Repeat repairs
- Warranty cases
- Customer satisfaction
- Complaint trends
- Parts availability

## 69. Store-Service Integration

Store teams should be able to create or access service cases without losing customer context.

## 70. CRM Integration

Service history should connect to the customer record where appropriate and authorized.

## 71. Commerce Integration

Purchase history should support warranty and service verification where appropriate.

## 72. Inventory Integration

Service consumption and returned parts should reconcile with inventory records.

## 73. Finance Integration

Service charges, refunds and warranty reimbursements should reconcile with finance systems.

## 74. Analytics

Service analytics should identify:

- Product failure patterns
- Supplier quality problems
- Staff training needs
- Process bottlenecks
- High-cost service categories
- Customer churn risks

## 75. Closed-Loop Improvement

Service insights should feed back into:

- Procurement
- Product selection
- Training
- Store operations
- Marketing
- Vendor management

## 76. Customer Retention

Service quality should be treated as a major driver of repeat purchases, referrals and long-term customer value.

## 77. Trust Principle

The company should never deliberately hide a material service problem, warranty limitation or delay that affects the customer's decision.

## 78. Service Economics

Service should be financially sustainable while maintaining fair customer treatment.

Management should understand service cost, recovery and customer lifetime value.

## 79. Service Pricing

Published or quoted service prices should be clear enough to avoid avoidable disputes.

## 80. Warranty Cost Analysis

Management should monitor warranty cost by:

- Product
- Supplier
- Failure type
- Store
- Service provider
- Time period

## 81. Vendor Quality Feedback

Material service-quality problems should be communicated back to suppliers and manufacturers.

## 82. Product Recall Support

If a product recall occurs, service operations should support identification, communication, repair/replacement and reporting according to the responsible authority's requirements.

## 83. Safety-Critical Issues

Safety-related product issues should receive immediate escalation and appropriate customer protection measures.

## 84. Service Continuity

Service operations should have fallback procedures for major technology, staffing, supplier or facility disruptions.

## 85. Service Recovery During Outages

When service technology fails, teams should use approved temporary procedures while protecting customer data and preserving records for later reconciliation.

## 86. Knowledge and Institutional Memory

Important service lessons should be recorded in K&S OS so they remain available beyond individual employees.

## 87. Service Governance

Material service-policy changes should be documented and approved through appropriate governance.

## 88. Continuous Improvement

Service teams should periodically review:

- Customer feedback
- Complaints
- Repair quality
- Resolution times
- Escalations
- Cost

## 89. Service KPIs

Core KPIs may include:

- First response time
- Resolution time
- First-contact resolution
- Customer satisfaction
- Net promoter/recommendation measure where used
- Repeat contact rate
- Rework rate
- Warranty cost
- Complaint rate
- Case aging

## 90. Risks

Major service risks include:

- Poor customer communication
- Warranty disputes
- Lost customer devices
- Data exposure
- Repair errors
- Counterfeit or poor-quality parts
- Service fraud
- Excessive case backlog
- Vendor failure
- AI misinformation

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish standardized service intake and case management.
2. Define warranty and return rules in approved knowledge sources.
3. Establish service authority and escalation matrices.
4. Standardize repair, parts and chain-of-custody controls.
5. Connect service with CRM, commerce, inventory and finance.
6. Build service dashboards and customer feedback loops.
7. Establish service training and technical certification.
8. Build manufacturer and service-partner governance.
9. Introduce controlled AI Concierge support.
10. Use service analytics to continuously improve products, suppliers and operations.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **customer service, support, warranty and after-sales experience framework**. Specific warranty policies, service prices, repair procedures, service partners and customer communication standards should be implemented through approved operating procedures.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document customer, legal, financial, operational and brand implications.
