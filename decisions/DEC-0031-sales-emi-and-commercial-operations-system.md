# DEC-0031 — Sales, EMI & Commercial Operations System

**Title:** Establish the KAMLESH & SONS Sales, EMI and Commercial Operations System

**Version:** 0.1.0

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build its commercial operation around **trusted selling, transparent pricing, disciplined controls, and customer-appropriate financing**.

Sales should maximize long-term customer value and company sustainability rather than short-term transaction volume alone.

> **Sell with knowledge. Price with transparency. Finance with responsibility.**

## 1. Sales Philosophy

The sales team should act as technology advisors.

The commercial process should prioritize:

- Customer needs
- Product suitability
- Transparent information
- Accurate pricing
- Appropriate recommendations
- Sustainable margins
- Long-term relationships

Aggressive pressure selling is inconsistent with the KAMLESH & SONS customer experience philosophy.

## 2. Sales Journey

The standard commercial journey should align with DEC-0028:

**Welcome → Discover Needs → Demonstrate → Compare → Recommend → Explain Price/Terms → Confirm → Complete Sale → Handover → Follow-up**

The exact workflow may vary by category and customer situation.

## 3. Product Recommendation

Recommendations should be based on documented customer needs rather than simply the highest available price.

Where two products are suitable, employees should explain meaningful differences and trade-offs.

If KAMLESH & SONS does not have the right product, staff should be comfortable saying so rather than forcing an unsuitable sale.

## 4. Pricing Integrity

Customer-facing pricing should be:

- Accurate
- Current
- Clearly communicated
- Consistent with approved commercial policy
- Supported by applicable terms

Any discount must be authorized under the company's approval structure.

Employees must not create unofficial discounts, false urgency, or misleading price comparisons.

## 5. Margin Discipline

Revenue alone is not sufficient to evaluate commercial performance.

Future commercial reporting should consider, as appropriate:

- Revenue
- Gross margin
- Contribution margin
- Product/category mix
- Accessory attachment
- Service revenue
- Financing impact
- Returns
- Discounts
- Customer acquisition cost where measurable

Specific targets require financial planning and later approval.

## 6. Discount Governance

Discount authority should be structured by role and threshold.

Future policy should define:

- Standard pricing
- Promotional pricing
- Maximum frontline discount
- Manager approval threshold
- Exceptional approval threshold
- Documentation requirements

The objective is to protect both margin and employee clarity.

## 7. EMI and Financing Strategy

EMI should become an important part of the KAMLESH & SONS sales strategy because appropriate financing can improve access to premium technology.

Financing should be offered transparently and responsibly.

Employees should clearly explain relevant:

- Eligibility
- Tenure
- Interest or applicable charges
- Processing fees
- Down payment where applicable
- Documentation
- Approval conditions
- Cancellation/return implications

Actual financing terms must come from the authorized financing partner or approved commercial system.

## 8. Financing Partner Strategy

KAMLESH & SONS may partner with banks, finance companies, payment providers, or other authorized institutions.

Partner selection should evaluate:

- Customer experience
- Approval rates
- Pricing
- Reliability
- Settlement process
- Support
- Compliance
- Data handling
- Integration capability
- Geographic coverage

Specific partners require separate research and approval.

## 9. Responsible Financing

Employees should never encourage customers to take financing they do not understand or cannot reasonably afford.

Financing should solve an access problem, not become a mechanism for misleading customers about the real cost of a product.

## 10. Payment Operations

The company should support approved payment methods appropriate to the market, potentially including:

- Cash
- Cards
- Bank transfers
- Digital payments
- Approved financing/EMI

Payment confirmation must be verified before product release according to the applicable SOP.

## 11. Billing and Invoicing

Sales records and invoices should be accurate and complete according to applicable requirements.

The commercial system should maintain appropriate links between:

**Customer → Sale → Product/SKU → Serial/IMEI where applicable → Invoice → Payment → Warranty**

Financial records should remain under appropriate accounting controls.

## 12. Returns, Exchanges and Cancellations

The company should maintain a clearly documented returns/exchange policy appropriate to product category, condition, supplier requirements, and applicable law.

Employees should not make promises outside approved policy.

Exceptions should have documented authority and an audit trail where appropriate.

## 13. Trade-In

Future trade-in capability should use a standardized valuation process.

Potential factors include:

- Device model
- Age
- Condition
- Storage
- Functional status
- Physical damage
- Accessories
- Market value
- Demand

The future AI Trade-in Estimator should support, not bypass, approved valuation controls.

## 14. Corporate Sales

Corporate sales should become a potential dedicated commercial channel.

Future processes may include:

- Business account onboarding
- Bulk quotations
- Product configuration
- Volume pricing
- Procurement documentation
- Invoicing
- Delivery coordination
- Device lifecycle support
- Service agreements

Corporate commercial policies should be developed separately as this channel matures.

## 15. Sales Targets and Incentives

Future targets should balance commercial performance with customer experience and operational quality.

A sales incentive system should avoid rewarding behavior that causes:

- Misleading recommendations
- Excessive discounting
- Unnecessary accessories
- Customer complaints
- Policy violations
- Unsustainable returns

Potential scorecard dimensions may include revenue, margin, customer satisfaction, compliance, product knowledge, and service quality.

Specific compensation structures require a separate people/finance decision.

## 16. Customer Data in Sales

Customer data should be collected only for defined business purposes and with appropriate consent where required.

Sales staff should not collect unnecessary personal information simply because a CRM field exists.

Sensitive information should be protected according to company privacy and security requirements.

## 17. Sales + CRM

Relevant commercial events should eventually integrate with CRM so that authorized staff can understand the customer relationship without recreating records manually.

Potential data includes:

- Customer identity
- Purchases
- Preferences
- Follow-up tasks
- Service history
- Appointments
- Consent

The authoritative source for each data domain must remain clear.

## 18. Sales + AI

Customer AI may help customers compare products and understand pricing or financing information from approved sources.

Internal AI may help staff with product knowledge, sales guidance, and process lookup where authorized.

AI must not invent prices, availability, financing terms, discounts, or policy exceptions.

## 19. Commercial Reporting

Future dashboards should provide visibility into:

- Sales revenue
- Gross margin
- Units sold
- Average transaction value
- Category mix
- Brand mix
- Accessory attachment
- Financing penetration
- Returns
- Discounts
- Conversion where measurable
- Repeat purchases

Reports should support decisions and should distinguish gross sales from actual economic performance.

## 20. Fraud and Commercial Controls

Commercial operations should include controls against:

- Fake payment confirmation
- Unauthorized discounts
- Refund abuse
- Inventory manipulation
- Duplicate transactions
- Financing fraud
- Identity misuse
- Collusion
- Product substitution

High-risk activities should have appropriate approval, verification, and audit trails.

## 21. Commercial Calendar

Future commercial planning should coordinate:

- Product launches
- Promotions
- Seasonal demand
- Financing campaigns
- Inventory levels
- Marketing campaigns
- Staff training
- Supplier programs

Commercial activity should not be planned independently from inventory and customer experience.

## 22. Risks

Major commercial risks include:

- Margin erosion
- Over-discounting
- Mis-selling
- Financing misunderstandings
- Payment fraud
- Unauthorized refunds
- Poor inventory synchronization
- Aggressive sales culture
- Inaccurate pricing
- Overdependence on promotions

The company should manage these risks through clear authority, reliable systems, training, and monitoring.

## Implementation Sequence

The preferred sequence is:

1. Define sales process and authority levels.
2. Establish pricing and discount governance.
3. Define payment and billing controls.
4. Research financing/EMI partners.
5. Build financing SOPs.
6. Establish commercial reporting.
7. Define returns/exchange rules.
8. Pilot trade-in processes.
9. Develop corporate sales capability.
10. Align incentives with customer experience and profitability.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **sales, EMI and commercial operations direction**. It does not select banks, finance companies, payment providers, commission plans, pricing targets, or final return policies.

Those require separate research, financial analysis, legal review, operational testing, and approval.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Commercial standards should evolve with market conditions, financing availability, product mix, customer behavior, and store maturity.

Material changes should document the rationale, financial/customer impact, affected systems, and related repository documents.
