## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will maintain an **Innovation Vault** as the structured home for ideas that may create future customer, operational, commercial, or technological value.

The Innovation Vault will separate **ideas from approved decisions** and provide a disciplined path for promising concepts to be researched, tested, validated, and potentially implemented.

## Why the Innovation Vault Exists

The company should encourage ambitious ideas without allowing every idea to become company policy or consume resources prematurely.

The Innovation Vault creates a safe place to capture ideas while preserving strategic discipline.

> **Capture everything valuable. Commit selectively. Learn continuously.**

## Innovation Lifecycle

The standard lifecycle is:

**Idea → Hypothesis → Research → Experiment → Validation → Decision → Implementation → Review**

An innovation may stop at any stage. Not every idea needs to reach implementation.

## Innovation IDs

Each formal innovation should receive a unique identifier, for example:

- `INN-0001` — AI Concierge
- `INN-0002` — Experience Passport
- `INN-0003` — Customer Digital Identity
- `INN-0004` — Founder Dashboard
- `INN-0005` — Employee AI
- `INN-0006` — AI Trade-in Scanner

IDs should remain stable even if an idea is renamed later.

## Innovation Record Standard

Each Innovation Vault record should capture, where applicable:

- Innovation ID
- Name
- Status
- Owner
- Date created
- Problem
- Proposed solution
- Target customer or user
- Hypothesis
- Expected value
- Evidence
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Estimated effort
- Experiment design
- Success criteria
- Results
- Related research
- Related decisions
- Related roadmap items

## Status Definitions

### Captured

The idea has been recorded but has not yet been evaluated.

### Exploring

The problem, customer, technology, or opportunity is being investigated.

### Experimenting

A limited test is underway to evaluate a specific hypothesis.

### Validated

Evidence supports moving the concept toward a formal decision.

### Approved

A formal decision authorizes implementation or further investment.

### Implemented

The capability has been introduced into the company or an approved operating environment.

### Paused

The concept remains relevant but is not currently being pursued.

### Rejected

The concept has been evaluated and is not being pursued under the current strategy.

### Superseded

The concept has been replaced by another approach.

## Experimentation Principles

### 1. Test Before Scaling

When uncertainty is significant, run a limited experiment before committing major resources.

### 2. Define the Hypothesis

An experiment should state what is expected to change and why.

### 3. Define Success Before Testing

Where practical, success criteria should be established before results are known to reduce confirmation bias.

### 4. Keep Experiments Proportionate

A small uncertainty should not require a large project. Experiments should be appropriately scoped.

### 5. Record Negative Results

Failed or inconclusive experiments should be documented because they prevent repeated mistakes and improve institutional knowledge.

### 6. Protect Customers

Experiments involving customers, personal information, payments, AI decisions, or production systems must include appropriate consent, privacy, security, and operational safeguards.

## Innovation and Decision Separation

The Innovation Vault is **not** the Decision Log.

An innovation can exist for months without becoming company policy.

A formal decision should be created when the company commits to a material strategic direction, investment, policy, architecture, or operating change.

The relationship should be:

**Innovation → Evidence → Decision → Implementation**

## Resource Discipline

Innovation work should be prioritized according to:

- Customer value
- Strategic alignment
- Evidence
- Differentiation
- Feasibility
- Cost
- Risk
- Scalability
- Learning potential

High-visibility ideas should not automatically receive priority over less exciting improvements that solve important customer or operational problems.

## AI and Innovation

AI may be used to accelerate research, prototyping, analysis, and experimentation. However, AI-generated proposals must be evaluated by responsible humans before becoming company decisions.

The company should distinguish clearly between:

**AI-generated possibility** and **validated business evidence**.

## Cross-Referencing

Innovation records should link to:

- Research
- Decisions
- Roadmap items
- Product requirements
- Architecture
- UX work
- Experiments
- Meeting records

When an innovation is approved or rejected, the relevant decision should link back to the original innovation record so the history remains traceable.

## Governance

The Innovation Vault should be reviewed periodically to identify:

- High-potential ideas ready for research
- Experiments requiring decisions
- Stalled concepts
- Duplicate ideas
- Superseded concepts
- Valuable lessons from completed experiments

The review should not become a ceremony for its own sake. Its purpose is to keep the innovation portfolio useful and connected to company strategy.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **innovation and experimentation framework**. It does not create all Innovation Vault records or approve every innovation listed above.

Individual innovations should receive their own records as they are formally captured and developed.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. The system may evolve as K&S OS develops more mature product, research, engineering, and experimentation practices.

Any material change to the innovation lifecycle should document the rationale, implications, alternatives considered, and affected repository documents.
