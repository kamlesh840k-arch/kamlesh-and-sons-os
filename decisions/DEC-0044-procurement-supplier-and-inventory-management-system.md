**Version:** 0.1.0

## Decision

KAMLESH & SONS will build a disciplined procurement and inventory system designed to ensure the **right products, in the right quantities, at the right cost, with the right traceability**.

> **Inventory is not just stock on a shelf. It is capital, customer promise and operational risk.**

Procurement and inventory decisions must therefore connect customer demand, supplier relationships, cash flow, product availability, margin and operational controls.

## 1. Procurement Philosophy

Procurement should optimize for total business value rather than lowest purchase price alone.

Evaluation should consider:

- Product authenticity
- Supplier reliability
- Purchase cost
- Payment terms
- Delivery reliability
- Warranty/support
- Return terms
- Availability
- Lead time
- Brand alignment
- Risk

## 2. Authorized and Trusted Supply

KAMLESH & SONS should prioritize legitimate, traceable supply channels appropriate to its intended premium positioning.

Supplier status and authorization claims must be verified rather than assumed.

Grey-market or otherwise non-compliant supply should not be presented to customers as authorized supply.

## 3. Supplier Master

The company should maintain a controlled supplier record containing, where appropriate:

- Legal/business name
- Contact information
- Supplier category
- Authorized products/brands
- Commercial terms
- Payment terms
- Delivery terms
- Warranty/return terms
- Key contacts
- Compliance documents
- Performance history
- Risk classification

## 4. Supplier Due Diligence

Important suppliers should be evaluated before significant purchasing commitments.

Due diligence may include:

- Business identity
- References
- Authorization evidence
- Commercial terms
- Product authenticity controls
- Financial reliability
- Warranty support
- Delivery capability
- Conflict-of-interest checks

The depth of review should match supplier risk.

## 5. Supplier Segmentation

Suppliers may be classified by:

- Strategic importance
- Spend
- Product criticality
- Availability risk
- Brand impact
- Customer impact

Strategic suppliers should receive more structured relationship management.

## 6. Procurement Authority

Purchasing authority should be defined by role and transaction value.

No employee should be able to create unlimited purchasing commitments without appropriate approval.

## 7. Purchase Request

Procurement should begin with a documented need where practical.

A purchase request should identify:

- Product/SKU
- Quantity
- Reason
- Expected timing
- Required budget
- Preferred supplier if known

## 8. Purchase Order / Authorization

Significant purchases should use a controlled purchase order or equivalent authorization.

The record should connect the approved requirement to the supplier commitment.

## 9. Three-Way Verification

Where practical, finance and operations should reconcile:

**Purchase Authorization → Goods Received → Supplier Invoice**

Differences should be investigated before payment.

## 10. Product Master

K&S OS should maintain a controlled product master.

Important fields may include:

- Product ID/SKU
- Brand
- Model
- Variant
- Storage/configuration
- Color
- Supplier
- Cost
- Selling price
- Tax information where applicable
- Warranty information
- Category
- Serial/IMEI tracking requirement
- Active/inactive status

## 11. SKU Discipline

Each sellable product configuration should have a unique, controlled identifier where practical.

SKU naming should be consistent and scalable across locations.

## 12. Serial and IMEI Tracking

Products that require serial or IMEI traceability should be recorded from receipt through sale, service, return or other disposition.

This is especially important for smartphones, tablets, laptops and other serialized technology products.

## 13. Goods Receiving

Receiving should verify, as appropriate:

- Quantity
- Product/SKU
- Packaging
- Physical condition
- Serial/IMEI
- Supplier documentation
- Purchase authorization

Discrepancies should be documented before stock is accepted into available inventory.

## 14. Authenticity and Condition

Products should be inspected according to category-specific controls to reduce the risk of counterfeit, damaged, altered or incorrectly supplied goods.

Receiving procedures should distinguish:

- New
- Open-box where legitimately offered
- Refurbished where legitimately offered
- Returned
- Damaged
- Demo/display

## 15. Inventory Statuses

K&S OS should distinguish inventory states such as:

- Available
- Reserved
- Sold
- In transit
- Receiving hold
- Damaged
- Service/repair
- Return pending
- Demo/display
- Quarantine

This prevents operational teams from treating all physical units as immediately sellable.

## 16. Inventory Location

Inventory should be traceable to a physical or logical location.

Potential locations include:

- Store floor
- Secure back room
- Warehouse
- Service area
- Customer reservation
- Transit
- Supplier return

## 17. Stockroom Security

High-value technology inventory requires controlled access.

Access should be limited and material movements should be traceable.

## 18. Inventory Counts

The company should use a cycle-count and/or full-count program appropriate to inventory risk.

High-value or high-risk products may require more frequent verification.

## 19. Inventory Reconciliation

Physical counts should be compared with system records.

Material differences should be investigated and documented.

## 20. Shrinkage Control

Inventory shrinkage may result from:

- Theft
- Misplacement
- Receiving errors
- Sales-entry errors
- Returns
- Service movements
- Damage
- Administrative mistakes

The company should track root causes rather than simply adjusting numbers.

## 21. Inventory Valuation

Inventory valuation should follow a consistent method appropriate to the business and applicable accounting requirements, aligned with DEC-0043.

## 22. Reorder Strategy

Replenishment should consider:

- Sales velocity
- Lead time
- Safety stock
- Supplier reliability
- Seasonality
- Launch cycles
- Promotions
- Cash constraints
- Minimum order quantities

## 23. Demand Forecasting

Future forecasting may use:

- Historical sales
- Current sales velocity
- Product launches
- Seasonal patterns
- Marketing campaigns
- Store-level demand
- Supplier lead times

Forecasts should be treated as decision support, not certainty.

## 24. Slow-Moving Inventory

Management should identify slow-moving products before they consume excessive working capital.

Possible actions include:

- Better merchandising
- Bundling
- Targeted promotion
- Supplier negotiation
- Transfer between locations
- Return where contractually permitted
- Controlled markdown

## 25. Obsolete Inventory

Technology products can become obsolete quickly.

The company should monitor product lifecycle risk and avoid excessive purchasing near major replacement cycles.

## 26. Inventory Aging

Inventory reporting should include age bands appropriate to technology retail.

Older inventory should receive management attention because its commercial value may decline over time.

## 27. Allocation Across Stores

As the company expands, inventory allocation should consider location-level demand rather than distributing stock equally by default.

## 28. Inter-Store Transfers

Transfers should have controlled documentation covering:

- Sending location
- Receiving location
- Product/SKU
- Quantity
- Serial/IMEI where applicable
- Date
- Responsible employees
- Reason

## 29. Customer Reservations

Reserved inventory should be clearly identified so it is not accidentally sold to another customer.

Reservation rules should define expiry, payment requirements and release procedures.

## 30. Demo and Display Inventory

Demo units should be separately identified from sellable new inventory.

The company should track:

- Demo location
- Condition
- Accessories
- Responsible team
- Replacement cycle
- Final disposition

## 31. Returns to Supplier

Supplier returns should preserve traceability to:

- Original purchase
- Product/SKU
- Serial/IMEI where applicable
- Reason for return
- Credit/replacement
- Shipping
- Final resolution

## 32. Damaged Inventory

Damaged units should be moved into a controlled status and not sold as normal stock without an approved disposition.

## 33. Service Inventory

Products awaiting service, diagnosis or warranty processing should be separated logically from available sales inventory.

## 34. Accessories and Consumables

Accessories should be managed according to their sales velocity, value and shrinkage risk.

Small high-value accessories may require stronger controls than their unit price alone suggests.

## 35. Bundles and Kits

If products are sold as bundles, the system should preserve component-level inventory and financial traceability where practical.

## 36. Supplier Performance

Supplier performance should eventually be measured using indicators such as:

- On-time delivery
- Fill rate
- Defect rate
- Return resolution time
- Warranty support
- Price stability
- Communication quality
- Commercial reliability

## 37. Supplier Negotiation

Supplier negotiations should consider total economics rather than headline purchase price.

Relevant factors may include:

- Credit terms
- Rebates
- Volume pricing
- Return rights
- Warranty support
- Delivery costs
- Marketing support

## 38. Conflict of Interest

Employees involved in procurement should disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

Personal relationships or benefits should not improperly influence supplier selection.

## 39. Gifts and Supplier Benefits

The company should define acceptable and prohibited supplier gifts, incentives or benefits.

Commercial decisions must remain independent and customer-focused.

## 40. Procurement Fraud Controls

Controls should address risks including:

- Fake suppliers
- Inflated prices
- Unauthorized purchases
- Duplicate invoices
- Kickbacks
- False receiving
- Product substitution
- Quantity manipulation

## 41. Procurement and Finance Integration

Procurement should integrate with finance for:

- Budget control
- Purchase commitments
- Invoice verification
- Accounts payable
- Cash planning
- Inventory valuation

## 42. Procurement and Sales Integration

Procurement should understand commercial demand without allowing sales pressure to bypass financial or supplier controls.

## 43. Procurement and Marketing Integration

Major campaigns and product launches should coordinate inventory availability before customer promises are made.

## 44. AI-Assisted Inventory

Future Internal AI may assist with:

- Demand analysis
- Reorder recommendations
- Aging alerts
- Supplier performance summaries
- Stock anomaly detection
- Product lifecycle alerts

AI recommendations must remain subject to authorized human review.

## 45. Inventory Alerts

The future operating system should support alerts for:

- Low stock
- Excess stock
- Slow-moving stock
- Aging inventory
- Unexpected shrinkage
- Receiving discrepancies
- Supplier delays

## 46. Inventory KPIs

Management may eventually monitor:

- Inventory turnover
- Sell-through rate
- Days of inventory
- Stockout rate
- Gross margin return on inventory
- Aging inventory
- Shrinkage rate
- Supplier fill rate
- Forecast accuracy
- Inventory accuracy

## 47. Inventory and Cash

Inventory purchasing must be evaluated against available working capital.

A product that sells eventually can still create a cash-flow problem if purchased too aggressively.

## 48. Expansion Readiness

The inventory system should be designed so that additional stores and warehouses can be added without rebuilding the underlying model.

## 49. Traceability Standard

For high-value technology, K&S OS should aim for end-to-end traceability:

**Supplier → Receipt → Location → Reservation/Sale → Customer → Service/Return → Final Disposition**

## 50. Risks

Major risks include:

- Overstock
- Stockouts
- Counterfeit products
- Supplier failure
- Inventory theft
- Data inaccuracies
- Obsolescence
- Cash trapped in stock
- Unauthorized purchasing
- Poor receiving controls
- Warranty/return leakage

## Implementation Sequence

1. Establish supplier master and procurement authority.
2. Establish product master and SKU standards.
3. Define receiving and serial/IMEI controls.
4. Establish inventory statuses and locations.
5. Implement stock-count and reconciliation procedures.
6. Connect procurement with finance and inventory.
7. Establish supplier performance management.
8. Build reorder and inventory-aging reporting.
9. Establish inter-store transfer controls for expansion.
10. Introduce AI-assisted inventory analysis only after reliable underlying data exists.
11. Build end-to-end traceability across procurement, sales and service.

## Implementation Status

This decision establishes the **procurement, supplier and inventory-management direction** for K&S OS. It does not approve any specific supplier, purchase commitment, product authorization claim, accounting method, or commercial contract.

Supplier and product decisions should be separately evaluated and documented according to risk and business need.

## Review / Supersession

This decision remains active until formally superseded. Material changes should document rationale, commercial impact, operational risk, affected systems and related decision IDs.
