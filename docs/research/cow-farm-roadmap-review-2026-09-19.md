# Cow Farm OS — Nepal/Bardiya Dairy Roadmap Review

**Review date:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** Action required  
**Scope:** Bardiya/Badhaiyatal 10-cow pilot; Nepal milk market, subsidy, insurance and credit  
**Evidence labels:** V = verified/public official or direct report; S = secondary signal; H = hypothesis/open validation

## Executive decision

**Remain NO-GO on purchasing cows or committing to a single milk buyer.**

The local payment risk is still the controlling gate. A Bardiya report published on 2026-09-03 says farmers in Bardiya and Banke had not received milk payments for about eight months; nine of Bardiya’s 12 cooperatives reportedly supply the Nepalgunj Milk Distribution Project, with roughly NPR 80 million pending in Bardiya alone. This is more decision-relevant than national statements about improving payment cycles. [S/V local reporting]

## Meaningful changes since the previous review

### 1. Local milk-payment risk remains severe — action required

- Bardiya/Banke farmers were reported unpaid for approximately eight months as of 2026-09-03.
- The Nepalgunj Milk Distribution Project reportedly had not paid since Magh 2081 / January 2025.
- The report states that nine of Bardiya’s 12 cooperatives supply that project.
- The reported Bardiya exposure was approximately NPR 80 million, with some cooperatives carrying several million rupees of unpaid milk value.

**Roadmap impact:** keep a **90-day minimum working-capital buffer** in the base case and test a 120-day downside case. Do not underwrite the farm on a promised 25–30 day payment cycle without local evidence.

### 2. New opportunity: temperature-index milk-production insurance — action required

Sagarmatha Lumbini Insurance, with Plant Sat, launched a temperature-index milk-production insurance product for heat-related production losses. Bardiya was explicitly included in the target districts. The reported coverage window for the first product year was 2026-04-21 through 2026-09-14, so that window has now ended.

**Roadmap impact:** request the next available season’s terms immediately and ask whether the product will be renewed, expanded or re-priced for Bardiya. Treat this as a potential heat-risk hedge, not a replacement for normal livestock mortality/health cover.

### 3. Livestock-insurance subsidy is more attractive, but implementation must be verified

The Agricultural, Livestock and Herbal Insurance Premium Subsidy Procedure, 2083, has been reported to provide up to 80% premium subsidy for insured value up to NPR 10 million, with 50% subsidy on the excess portion. Provincial governments are expected to coordinate implementation.

**Roadmap impact:** obtain written insurer quotations showing: subsidy eligibility, animal valuation basis, farmer/cooperative documentation, claim timing, exclusions and provincial implementation route. Do not model subsidy as cash until an insurer or authority confirms eligibility for this project.

### 4. Milk-market signal is mixed, not resolved

DDC reported plans to reduce payment delays and target 150,000 litres/day collection, while separate reporting still described financial stress, farmer arrears and large stocks. The Supreme Court has ordered the government to ensure dairy-farmer dues are cleared, but this does not prove that a Bardiya supplier will receive timely payment.

**Roadmap impact:** treat national DDC reform and court action as a **supportive policy signal**, not as evidence of local cash-flow reliability.

### 5. Credit remains available, but the bank route is still unproven

NRB changes have maintained agriculture as a priority lending area while giving banks more flexibility. The revised subsidized-loan framework may improve access for eligible agricultural borrowers, but actual pricing, collateral, guarantee and documentation terms remain bank-specific.

**Roadmap impact:** request two written bank term sheets and explicitly ask whether the application can use a livestock-guarantee route and whether the bank recognizes insured livestock as part of the risk package.

## Subsidy-call and local-program check

I found **no newly verified Badhaiyatal-specific FY 2083/84 dairy-equipment or cattle-purchase call** in the public pages reviewed for this cycle. The Badhaiyatal budget/program page and publications page were available, but no clearly identified open dairy call was visible in the reviewed material.

**Action:** contact Badhaiyatal’s livestock/agriculture section and Bardiya IALDO directly before purchasing any milking, fodder, chilling or livestock assets. Ask for the current FY 2083/84 notice list, application deadlines, eligible applicants, co-financing percentage, required registration documents and whether pre-purchase costs are disallowed.

## Research gaps that now affect the next decision

1. **Buyer payment ledger:** actual payment dates for at least 3–6 months from three possible buyers/cooperatives.
2. **Contract terms:** written price, collection schedule, quality deductions, rejection rules, transport responsibility and dispute process.
3. **Buyer concentration risk:** whether one buyer can absorb output and what the backup route is.
4. **Insurance implementation:** written confirmation of subsidy and the availability of the temperature-index product for the next season.
5. **Bank terms:** two written term sheets, including collateral, grace period, rate, guarantee and repayment schedule.
6. **Local feed economics:** verified prices and seasonal availability for maize, silage, concentrate, mineral mix and labour in Bardiya.
7. **Farm-level production baseline:** candidate cows’ actual 7-day milk records, lactation stage, parity, pregnancy status, mastitis history and transport distance.

## Updated roadmap recommendation

### Gate 1 — Buyer and cash-flow validation (must pass before cow purchase)

- Secure three local buyer/cooperative offers.
- Verify recent payment dates with existing supplying farmers.
- Model 90-day base-case and 120-day downside receivables delay.
- Identify one backup buyer or direct-sale route.

### Gate 2 — Risk-transfer and financing validation

- Obtain two livestock-insurance quotes under the 2083 framework.
- Ask for the next Bardiya temperature-index milk-production policy cycle.
- Obtain two bank term sheets and one livestock-guarantee route assessment.

### Gate 3 — Farm and animal validation

- Confirm site, water, fodder acreage, drainage, shade/cooling and manure plan.
- Screen at least 20 cows and shortlist 10 using records rather than appearance alone.
- Require veterinary examination and a 7-day observed milk baseline before purchase.

### Current control decision

**NO-GO on cow purchase today.** Proceed only after Gates 1 and 2 are documented and the 10-cow downside model remains solvent under a 120-day milk-payment delay.
