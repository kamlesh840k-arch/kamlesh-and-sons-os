## Research question and evidence boundary

What brand/distributor support could improve the flagship's economics and execution without surrendering customer choice, evidence integrity or control of the store?

- **V — verified fact:** official program/policy evidence shows the mechanism exists somewhere.
- **P — public claim:** a brand/distributor/retailer describes its own network or program.
- **E — estimate:** value calculated from disclosed inputs.
- **H — hypothesis:** proposed K&S value or eligibility requiring written Nepal evidence.
- **D — founder decision:** only a signed decision record can approve exclusivity, space, spend or partner selection.

Public global or India programs are **mechanism benchmarks only**. They do not establish K&S eligibility, budget, rates or entitlement in Nepal.

## Findings

1. **V/P:** Electronics channel programs can include fixtures, demo support, rebates, sales incentives, promotions, MDF and training. Current Dell partner terms describe discounts, rebates, spiffs, promotions and MDF; HP's current anti-corruption policy governs MDF/demand-generation funds, proof of performance and demo products.
2. **P:** Samsung India currently runs paid retail on-the-job training that includes product education, demonstrations and store processes. This proves brand-supported retail staffing/training is an active mechanism, not that it is available to K&S.
3. **P:** Apple and Xiaomi maintain official Nepal retailer locators. These establish formal retail networks but publish no Nepal partner economics or K&S eligibility.
4. **V/H:** Support has economic value only after ownership, eligibility, claim evidence, settlement, tax, clawback, staffing and exit obligations are priced. A large branded fixture can have negative value if it consumes better-selling space or creates restoration cost.
5. **H:** The best K&S negotiation is a measured “brand experience partnership”: a defined bay/campaign with service levels and reporting, in exchange for a written support menu and a short reviewable pilot.

## Support taxonomy and true-value test

| Support type | Typical mechanism | Evidence required | True-value calculation | Main hidden risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixtures/signage/shop-in-shop | Brand supplies, loans or reimburses build | Design approval, asset owner, invoice/payment, removal terms | Cash received + usable asset value − K&S build/maintenance/restoration − space opportunity cost | Locked layout, outdated fixtures, landlord damage |
| Live/dummy demos | Free/loaned/discounted units or later sell-off support | Serial/title, activation, refresh/return, damage and sale rules | Avoided demo cost + recoverable resale − depreciation, theft/damage, activation and insurance | Obsolete or non-saleable stock |
| MDF/co-op reimbursement | Preapproved eligible marketing reimbursed after proof | Program version, preapproval, cap/rate, proof and claim record | Cash/credit note collected − K&S spend − production/admin/tax − rejected-claim risk | Spending before approval; slow/rejected claim |
| Launch/festival support | Event, gift, media or temporary placement funding | Campaign brief, budget, approvals, settlement and unused-gift rules | Collected funding + incremental contribution − total campaign cost | Vanity spend or customer promise not funded |
| Promoter/brand specialist | Brand/distributor funds or seconds staff | Employer, wage/hours, training, supervision, data and complaint rules | Avoided payroll + incremental contribution − management/space/neutrality cost | Mis-selling, customer-data leakage, brand bias |
| Sell-in/sell-out rebate or spiff | Rate per purchase, sale, target or staff action | Eligible SKU/event, base, deadline, tax and settlement evidence | Collected eligible rebate − incremental stock/discount/admin cost | Overstock to hit target; unearned margin |
| Training/content | Product, launch, systems and approved content | Curriculum, attendance, certification, licence/version | Avoided training/content cost + measured conversion/service benefit | Promotional training without needs-led advice |
| Allocation/O2O leads | Launch stock, reservation, click-and-collect or leads | Allocation rule, fulfilment duty, fee, refund/data terms | Incremental contribution − fulfilment, cancellation and acquisition cost | Channel conflict and poor availability |
| Credit/protection/rotation | Commercial inventory support | Signed finance/protection schedules | Model separately in supplier economics | Double counting support as both margin and finance |

## Commercial proposal schedule

K&S should request the same dated, version-controlled written menu from each target brand/distributor. “Available” without a value, condition, owner and settlement path is **not confirmed**.

### Fixtures, signage and shop-in-shop

Record bay dimensions, approved design, cap/rate, cash versus in-kind value, procurement party, asset ownership, delivery/install date, landlord consent, electricity/network, maintenance, insurance, refresh, exclusivity, removal and restoration. Estimate opportunity cost using contribution per square foot/metre and the next-best use.

### Demo units

Record live/dummy quantity, model/serial, free/loan/discount, tax invoice, title, activation, warranty, security, damage/theft, insurance, refresh, price protection, return, resale permission, end valuation and replacement SLA.

### MDF/co-op/launch funds

Record program name/version, eligibility period, fund base/rate/cap, campaign preapproval, eligible/ineligible costs, procurement/vendor constraints, brand/content approval, invoices, photo/performance proof, claim deadline, expected acknowledgement/payment, cash versus credit note, VAT/tax, audit, rejection, clawback and unused-fund treatment.

**Control:** no campaign spend relies on reimbursement until written preapproval is stored. No unreceived claim is available cash.

### Promoters and brand staff

Record legal employer, pay/benefits, schedule, absence replacement, hiring/control, training/certification, targets/incentives, uniform, conduct, needs-led comparison, customer-data/privacy access, complaint ownership, workplace safety, termination and handover. K&S keeps the right to stop mis-selling or unsafe conduct.

### Rebates and incentives

Record eligible SKU/serial and event, sell-in versus sell-out, calculation base, tier/target, return/cancellation impact, staff-payment path, evidence, deadline, settlement, tax, disputes and clawback. The accountant classifies purchase-price reductions versus selling-expense reimbursements.

## Evaluation model and non-negotiable gates

Compare support on **expected collected value**, not headline value:

`Expected collected value = contractual value × evidence-valid probability × settlement probability`

`Net support value = expected collected value − K&S cash spend − operating/admin cost − tax/leakage − space/customer-experience cost − exit/restoration liability`

`Realisation rate = reconciled cash/credit notes received ÷ valid claims submitted`

Do not approve support when:

- K&S authorisation or brand-rights evidence is missing;
- spend is required before written preapproval;
- exclusivity or placement blocks needs-led comparison or a more valuable category;
- demo title, damage, return or resale is unclear;
- promoter employer, customer-data or complaint responsibility is unclear;
- claims lack objective evidence, deadline and settlement method;
- support is necessary to make the underlying inventory profitable but remains discretionary;
- removal/restoration or early-termination liability is unbounded;
- a high support score would override failed authorisation, warranty, customer-experience or downside-cash gates.

## K&S offer and negotiation sequence

Present a one- to two-page **KAMLESH & SONS Brand Experience Partnership** with location/catchment evidence, exact bay/campaign assets, launch readiness, staff capability, authorised-channel controls, measured sell-through reporting and a 6- or 12-month pilot review.

Ask the Nepal distributor/channel manager—not simply global headquarters—for:

1. current retailer appointment and brand-use requirements;
2. fixture/signage and demo menu;
3. MDF/co-op and launch/festival programs;
4. promoter/training and systems support;
5. sell-out incentives and claim process;
6. stock allocation, price protection, rotation and credit as separate commercial schedules;
7. territory, online-price, channel-conflict, exclusivity and exit rules.

Protect the independent multi-brand experience. Temporary campaign dominance can be tested; permanent category surrender requires founder approval and measured economics.

## Field evidence plan

1. Send the canonical proposal form to at least three handset and two laptop/accessory distributors.
2. Ask each for a current program guide, sample claim, sample credit note and two retailer references.
3. Verify one settled support claim with consent, including submission-to-payment days.
4. Inspect at least six comparable branded bays in Nepalgunj/Kohalpur/Kathmandu: dimensions, live demos, staffing, neutrality, maintenance and campaign freshness.
5. Interview the store manager separately from the brand representative about ownership, claim rejection, target pressure, stock risk and removal.
6. Enter only written terms in the operating workbook; label verbal statements and estimates explicitly.
7. Run a short pilot and measure claim realisation, conversion, attachment, customer experience, space contribution and operational time before renewal.

## Founder-decision boundary

This document does not approve a brand, distributor, fixture, demo, MDF spend, promoter, target, exclusivity, customer-data access or claim accounting. Founder approval follows comparable written proposals, true-value/downside analysis and qualified review where required.

## Major-batch synthesis

### What we learned

The public evidence confirms the support mechanisms but not Nepal terms. The practical asset is a written, claimable program with low friction and a clean exit—not a verbal promise of “branding support.”

### What changed

The earlier shelf-rent framing has become a complete support taxonomy with cash, accounting, space, customer-experience, promoter and exit controls. Fixtures and demos are no longer treated as automatically free value.

### K&S opportunity

K&S can trade credible Western Nepal execution, clean data and a premium launch surface for selective brand investment while preserving multi-brand trust.

### Risks and unresolved questions

No current public source establishes 2026 K&S eligibility, rates, claim performance or promoter structure in Nepal. These require written proposals and retailer-reference checks.

### Recommended next research

Collect the program menus and sample claims in parallel with supplier term sheets, then compare expected collected value and customer/space costs in the operating workbook. The next desk batch should model trade-in, service concierge, pickup/drop and loaner economics.
