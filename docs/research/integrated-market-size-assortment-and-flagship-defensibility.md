## Decision question and evidence labels

What market evidence, assortment structure and operating advantages would make the proposed Nepalgunj flagship commercially defensible without treating a weak proxy, planning target or attractive service idea as proof?

- **V — verified fact:** current official/primary evidence.
- **P — public claim:** a company describes its own offer; execution is not independently verified.
- **E — estimate:** a visible calculation using stated inputs.
- **H — hypothesis:** requires field, commercial or operating validation.
- **D — founder decision:** an accepted decision record; this study creates none.

## Executive conclusion

1. **V:** Nepalgunj had 164,444 people and 34,565 households in the 2021 census. Of those households, 25,535 reported a smartphone, 6,347 a computer/laptop and 16,335 home internet. These are installed-base indicators, not annual purchases or unique-device counts.
2. **V:** Nepal's preliminary FY 2082/83 customs data records 2,267,419 smartphone imports, 154,991 laptops/notebooks and 32,727 tablets. Customs units and values establish national category scale; they do not reveal Nepalgunj sell-through, brand share, price band, official/grey split or retailer inventory.
3. **E:** Allocating national smartphone imports mechanically by Nepalgunj's share of the 2021 national population gives approximately 12,800 phones per year, or 1,065–1,066 per month. This remains a scale check—not local TAM, SAM or a sales forecast.
4. **E/H:** The existing month-three target of 70 phones would equal roughly 6.6% of that city-only proxy; the year-one target of about 1,070 phones would equal roughly 8.4%. Those percentages are not defensible market-share claims because the denominator omits regional inflow, channel leakage, inventory timing and local replacement behavior.
5. **V:** No current authoritative public source located reports Nepalgunj phone/laptop retail units, price-band mix, retailer share or model sell-through. The 2018 National Economic Census can describe an older establishment base, but it cannot substitute for a 2026 technology-retail denominator.
6. **H:** The opening commercial centre should remain phone-led and value-led, with deep evidence requirements below NPR 35,000, shallow stock above that band, laptops and premium devices mainly demonstration/order-led, and accessories tied to observed attachment rather than shelf breadth.
7. **H:** The defensible proposition is not a collection of EMI, exchange, setup and warranty services. Public competitor evidence shows those are table stakes. The stronger candidate is a repeatable **Decision-to-Ownership Confidence System** that joins advice, true-cost disclosure, verified handover, privacy-safe migration, traceable case ownership and a controlled upgrade path.
8. **H:** The flagship should reach founder review only when market demand, store capture capacity, contribution, inventory exposure and execution readiness agree. A large theoretical market cannot rescue an unprofitable basket; a profitable basket cannot rescue insufficient traffic or demand.

## What the current evidence can and cannot size

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| Nepalgunj population and households | Resident scale and sampling frame | Annual device demand, regional catchment or retailer share |
| Household smartphone/computer/internet access | Installed-base and category-relevance signal | Devices per household, age, replacement timing or purchase channel |
| National customs units/value | National category flow and broad value signal | Local units, retail price, brand/price-band mix or grey-market volume |
| NTA subscriptions | Connectivity/use context | Unique people, unique devices, purchase rate or Nepalgunj demand |
| Public directories and 2018 Economic Census | Competitor/establishment discovery and older structural context | Current active stores, category turnover, sales quality or 2026 market size |
| Competitor public pages | Advertised table stakes and benchmark mechanisms | Consistent execution, economics, customer satisfaction or local availability |
| K&S targets and budgets | Scenario and capacity questions | Market facts or founder approval |

The correct output at this stage is therefore a **sizing system with explicit missing inputs**, not a precise market-size number.

## Four-part market definition

Do not use TAM, SAM and SOM without defining geography, channel, category, period and inclusion rules.

1. **Resident consumer demand:** purchases by Nepalgunj residents, separated into phones, laptops/tablets and accessories.
2. **Regional/catchment demand:** customers travelling from Banke outside the city, Kohalpur, Karnali and other western districts. Count only observed origin-linked transactions or evidence-backed interviews; do not multiply city demand by an assumed gateway factor.
3. **Institutional demand:** schools/campuses, healthcare, NGOs/INGOs, SMEs and public/procurement buyers. Keep separate from consumer demand until duplicate buyers and periods are controlled.
4. **Ownership-support demand:** setup, repair coordination, warranty intake, pickup/drop, membership and trade-in. Size by cases and contribution, not device units.

For each category, define:

- **Market universe:** all relevant purchases in the stated geography and period, regardless of channel.
- **Serviceable market:** purchases K&S can lawfully serve through approved authorised supply, price bands, finance, warranty and fulfilment.
- **Capture case:** purchases K&S could win given traffic, qualified visits, conversion, stock/lead time, price position and operating capacity.
- **Break-even requirement:** sales/cases required for contribution to cover fixed, finance and risk costs.

## Triangulated sizing methods

No single method is sufficient. Use at least two independent local methods and retain the public proxy only as a reasonableness check.

### Method A — public pro-rata scale check

`City category proxy = national annual import units × Nepalgunj population ÷ Nepal population`

Use only to flag impossible results. It assumes uniform per-capita demand and ignores regional draw, channel leakage, inventory changes, institutional purchases and demographic/income differences.

### Method B — comparable sell-through coverage

`Estimated monthly market units = comparable observed seller/distributor units ÷ documented coverage share`

Requirements:

- eight to ten comparable sources covering the same 30- and 90-day windows;
- category, price band and channel scope stated;
- system export/invoice separated from recall and estimate;
- duplicate distributor-to-retailer units removed;
- coverage share justified through an outlet frame, distributor territory data or another auditable method;
- a low/base/high range reflecting missing sellers and evidence quality.

If coverage share is a guess, the result is H—not E presented as fact.

### Method C — replacement/upgrade demand

`Annual consumer units = addressable users × annual replacement/purchase rate × in-scope channel share`

The census household-access rate cannot directly fill addressable users or replacement rate. Obtain device counts/age and purchases in the last 12/24/36 months from a probability-oriented study if a population estimate is required. The canonical 100-person purposive survey can guide segmentation and hypotheses but cannot produce a Nepalgunj population prevalence estimate.

### Method D — transaction-origin catchment

Use at least four weeks of consent-safe transaction/postcode/locality evidence from participating retailers or a K&S pilot. Separate Nepalgunj resident, Banke nonresident, Karnali/other Nepal and India/other origins. Regional uplift is the observed nonresident share after duplicates—not a narrative multiplier.

### Method E — institutional procurement

Aggregate written last-12-month and expected-next-12-month quantities from the twelve-institution protocol. Report confirmed budget/order, documented plan, verbal estimate and exploratory interest separately. Do not add a tender pipeline to consumer TAM or treat intention as committed revenue.

### Triangulation rule

Create low/base/high cases only after Methods B and C, or another two genuinely independent local methods, are available. Use the lower result as the downside anchor, investigate material disagreement and show each method separately. The workbook's optional min/average/max triangulation is a transparent comparison convention—not an approved forecast rule.

## Store capture and capacity bridge

Market size and K&S sales are connected through observable funnel stages:

`Potential category visits → qualified visits → quoted exact SKU → stock/order availability → accepted price/finance → completed sale → retained contribution`

Track monthly:

- traffic and qualified visits by source;
- need/category, budget and intended timing;
- quote, stock, order-lead-time and follow-up status;
- lost-sale reason: price, finance, unavailable SKU, warranty/channel, trust, delay, competitor relationship or postpone;
- exact-SKU price gap at time of loss;
- completed units, cancellations/returns and contribution;
- resident versus catchment origin without collecting unnecessary identity.

`Funnel sales capacity = qualified visits × observed conversion rate`

`Required market share = planned K&S units ÷ evidence-backed serviceable-market units`

`Operationally credible units = minimum(market capture capacity, funnel capacity, inventory/fulfilment capacity, staff/service capacity)`

A scenario fails when any one capacity is below the break-even requirement, even if the other capacities appear large.

## Contribution and break-even bridge

Use realized contribution, not revenue or headline margin:

`Phone contribution = units × (net selling price − landed cost − transaction/finance cost − discount − expected warranty/return/shrink reserve)`

`Accessory contribution = attached units × (net selling price − landed cost − installation/return/shrink reserve)`

`Ownership-support contribution = collected fee/commission − staff time − partner/courier/payment cost − rework/refund/custody/privacy reserve`

`Operating contribution = phone + accessory + laptop/tablet + service/membership contribution − fixed operating cost − finance/BG cost`

Calculate break-even from category contribution per sale and realistic mix. The earlier 9% blended-margin illustration remains a planning sensitivity; it is not a validated margin or proof that 70 phones is sufficient.

## Evidence-led assortment architecture

### Opening category roles

| Role | Proposed treatment | Evidence required before stock |
|---|---|---|
| Core stocked | Proven fast phone bands and high-confidence immediate attachments | Comparable 30/90-day sell-through, written authorised terms, contribution, lead time and 30-day demand |
| Shallow stocked | Step-up phones, selected business/student laptops, audio/power and ecosystem accessories | Repeated lost-sale/quote evidence, acceptable days cover and protection/rotation |
| Demonstration/order-led | Premium phones, creator/gaming/premium laptops, tablets, wearables and uncertain variants | Demo support, executable fulfilment SLA, deposit/refund terms and customer interest; no shelf-filling depth |
| Partner/service-led | Trade-in, repair, pickup/drop and later protection/loaner services | Approved partner, customer terms, custody/privacy/insurance and case contribution |
| Reject/pause | Unauthorised, unserviceable, low-contribution, high-ageing or unclear-title items | Reconsider only when failed gate is replaced by evidence |

### SKU opening-quantity rule

For every SKU/variant:

`Opening units = display/demo requirement + approved sale units`

`Approved sale units ≤ minimum(demand-supported cover, finance/open-to-buy capacity, supplier/brand cap, downside-liquidity capacity)`

`Reorder point = lead-time demand + evidence-backed safety stock`

Supplier MOQ does not create demand. A large bay or available BG does not justify stock. If MOQ exceeds the financially and demand-supported quantity, negotiate, share/transfer stock, use order-led fulfilment or reject the SKU.

### Mandatory SKU gates

No weighted score overrides:

1. authorised source, invoice, MDMS/serial and lawful-sale evidence;
2. warranty/DOA/service route;
3. net landed cost, executable selling price and positive downside contribution;
4. due-date cash and supplier-payment fit;
5. price protection/rotation or acceptable unprotected exposure;
6. customer-demand evidence by exact or genuinely comparable SKU;
7. opening days cover, concentration and liquidity limits;
8. staff ability to explain, demonstrate, fulfil and support the product.

### Current tier hypothesis—not an allocation decision

- **Highest evidence priority:** phones below NPR 35,000; cases, screen protection, certified charging and cables.
- **Selective evidence priority:** phones NPR 35,000–80,000; mainstream work/student laptops; power banks, audio and laptop essentials.
- **Demonstration/order-led:** phones above NPR 80,000, premium Apple/Samsung/Nothing variants, creator/gaming/premium laptops, tablets and long-tail ecosystem products.
- **Field-led inclusion:** feature phones and rural-use devices; retain only if current local demand, authorised supply and support justify them.

The earlier 120-phone opening table and brand percentages remain planning hypotheses. Replace them with the workbook's SKU-level gate after actual distributor and demand inputs are loaded.

## Flagship-defensibility framework

### Table stakes

Current competitor evidence already places these in the baseline: genuine/authorised products, credible price, EMI, exchange, setup, delivery, warranty referral, attractive displays, promotional gifts and some protection/service access. K&S must execute them well but cannot claim them as the moat.

### Candidate system advantage

The defensible unit is the integrated workflow:

1. **Decision Confidence Record:** needs, shortlist, trade-offs and fit reason.
2. **One True Cost Sheet:** cash/EMI, fees, trade-in, bundle and final payable separated.
3. **Verified Handover:** exact SKU/serial/IMEI, channel, invoice, warranty and condition.
4. **Privacy-Safe Migration Record:** consent, credentials boundary, backup/transfer/verification and old-device action.
5. **K&S Case Ownership:** intake evidence, named owner, next update, escalation and closure.
6. **Transparent Upgrade Path:** estimate/provisional/final/settled value and partner responsibility.
7. **Consented Service Continuity:** purchase, warranty and support context available when the customer returns.
8. **Assortment Learning Loop:** lost sale, request, sell-through, ageing and service data change weekly purchasing.

### Defensibility test

For each proposed advantage, obtain evidence on:

- customer problem severity and observed frequency;
- conversion, retention, referral or effort reduction;
- competitor execution—not just public absence;
- contribution after staff, partner, incident and funding cost;
- repeatability across people, shifts and channels;
- time/capability required for competitors to copy;
- cumulative learning/data benefit with consent and privacy controls;
- legal, reputation and service-failure downside.

Classify only after evidence:

- **Table stake:** expected by customers or commonly delivered.
- **Experiment:** promising but value/economics/repeatability unproven.
- **Differentiator candidate:** noticeable customer lift, positive contribution and reliable execution with limited local parity.
- **System advantage:** several mutually reinforcing capabilities produce repeat/retention/learning and are harder to copy together.
- **Reject/pause:** weak customer value, negative economics or unacceptable risk.

Store design, hospitality, AI, loyalty points or packaging remain supporting elements unless they measurably improve the system.

## Commercial-defensibility gates

### Gate 1 — lawful and controllable foundation

Site, authorised supply, invoice/MDMS, warranty, privacy, insurance, safety, accounting and partner responsibilities are documented.

### Gate 2 — market evidence

At least two independent local demand methods, the standard price/competitor basket, customer study, sell-through evidence and institutional split are complete for the same defined periods.

### Gate 3 — assortment economics

Every opening SKU passes mandatory gates; total cost, ageing, concentration, due-date cover and downside reserves fit the approved envelope. The store can look complete through demos/order fulfilment rather than speculative inventory.

### Gate 4 — capture and operating capacity

Observed traffic/qualified visits, conversion, stock/lead time, staff throughput and service capacity support the required monthly units and cases.

### Gate 5 — contribution and liquidity

Realistic low/base/high cases cover fixed cost, financing/BG cost, downside sales, claim delays, warranty/returns and six months of liquidity protection under the approved finance model.

### Gate 6 — founder decision

The founder reviews the evidence pack and explicitly approves, modifies, phases or rejects the lease, capital exposure, assortment, public promises and launch timing. A workbook status of “ready for founder review” is not approval.

## Evidence package required for founder review

1. Eight to ten comparable local sell-through records and a documented coverage frame.
2. Twelve cognitive/discovery and 100 valid directional customer interviews, plus disclosure of the non-probability limitations.
3. Thirty recent-purchase audits and ten service cases.
4. Twenty-two to twenty-six competitor checks and the same-day 12-phone/3-laptop/6-accessory basket.
5. Twelve institutional interviews with confirmed/planned/verbal quantities separated.
6. Four weeks of transaction-origin, qualified-visit, quote, conversion and lost-sale evidence from a pilot or willing retailers.
7. Three device-distributor and two laptop/accessory proposals plus current brand-support schedules.
8. Four EMI proposals and supervised settlement/refund tests.
9. Trade-in, repair, insurer, accountant and legal evidence from RESEARCH-OWNERSHIP-001.
10. Five rent quotations, site measurement, utilities/safety inspection, fit-out quotations and approved staffing/payroll.
11. Completed low/base/high model with all material inputs dated and source-labelled.
12. Founder review record listing every accepted exposure, rejected assumption and monitoring trigger.

## Major-batch synthesis

### What we learned

The public evidence supports a meaningful phone-led category and a selective computing opportunity, but cannot prove a precise Nepalgunj market or opening share. The present 12,800-phone result is useful only as a scale check. Commercial viability depends on connecting two independent local demand estimates to the store funnel, assortment contribution and capacity.

### What changed in assumptions

The 70-phone month-three target, 1,070-phone year-one target, 120-phone opening stock and brand percentages are now explicitly model inputs—not market conclusions. Premium appearance no longer implies premium inventory depth; demonstration and rapid order fulfilment can carry presentation while protecting capital.

### K&S opportunity

K&S can combine a value-market assortment with a premium, evidence-led ownership system. The strongest potential advantage is the consistency and continuity of the whole system, reinforced by learning from customer needs, lost sales, service cases and SKU economics.

### Risks and disconfirming evidence

A smaller local market, lower store conversion, narrow margins, border/online price gaps, weak supplier protection, poor repair partners or costly service promises can invalidate the flagship even when the concept is attractive. Competitors may already deliver the proposed whitespace privately.

### Unresolved questions

What are current local units by price band and channel, how much regional demand reaches Nepalgunj, which exact SKUs support positive contribution, how many qualified visits can the site generate, and will customers choose the integrated confidence system in real transactions?

### Recommended next research

After founder/privacy approval, run the supervised field pilot through the canonical collection workbook, freeze the reviewed Nepali instrument and then activate production. Load only reviewed, source-labelled summaries—not raw personal or confidential records—into the commercial evidence model. The next desk research should focus only on a newly identified decision/risk or on analyzing collected evidence.
