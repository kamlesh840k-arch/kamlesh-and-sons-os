## Purpose

Define how customers may actually choose smartphones and where KAMLESH & SONS should compete on price, trust, product understanding, service, financing and buying experience.

## Core Hypothesis

K&S should not choose between being cheap and being premium. The stronger strategy is to remove buying uncertainty while keeping the effective final price close enough to market alternatives that customers do not feel penalized for choosing K&S.

## What Customers Are Really Buying

Customers are not only buying a sealed phone. They are buying confidence that:

- the selected model suits their needs;
- the product is genuine and officially supported;
- they are not overpaying materially;
- the camera, battery, performance and storage are sufficient;
- the phone can be handled and compared before purchase;
- financing or EMI is available if needed;
- data transfer, setup and migration will be handled correctly;
- warranty/service help will exist after the sale;
- the retailer will remain accountable if something goes wrong.

## Buying Journey to Design

1. **Need recognition** — replacement, upgrade, gifting, status, work, gaming, camera, battery or broken device.
2. **Online/social research** — reviews, YouTube, friends, TikTok, Facebook, price checking.
3. **Shortlist** — usually 2-4 models or one preferred brand with uncertainty around variant.
4. **Price check** — official price, local shops, online sellers, Indian-border alternatives, offers and EMI.
5. **Physical validation** — hold device, camera test, display, weight, design, speed, comparison.
6. **Trust validation** — invoice, warranty, authenticity, return/service expectations.
7. **Affordability decision** — cash, card, EMI, trade-in, down payment.
8. **Purchase and setup** — accessories, data migration, protector, accounts and backup.
9. **Post-sale reassurance** — support, warranty, service and future upgrade relationship.

## Research Signals

Nepal-focused smartphone studies do not point to one universal purchase driver. Earlier research found product attributes and price significantly influenced purchase intention, while a newer Nepal study found reviews/recommendations and product features more influential and found little direct effect from price, place and promotion. This suggests meaningful customer segmentation rather than one universal retail strategy.

## Price vs Experience — Working Segmentation

### Price-sensitive buyer

Experience helps, but a visible price gap can overpower it. K&S must provide transparent value and avoid looking significantly more expensive than credible alternatives.

### Mid-market buyer

Likely to respond strongly to comparison quality, availability, EMI, trade-in, confidence and setup support. This is a major opportunity for K&S.

### Premium buyer

Price still matters, but trust, authenticity, availability, status, immediate ownership, expert support, migration, warranty and financing can carry much more weight.

### Urgent/problem-solving buyer

Availability, speed, trust and support may matter more than achieving the absolute lowest price.

## Important Commercial Principle

Experience should not be used as an excuse for poor pricing.

The K&S objective should be:

> **Competitive-enough price + clearly superior confidence and service.**

## What Experience Must Actually Deliver

A premium store alone is not enough. Experience must reduce risk or effort through:

- unbiased product recommendation;
- live product comparison;
- clear pricing;
- instant stock confirmation;
- transparent official warranty/VAT information;
- EMI choices;
- trade-in support;
- data transfer and device setup;
- accessory fitting;
- calm complaint handling;
- post-sale follow-up.

Decor, scent, lighting and packaging strengthen perception, but they should support these functional benefits rather than replace them.

## Pricing Hypothesis to Validate Locally

K&S should not assume a fixed acceptable price premium. The acceptable gap likely changes by customer type, product price, urgency, brand and included services.

K&S should experimentally measure:

- conversion when equal to lowest credible local price;
- conversion when 1% higher;
- conversion when 2% higher;
- conversion when 3% higher;
- effect of free setup/accessory/warranty/service value;
- effect of EMI availability;
- effect of trade-in;
- reasons customers leave without purchasing.

## Nepalgunj Validation Plan

Before final pricing policy, interview and observe at least 100-200 target buyers across budget, mid-range and premium segments.

Record:

- intended budget;
- brands shortlisted;
- primary feature need;
- current phone;
- preferred payment method;
- price checked elsewhere;
- willingness to pay more for official warranty;
- willingness to pay more for setup/service;
- EMI requirement;
- importance of trying the device physically;
- final purchase/no-purchase reason.

## Working K&S Buying Concept

> **Research anywhere. Compare clearly. Try everything. Pay your way. Leave fully set up. Come back whenever you need help.**

This is a hypothesis for testing, not yet a final customer-experience decision.

## Competitor Cross-Check

The [Competitor Experience Matrix](competitor-experience-matrix.md) confirms from current public evidence that EMI, exchange, delivery, basic support and even retailer/partner protection plans already exist in parts of Nepal and India. The remaining K&S hypothesis is therefore the value of an integrated, transparent system for decision confidence, true cost, migration privacy, warranty-case ownership and future upgrade—not the mere presence of those individual services.
