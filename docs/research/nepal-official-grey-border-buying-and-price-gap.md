## Decision-relevant conclusion

The evidence does not support a simple “Nepal official versus cheaper India/grey” story.

- A product bought from an authorised Indian retailer can still sit outside Nepal's authorised distribution and warranty route.
- A personally imported phone that follows customs and MDMS requirements is not the same as undeclared commercial stock.
- MDMS registration is necessary evidence for a phone used or sold in Nepal, but it does not by itself prove a Nepal VAT invoice, authorised distributor sourcing, local brand warranty, new/sealed condition or lawful commercial resale.
- India is also a material source of Nepal's **formal** smartphone imports, so the phrase “Indian set” does not prove grey or illegal sourcing.
- A same-day five-model official-MRP snapshot shows India materially cheaper for two matched iPhones, while Nepal's published price is lower for three matched Galaxy S26 models. Border advantage is SKU-, date-, offer- and channel-specific.

K&S should compete with an auditable **True Ownership Comparison**, not fear-based claims or automatic price matching. No price premium, price-match ceiling, imported-device support promise, channel exception or legal interpretation is approved by this study.

## Evidence and classification rule

- **Verified fact (V):** current official regulator, government, law or directly inspected primary dataset.
- **Public claim (P):** brand, association, retailer or news-source statement; it does not prove local execution.
- **Secondary signal (S):** established reporting, directories or qualitative discovery evidence.
- **Calculated estimate (E):** arithmetic from disclosed inputs.
- **Hypothesis (H):** buyer motive, willingness to pay, risk value or proposed commercial response requiring field evidence.
- **Founder decision (D):** accepted only in a separate decision record; none is created here.

Current laws, customs practice, NTA implementation, prices, offers and warranty rules can change. Qualified Nepal legal, customs, tax and consumer-protection review is required before K&S publishes legal claims or accepts any non-standard supply route.

## Channel taxonomy — do not collapse these categories

| Channel state | Minimum evidence | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authorised Nepal commercial supply** | Supplier appointment/authorisation, commercial import and tax records, K&S purchase invoice, IMEI/serial trace, MDMS/type-approval status, customer VAT invoice and written Nepal warranty route | Documented Nepal-market supply and customer protection for the stated SKU/offer | Lowest price, perfect service or unconditional warranty approval |
| **Lawful personal import** | Eligible traveller, purchase invoice, customs declaration/clearance or valid exemption evidence, travel documents and successful individual/automatic MDMS registration | The named individual followed the evidenced personal-import route | Commercial resale entitlement, Nepal authorised-distributor warranty or K&S stock eligibility |
| **Parallel/grey-market product** | Genuine product sourced outside the manufacturer's authorised Nepal distribution | Different distribution route | Counterfeit, stolen, tax-unpaid or illegal status; each of those requires separate evidence |
| **Undeclared/non-compliant commercial stock** | Missing or contradictory customs, tax, MDMS, supplier or invoice evidence | A compliance red flag requiring rejection/escalation | Product authenticity by itself |
| **Used/open-box/refurbished product** | Condition/activation/repair/parts history and seller disclosure | Product condition | Authorised or grey channel; either channel can contain returned/opened products |
| **Counterfeit, cloned, stolen or materially misrepresented product** | Competent verification, official status or documented mismatch | Serious product/legal risk | It must not be used as a synonym for every grey product |

An Indian GST invoice establishes an Indian retail transaction. It is not a Nepal customs declaration, Nepal VAT invoice, Nepal distributor warranty or permission for K&S to resell the product.

## Verified regulatory and consumer-protection baseline

### MDMS and commercial sale

The current [NTA MDMS portal](https://mdms.nta.gov.np/public) tells buyers to check registration before purchasing in Nepal and tells mobile importers/sellers to obtain NTA approval before import and sell registered devices. The [MDMS Bylaw 2075](https://nta.gov.np/uploads/contents/MDMS_Bylaw_2075.pdf) requires authorised importers/OEMs to register device identifiers before import, requires individuals to comply with customs rules for personal imports, and provides for non-compliant devices to be blocked.

**Operational interpretation (not legal advice):** K&S must check and retain evidence for every IMEI, but a positive MDMS result is only one control. Supplier invoice, tax invoice, type approval/authorised route, serial/IMEI match, condition and warranty evidence remain separate.

### Personal import is a distinct route

NTA's portal states that customs-cleared personal phones can be registered automatically and provides individual registration using identity and travel evidence. The Department of Customs publishes a [2082 passenger personal-goods notice](https://www.customs.gov.np/content/161/notifications-related-to-goods-and-revenue-execution/). Current [Tribhuvan International Airport guidance](https://tiairport.com.np/public/index.php/faq/en) says one personal phone is normally allowed and describes a further concession for qualifying Nepali foreign workers.

These sources do **not** establish that an ordinary same-day Nepalgunj–Rupaidiha shopping trip qualifies for the same concession, that an extra device can be commercially resold, or that Jamunaha's current declaration/valuation process is identical to an airport process. Obtain written current guidance from Jamunaha Customs before publishing a landed-cost example.

### Invoice, price and warranty disclosure

The [Consumer Protection Act 2075](https://repository.lawcommission.gov.np/np/category/documents/prevailing-law/statutes-acts/%E0%A4%89%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%AD%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BE-%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%A3-%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%A8-%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%AD%E0%A5%AB/) includes seller duties to display prices, honour applicable guarantee/warranty arrangements and issue a bill or receipt. Exact application to a proposed policy or disputed case requires legal review.

### Warranty is product-, territory- and entitlement-specific

[Apple's rest-of-world limited warranty](https://www.apple.com/legal/warranty/products/ios-warranty-row-english.html) says Apple may restrict iPhone service to the country where Apple or its authorised distributors originally sold the device. [Samsung India's warranty page](https://www.samsung.com/in/support/warranty/) describes warranty for products purchased in India and requires proof of purchase. Laptop support can also be entitlement-specific: [Dell](https://www.dell.com/support/kbdoc/en-us/000125103/ownership-transfer-and-dell-product-registration-guides) describes ownership/service transfer, destination availability and service-contract restrictions.

Therefore staff must verify the exact serial/service tag and written entitlement. “International warranty” and “no warranty” are both unsafe blanket statements.

## Formal-import evidence changes the “Indian set” assumption

The Department of Customs preliminary FY 2082/83 workbook reports:

| Smartphone measure — HS 85171300 | Official workbook value | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Total formal imports | 2,267,419 units; NPR 48.693bn import value | National formal-channel scale; not retail sell-through |
| Reported customs revenue | NPR 9.082bn | Includes the workbook's reported revenue; not a published statutory rate breakdown |
| Calculated revenue/import-value ratio | 18.65% | Descriptive effective ratio only; do not call it “the mobile tax rate” |
| Imports recorded from India | 364,842 units; NPR 10.991bn | India is a formal supply origin as well as a border-shopping market |
| Calculated India share | 16.09% of units; 22.57% of import value | Does not identify brands, distribution rights or Nepalgunj flow |
| Calculated average customs value from India | NPR 30,125 per unit | Product-mix signal, not an Indian or Nepali retail price |

Source: [Department of Customs FY 2082/83 statistics page](https://customs.gov.np/content/450/foreign-trade-statistics-came-208-83/) and [annual commodity workbook](https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/files/FTS_Upto_Ashadh_82_83_gdfkmo9.xlsx). Values are recorded in NPR thousands in the workbook; conversions and ratios above are **E**.

Do not repeat public articles claiming a single combined tax percentage unless the current tariff, VAT/excise treatment, valuation base and product conditions are verified by a qualified adviser. The workbook ratio is not a substitute for the tariff.

### What this snapshot establishes

- “India is cheaper” is false as a universal claim.
- Premium Apple price pressure can be materially different from Samsung price pressure.
- A single percentage premium cannot govern the full assortment.
- Official MRPs are not transaction prices. A current local cash offer, India offer, gifts, exchange, EMI, stock and exact regional SKU can reverse a comparison.

### What it does not establish

- The price a Nepalgunj or Rupaidiha customer can actually obtain.
- Whether the exact India regional model has identical bands, SIM configuration, software, charger/accessories or Nepal warranty eligibility.
- Any lawful customs payment or exemption for the buyer.
- The price of grey stock, retailer margin, border-market volume or willingness to travel.
- A laptop gap. Public Nepal/India laptop pages frequently omit a matching manufacturer part number and service entitlement; compare exact MTM/SKU/service tag only.

## Three-layer price-gap method

Never present one “India savings” number. Record three layers.

### Layer 1 — headline same-SKU price gap

`Headline gap = Nepal official cash price − (India cash price × dated NRB INR selling rate)`

Use the same model code/variant, RAM, storage, condition, colour where price differs, included accessories and purchase date. Keep MRP and executable cash price in separate columns.

### Layer 2 — lawful acquisition cash gap

`Lawful acquisition gap = Nepal official cash price − (converted India invoice price + FX/payment cost + travel + documented customs/duty + documented registration cost + unavoidable accessory difference)`

Customs/duty must be an actual receipt or a written current rule applied by a qualified adviser/customs authority. Never enter zero merely because the buyer crossed without being charged.

### Layer 3 — ownership protection ledger

Show, but do not invent a rupee value for:

- Nepal versus foreign/seller warranty and verified serial entitlement;
- DOA/return destination, travel and turnaround;
- local case ownership and status updates;
- activation/condition/refurbishment confidence;
- MDMS, VAT invoice, proof-of-purchase and resale documentation;
- EMI, exchange, setup/migration and loaner/protection eligibility;
- region-specific hardware/software/service limitations.

Only create a risk-adjusted rupee estimate after K&S has repair-frequency, repair-cost, travel and warranty-outcome evidence. Keep the cash gap and risk sensitivity visible separately.

## Buyer-behaviour hypotheses — not findings

| Hypothesis | Why plausible | Evidence that would confirm or reject it |
|---|---|---|
| Premium Apple buyers compare India/grey more often than mainstream Samsung buyers | The initial iPhone gap is large while matched Galaxy MRP is not cheaper in India | Recent-purchase channel records and consideration set by exact SKU/budget |
| Availability drives parallel imports independently of price | Nepal launches, colours/storage and model families can differ | Lost-sale log: unavailable official SKU versus lower-price reason |
| A trusted relative or known seller reduces perceived channel risk | Informal trust can substitute for formal protection in the buyer's mind | Interview source of recommendation and who customer expects to solve failure |
| Border shopping falls when enforcement strengthens, then can return when execution changes | April 2026 reporting observed fewer crossings under tighter enforcement | Monthly Jamunaha observation plus official notices; no causal volume claim without data |
| Official-channel value rises after a real repair/warranty problem | Benefits are otherwise delayed and abstract | Ten service-case interviews split by channel and outcome |
| EMI, VAT invoice and local after-sales can offset a modest gap but not any gap | These attributes create usable value and reduce friction | Randomised choice tasks and actual lost-sale reason by gap band |
| Budget customers respond more to absolute rupees; premium customers compare percentage and protection differently | Ticket size changes the meaning of the same percentage | Choice analysis by budget band; do not assume linear response |

The [Kathmandu Post's April 2026 border report](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/04/27/strict-customs-enforcement-empties-border-markets) is a current **S** signal: it reports strengthened Jamunaha checks, fewer cross-border shoppers and price concerns. It is not phone-specific, does not measure Banke phone purchases, and cannot be projected forward as permanent enforcement.

## Required field evidence

### 1. Same-day channel price basket

Run the existing 12-phone, three-laptop and six-accessory basket on the same one-to-two days across:

- four authorised/traceable Nepalgunj offers;
- two independent Nepalgunj/Kohalpur offers where the seller clearly states the channel;
- four Rupaidiha retailer offers;
- two Kathmandu comparators, including one authorised and one parallel-market quote where safely obtainable.

For every device record exact manufacturer model/part number, RAM/storage, region code, colour, condition, activation status, IMEI/serial, box/accessories, stock, MRP, executable cash price, payment/EMI price, invoice type, warranty issuer/territory, DOA/return route and quote time. For laptops, no cross-market match is valid without the exact MTM/SKU and warranty/service entitlement.

Do not ask a seller or carrier to evade customs, tax, MDMS or invoicing. Do not purchase, record covertly or publish named negative allegations without approval and legal/ethical review.

### 2. Thirty evidence-backed recent purchases

Recruit buyers who purchased a phone or laptop in the prior 18 months. With consent, inspect/redact the invoice or payment proof, box model/serial, MDMS result where applicable and warranty record. Record:

- purchase place and channel as described by the buyer, then independently classify evidence;
- exact item, condition, date, amount and payment method;
- alternatives considered and their dated quoted prices;
- who influenced the decision;
- invoice, MDMS and warranty understanding at purchase;
- any return, repair, block/registration or resale event;
- whether the same channel would be chosen again and why.

Thirty purposive cases reveal mechanisms and failure modes; they do not estimate market share.

### 3. Integrate into the 100-customer study

Use non-representative quotas so every decision segment is heard:

| Stratum | Minimum interviews |
|---|---:|
| Intended phone budget below NPR 25,000 | 25 |
| NPR 25,000–50,000 | 30 |
| NPR 50,000–100,000 | 25 |
| Above NPR 100,000 | 20 |

Recruit across Nepalgunj, Kohalpur and frequent Jamunaha/Rupaidiha shoppers; record geography separately. Do not report these quotas as population shares.

Core questions:

1. What exact device was last purchased, when, where and for how much?
2. Was an official Nepal, local parallel/grey or India option considered? What was the actual quote?
3. Which proof was checked: invoice, IMEI/MDMS, warranty, seal/activation, seller identity?
4. Who was expected to handle DOA, warranty, repair and return?
5. Which mattered most: price, availability, trust, finance, warranty, service, model/colour, gifting or speed?
6. What actually went wrong, if anything, and what did resolution cost in money and time?
7. At what documented cash gap would the buyer switch away from Nepal authorised supply?

### 4. Randomised choice test

Give each respondent six choices in varied order. Use an identical device and alter only disclosed attributes:

- price gap: 0%, 3%, 5%, 8%, 12%, 15% or 20%;
- Nepal VAT invoice versus foreign invoice versus seller receipt;
- verified Nepal brand/distributor warranty versus India warranty versus seller warranty;
- local case ownership versus customer must return to seller/country;
- cash only versus eligible EMI;
- setup/migration included versus self-service;
- MDMS/customs status documented versus unclear.

Ask the choice, confidence, reason and “would postpone purchase” option. Estimate segment-specific switching ranges with confidence intervals where possible. A 100-person purposive study is directional, not a statistically representative Banke forecast.

### 5. Ten cross-channel service cases

Interview owners and, with consent, verify case documents for authorised Nepal, lawfully personal-imported and seller-warranty devices. Capture fault, diagnosis, warranty decision, travel/shipping, parts, total customer cost, days without device, updates, repeat failure and final outcome.

### 6. Written authority and partner checks

Request current written clarification from Jamunaha Customs/Nepalgunj Customs, NTA and qualified advisers covering:

- land-border personal-use declaration/exemption and excess-device process;
- valuation, receipt and applicable duty/tax evidence;
- MDMS registration route and documents after land-border import;
- commercial resale boundary;
- current enforcement/appeal/contact route;
- treatment of repaired/replacement devices and changed IMEI;
- VAT invoice, return and warranty disclosures relevant to K&S.

No verbal border answer becomes a legal claim or pricing input.

## K&S commercial hypotheses

1. **True Ownership Comparison Sheet:** show exact SKU, cash/EMI price, invoice, MDMS, warranty issuer, return route, included setup and local case owner side by side.
2. **No blanket price match:** compare only identical, currently available, documented and lawfully acquirable offers. Founder must approve any maximum match cost and category contribution floor.
3. **Apple-specific response:** monitor Apple gaps more frequently and seek distributor/bank funding, order-led inventory and service/value bundles rather than silently funding a large price difference.
4. **Samsung and value-Android proof:** show when Nepal official pricing is already competitive; do not assume the customer has calculated the correct exchange rate or matched storage.
5. **Imported-device service boundary:** K&S may later offer paid diagnosis/setup or service coordination for customer-owned imported devices, but must not imply Nepal warranty or cure missing customs/MDMS evidence. This requires policy, insurer/legal and founder approval.
6. **Evidence-led language:** say what K&S provides. Do not label another seller's item illegal, fake or warranty-less without product-specific evidence.

These are **H**, not accepted pricing or service policies.

## Stop conditions and red flags

- Exact model/region/condition cannot be matched.
- Seller refuses any invoice/receipt or requests extra payment for a bill.
- IMEI/serial differs among device, box, invoice or system.
- MDMS status is absent, contradictory or shown only by an unverifiable screenshot.
- Warranty territory/issuer is described verbally but cannot be confirmed by serial and written terms.
- Customs cost is entered as zero without applicable written evidence.
- Staff describe all Indian or grey products as fake/illegal.
- A price match would breach the approved contribution floor.
- K&S is asked to stock, resell or facilitate an undeclared personal import.

## Founder decisions required later

- Whether imported-device paid support is offered and its exclusions
- Whether any lawful non-authorised product can ever be stocked; this would require explicit review of DEC-0018 and procurement/legal controls

## Batch synthesis

### What we learned

- MDMS, customs compliance, authorised distribution, VAT invoice, condition and warranty are separate evidence dimensions.
- India supplied 16.09% of formal smartphone units and 22.57% of formal smartphone import value in FY 2082/83; “Indian set” is not a channel verdict.
- The first controlled MRP snapshot shows a large iPhone gap but no India advantage on the matched Galaxy S26 models.
- Official manufacturer warranties can be territorial; the exact serial entitlement matters.
- No reliable public source establishes Nepalgunj channel share, buyer motivations or transaction-level Rupaidiha savings.

### What changed in prior assumptions

- Border competition must be managed by exact SKU and brand, not one assumed percentage.
- MDMS status alone cannot be used as the K&S “official product” proof.
- A lawfully imported personal device and commercial grey stock must not be discussed as the same thing.
- K&S cannot price the value of warranty/service until real cases show failure rate, cost and downtime.

### K&S opportunities

- Make documentation and ownership routes visible at the counter instead of relying on abstract trust language.
- Demonstrate Nepal price competitiveness where it exists.
- Build an Apple/premium response around current gap evidence, financing and ownership support.
- Serve customers with a neutral, auditable comparison rather than scare-selling.

### Risks

- Large premium gaps can overwhelm a generic “official is safer” pitch.
- Customs enforcement and offers can change faster than staff scripts.
- Regional model, condition and warranty differences can make an apparent same-SKU comparison false.
- Unsupported legal or competitor claims create reputational and compliance exposure.

### Unresolved questions

- Actual Nepalgunj/Rupaidiha executable prices and lawful landed costs.
- Buyer channel shares, motivations and switching thresholds by price band.
- Jamunaha personal-import process for a land-border purchase on the research date.
- Exact repair, return and resale consequences by device/channel.
- Laptop gaps for exact MTM/SKU and transferable service entitlement.
