## Decision question and evidence labels

What supplier, bank and brand-support structures can give KAMLESH & SONS useful assortment without converting every available rupee or guarantee into slow stock?

- **V — verified fact:** supported by an official source.
- **P — public program claim:** a brand describes a mechanism; it does not prove Nepal eligibility or K&S terms.
- **E — estimate:** calculated from disclosed inputs.
- **H — hypothesis:** must be tested with written proposals or operating evidence.
- **D — founder decision:** only a signed decision record can approve it.

## Executive findings

1. **V:** Normal trade credit, a revolving supplier limit, sell-through settlement, true consignment, BG-backed credit and bank working capital are different legal and economic arrangements. The label used by a salesperson is not sufficient.
2. **V:** Nepal Rastra Bank's current FAQ says an overdraft-type loan under the Working Capital Loan Guidelines is available only to a natural person, not a firm/company. K&S documents therefore use **cash-credit/approved working-capital facility** for the proposed company facility until a bank confirms the exact product in writing. This corrects terminology; it does not approve a bank or facility.
3. **V:** NRB bases larger working-capital limits on turnover and operating-cycle evidence and requires controls over current assets, drawing power, inspection, insurance and secured-transaction registration where applicable. A new business can be assessed on projections, but sanction remains the bank's decision.
4. **V:** A bank guarantee is contingent support, not cash and not supplier credit by itself. The supplier agreement must say how BG value converts into a usable limit, invoice due dates, invocation triggers, notice/cure, reduction and release.
5. **V:** Under IFRS 15, consignment indicators include the supplier retaining control until sale or another event, being able to require return/transfer and the dealer lacking an unconditional obligation to pay. A deal called “consignment” but payable on a fixed date regardless of sale may still behave like financed inventory. Nepal accounting, VAT and secured-transaction treatment require qualified review.
6. **V:** IAS 2 treats trade discounts, rebates and similar items as reductions of inventory purchase cost; an IFRS Interpretations Committee decision distinguishes those from genuine selling-expense reimbursements. K&S must classify each credit note with its accountant and must not count unearned support as cash margin.
7. **H:** The best opening structure is likely a mixed portfolio: short normal credit for proven fast sellers, rotation/price protection for volatile SKUs, order-led or low-quantity stock for uncertain models, and selective sell-through/consignment only where reconciliation and title rules are clear. Written proposals are needed to test this.

## Structure taxonomy

| Structure | Commercial substance | When K&S owes cash | Principal K&S risk | Minimum proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash/advance purchase | K&S funds inventory before or at delivery | Before/at delivery | Maximum cash conversion and markdown exposure | Quotation, invoice, title/risk terms |
| Normal trade credit | Supplier invoices K&S and sets a due date | Due date, whether sold or not | Slow stock matures before cash is collected | Signed credit schedule by invoice |
| Revolving/rolling trade limit | Repaid invoices restore purchasing headroom | Each invoice's due date unless expressly sell-through-linked | “Rolling” can be mistaken for payment-after-sale | Limit, availability formula, invoice ageing and suspension terms |
| Partial advance plus credit | Part funded before delivery; balance later | Advance plus stated due date | Cash tied up and residual maturity risk | Split, due date, cancellation/refund terms |
| Sell-through settlement | Payment is based on verified sold units | Agreed post-sale cycle, subject to contract | Reconciliation, shrink and deemed-sale disputes | IMEI/SKU report, reconciliation, deemed-sale and ageing rules |
| True consignment | Supplier retains control/title until defined sale/event | Sale/event defined in contract | Custody, theft, damage, tax/accounting and termination return | Title/control, return right, no unconditional payment obligation, risk/insurance and tax advice |
| BG-backed supplier credit | Bank promises payment if defined conditions occur; supplier separately grants credit | Supplier invoice schedule; bank pays only on valid invocation | Invocation, collateral/security, fees, expiry and cross-default | Bank sanction, exact BG wording and supplier credit conversion |
| Bank cash credit / working-capital finance | Bank funds eligible short-cycle business assets/cash need | Interest/charges and facility repayment terms | Rate/reset, drawing-power, inspection, collateral and misuse | Bank term sheet, permitted-use schedule, security, total-cost and covenant model |
| Term loan | Amortising finance for longer-lived uses | Instalments | Asset-life/repayment mismatch if used for volatile inventory | Amortisation, security, prepayment and total-cost terms |

**Control rule:** K&S will never describe ordinary invoice credit as sell-through finance, or fixed-date financed inventory as consignment, without contract and adviser confirmation.

## Current Nepal working-capital boundary

NRB's current consolidated Working Capital Loan Guidelines and FAQ establish the regulatory frame, but not K&S eligibility:

- Up to NPR 1 crore of aggregate working-capital borrowing (up to NPR 3 crore for manufacturing) sits outside the guideline's formula, while the bank's own working-capital policy still applies.
- For the next band, fluctuating working capital is normally capped at 20% of projected annual turnover, with expansion up to 50% based on operating-cycle evidence; larger exposures use different 25%/40% bands.
- Analysis can include the operating cycle, cash-conversion cycle, days sales outstanding, inventory conversion, lead time and accounts payable.
- New firms with less than three years of history may be assessed using projected operating cost/cash flow, subject to bank policy.
- The framework includes drawing-power/current-asset verification, inspection, insurance and security controls. Cash-credit proceeds cannot be used to pay loan principal, interest or fees.

**Implication (E/H):** a facility ceiling alone is not purchasing capacity. Usable capacity is the lowest of bank availability, supplier limit, owner inventory cap, eligible drawing power, due-date cash cover and demand-proven stock need.

## Economic model

All comparison inputs should be VAT-exclusive unless the accountant confirms a different treatment. The operating workbook implements these calculations.

### Core metrics

`Cash-conversion days = inventory days + customer-collection days − supplier-payment days`

`Due-date sell-through cover = forecast landed cost of units sold by due date ÷ supplier amount due`

`Cash due cover = unrestricted cash receipts available by due date ÷ amount due`

`Effective financing burden = explicit fee + foregone settlement discount + allocated BG commission/amendment/security cash cost + bank interest + mandatory insurance/audit cost`

`Realised unit contribution ex VAT = net selling price − landed inventory cost − payment/EMI subsidy − direct fulfilment/setup cost − expected unrecovered claim/shrink/warranty cost + collected eligible support`

`Price-protection exposure = eligible on-hand units × verified distributor price reduction`

`Net rotation value = avoided expected markdown loss − rotation fee − freight − administration − replacement-value downgrade`

`Support realisation = cash or reconciled credit notes received ÷ valid claims submitted`

### Interpretation gates

- A due-date sell-through cover below 1.00 means expected sold-cost is insufficient to cover the supplier due; it is not repaired by a high gross-margin percentage.
- A cash due cover below 1.20 is an escalation flag for an opening business, not an automatic approval threshold. The founder and accountant must approve the actual minimum.
- Supplier support is zero in the downside cash case until received or legally nettable against a due invoice.
- A waived cash discount, BG fee or exclusivity cost belongs in total economics even when absent from the supplier invoice.
- The facility should not fund durable fit-out indefinitely; bank-permitted term finance should be compared for longer-lived assets.

## Price protection and stock rotation

### Price protection schedule

Require a written schedule covering trigger, eligible serials and condition, maximum age, snapshot time, proof of on-hand inventory, claim window, calculation base, VAT/credit-note treatment, settlement deadline, exclusions, audit, dispute and termination. Do not assume a public MRP cut creates a claim: eligibility exists only under the signed rule.

### Stock rotation schedule

Require eligible age/condition, maximum units or value, colour/storage swaps, replacement valuation, fees, freight, approval time, credit-note time, end-of-life treatment and exit rights. Rotation is valuable only when avoided markdown exceeds all costs and the replacement inventory is better.

## Consignment control schedule

Before accepting anything described as consignment, obtain accountant, tax and legal review of:

1. title and control at delivery, display, reservation, sale, loss and termination;
2. the exact sale/deemed-sale event and settlement clock;
3. supplier recall/transfer and K&S return rights;
4. VAT invoice/credit-note and revenue/inventory recognition;
5. IMEI/serial segregation, physical counts and audit access;
6. theft, damage, demo use, insurance and deductible responsibility;
7. discount authority, promotions, price protection and stock rotation;
8. insolvency, secured interest/registry, seizure and other-creditor priority;
9. warranty, DOA, returns and customer-refund responsibility;
10. termination, removal, disputed stock and final reconciliation.

Nepal's Secured Transactions Act recognizes security interests in movable property, including inventory and purchase-money interests. Because retained title or control can interact with secured claims, K&S must obtain current qualified advice and any required registration before relying on a label.

## Credit, guarantee and security schedule

Every written supplier/bank proposal must disclose:

- sanctioned limit, available-limit formula and sublimits;
- whether replenishment headroom is restored by payment, sale report or both;
- credit start event, due dates, grace, interest/penalty and suspension;
- BG-to-credit conversion, revolving language, partial/full invocation, documentary demand, notice/cure, dispute, expiry, auto-extension, reduction and release;
- all collateral, cash margin, lien, personal/corporate guarantee and cross-default;
- bank interest benchmark/spread/reset, commissions, amendment, renewal, valuation, legal, insurance and audit costs;
- permitted uses, current-account routing, drawing-power and reporting requirements;
- rights over inventory, receivables and insurance proceeds;
- termination acceleration and treatment of disputed, defective, returned or price-protected stock.

No personal guarantee, blanket security or ambiguous on-demand BG wording should be accepted without founder and qualified legal/financial review.

## Evidence request and negotiation ladder

K&S offers serial-level receiving/sell-out reconciliation, realistic ageing caps, reliable settlement, no unauthorised diversion, approved presentation, trained staff, insurance and measured forecasts.

Ask at least three device distributors and two laptop/accessory distributors for the same completed [commercial proposal form](../evidence/nepalgunj-mobile-flagship/distributor-system/distributor-qualification-and-commercial-proposal-form.md). Negotiate in this order according to need, not headline generosity:

1. smaller demand-proven opening order and frequent replenishment;
2. normal credit matched to proven sell-through;
3. written price protection and rotation;
4. larger revolving limit after clean payment evidence;
5. selected sell-through settlement;
6. selected true consignment for volatile/halo/demo stock;
7. separately governed brand fixtures, demos, MDF and promoter support.

## Approval and monitoring gates

Reject, reduce or escalate when any of these applies:

- authorisation, VAT/MDMS, DOA/warranty, title, tax or security cannot be verified;
- expected sell-through is later than invoice maturity;
- downside cash due cover is below the founder-approved threshold;
- credit limit is presented without invoice-ageing or availability rules;
- BG invocation, release or security wording is ambiguous;
- support/rebate is required for profitability but is unconfirmed or claim-based;
- price protection/rotation is verbal, discretionary or misses the risk window;
- consignment lacks a sale trigger, recall/return, title/control or insurance rule;
- stock, claims and cash cannot be reconciled independently by IMEI/SKU.

Monitor weekly: inventory days, sold-cost before due date, cash due cover, invoice ageing, facility availability, BG exposure, claims submitted/received, claim cycle time, price-protection exposure, rotation deadlines and overdue/disputed items.

## Founder decisions still required

This study does **not** approve a bank, distributor, facility, BG, collateral/personal guarantee, consignment agreement, inventory amount or cash-cover threshold. Founder decisions are required after comparable written proposals, accountant/legal review and a downside cash model.

## Major-batch synthesis

### What we learned

The valuable question is not “how much credit is available?” but whether payment maturity, cash collection, stock ageing and protection terms fit the same SKU-level cycle. Public rules define the frame; actual K&S economics are private negotiated evidence.

### What changed

The prior generic “OD” wording is no longer suitable for a company facility. “Rolling credit” and “consignment” are now separated into testable structures, and uncollected rebates are excluded from downside cash.

### K&S opportunity

Offer Western Nepal sell-through transparency and disciplined reconciliation in exchange for frequent replenishment, protection and selective risk sharing—not simply more opening stock.

### Risks

BG invocation, fixed dues on slow stock, ambiguous retained title, ineffective claim recovery, exclusivity and facility/security costs can outweigh a headline margin or credit limit.

### Unresolved questions and next research

Obtain comparable 2026 Nepal distributor and bank proposals; commission accountant/tax and legal review of any shortlisted consignment/BG/security language; then populate the operating workbook and approve only the demand-proven portfolio.
