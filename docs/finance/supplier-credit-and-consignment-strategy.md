# Supplier Credit & Consignment Strategy

**Company:** KAMLESH & SONS  
**Status:** Strategy / negotiation framework  
**Purpose:** Preserve the preferred inventory-financing approach for brand and distributor negotiations.

## Strategic Preference

KAMLESH & SONS should prioritize **higher supplier credit limits and sell-through-linked inventory support** over shelf-rent or display-fee negotiations when doing so materially improves stock depth, assortment, and customer availability.

## Preferred Commercial Structures

### 1. Higher Trade Credit Limit
The distributor invoices KAMLESH & SONS on delivery, but allows a larger outstanding balance so the store can carry deeper inventory.

Target negotiation points:
- Higher sanctioned credit ceiling
- Longer payment period (for example 30/45/60 days where commercially available)
- Rolling replenishment while earlier invoices are still within agreed terms
- Credit-limit review after demonstrated sell-through and payment discipline

Important: Under normal trade credit, payment is due on the agreed date even if the product has not sold.

### 2. Sell-Through / Rolling Settlement
A stronger arrangement where settlement frequency is linked to products sold during the period.

Possible structure:
- Distributor supplies agreed inventory pool
- K&S submits weekly or fortnightly sell-through report by SKU/IMEI/serial
- Sold units are settled on an agreed cycle
- Unsold units remain within a defined aging limit
- Replenishment is triggered from verified sales

This should be negotiated only with clear reconciliation rules and stock controls.

### 3. Consignment
The strongest working-capital structure for K&S: goods remain owned by the supplier/distributor until sold, and K&S remits payment after sale.

This is commercially attractive but may be harder to obtain and requires careful accounting, VAT/tax, title, insurance, shrinkage, damage, warranty, return, and reconciliation rules before implementation.

## Negotiation Position

Do not lead with: “Give us free stock.”

Lead with a risk-controlled commercial proposal:

> KAMLESH & SONS wants to carry a significantly deeper assortment of your brand so customers in Western Nepal can buy immediately rather than wait for stock. We are prepared to provide transparent sell-through reporting, disciplined settlement, stock reconciliation, brand standards, and agreed inventory aging controls. In return, we want a higher rolling credit limit and, where possible, sell-through-linked settlement or consignment support.

## What K&S Should Offer the Distributor

- Transparent weekly/fortnightly sell-through data
- SKU + IMEI/serial-level reconciliation where applicable
- Agreed stock aging limits
- Minimum monthly/quarterly purchase or sell-out targets only when realistic
- Automatic settlement schedule
- Controlled returns/stock rotation rules
- Proper insurance/security for inventory
- No diversion into unauthorized channels
- Strong brand presentation and trained staff
- Reliable payment history

## Negotiation Ladder

If full consignment is rejected, negotiate progressively:

1. Larger normal credit limit
2. Longer credit days
3. Partial advance + balance on credit
4. Rolling settlement based on sell-through
5. Stock rotation for slow-moving SKUs
6. Price protection for sudden distributor price cuts
7. Demo/display units outside the commercial stock limit
8. Full or partial consignment for selected premium/slow-moving models

## Inventory Risk Controls

K&S should not accept a large credit line merely to overstock.

Track by brand/SKU:
- Days of inventory
- Sell-through rate
- Gross margin
- Aging buckets
- Credit utilized vs sanctioned
- Upcoming supplier dues
- Cash collected vs payable
- Return/rotation eligibility

Fast-moving inventory should receive the highest credit allocation. Premium or slow-moving SKUs should preferably be supplied through lower quantities, rotation support, or consignment.

## Key Contract Points Before Acceptance

Any formal arrangement should define:
- Credit ceiling
- Payment due dates / settlement cycle
- Ownership/title to goods
- VAT and invoicing treatment
- Credit note handling
- Price protection
- Product returns / stock rotation
- Damage, theft and insurance responsibility
- Warranty responsibility
- Unsold stock aging
- Security deposit / bank guarantee / personal guarantee if required
- Default consequences
- Distributor right to suspend supply
- Audit/reconciliation process

## Core Principle

> Use supplier credit to increase **productive inventory**, not simply inventory quantity.

The goal is to maximize product availability and sales while keeping K&S cash free for store operations, service quality, marketing, technology, and growth.

## Status

This document records a strategy for future distributor negotiations. It is not a commitment to any supplier, credit amount, payment period, guarantee, or consignment agreement. Specific terms must be reviewed before acceptance.
